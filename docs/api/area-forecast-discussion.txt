Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
243 AM PDT Wed Sep 9 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
The heat and humidity is expected to peak for the coast and
valleys today with Extreme HeatRisk for portions of the coast into
the western valleys. Gradual cooling of high temperatures will
begin to spread inland on Thursday and Friday and will continue to
spread inland through Sunday with not much change for Monday and
Tuesday of next week. A tropical air mass will remain in place
into Friday, then much drier for inland areas for this weekend
through Tuesday with are turn of the marine layer for coastal
areas. There is a  chance for showers and thunderstorms for this
afternoon and a slight chance of showers for Thursday afternoon.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

.SHORT TERM (Today through Friday)...
There were showers and thunderstorms on Tuesday afternoon with the
heavier rainfall mostly tied to peak heating during the mid
and late afternoon though there was some isolated development
less tied to terrain. For Riverside and San Diego Counties,
development was generally on convergence boundaries on the coastal
slopes of the mountains with a slow drift of the thunderstorms
toward the northwest. Rainfall near the core of the thunderstorms
was generally 0.25 to 0.5 inch, locally exceeding one inch. There
was a thunderstorm wind gust to 61 mph at 5:20 PM on Tuesday near
Rainbow near the pass along Interstate 15 between Riverside and
San Diego Counties.

It is once again a very warm night with temperatures at 2 AM of 5
to as much as 10 degrees warmer than 24 hours ago for the valleys
and inland coastal areas with temperatures for the coast and
valleys mostly in the 70s and 80s.

Today will once again be a very hot and humid day for the coast
and valleys. High temperatures will range from the mid 80s to
lower 90s near the beaches to 104 to 110 for the Inland Empire
with 110 to 114 for the lower deserts with Extreme HeatRisk for
portions of the coast and western valleys. There were a number of
locations near the Orange County coast on Tuesday where the
temperature warmed to around 95 to 100 during the early afternoon.
When a weak seabreeze arrived in the afternoon, surface dewpoints
increased into the mid 70s with heat index values increasing to
around 110.

Low temperatures for the coast and valleys for today and Thursday
will be mostly in the 70s with the most likely recordsetting
temperatures for record warm overnight temperatures. Cooling of
high temperatures of a few degrees will begin to spread inland
into the coast and valleys on Thursday and again on Friday.

&&

.LONG TERM (Saturday through Tuesday)...
Substantial drying aloft is expected for Saturday and Sunday.
Precipitable water values exceeding 1.5 inch into Friday will
decrease to around 1.25 inch on Saturday and 0.75 inch for Sunday
through Tuesday.

In addition to the drying aloft, high temperatures on Saturday
will cool a few to around 5 degrees with another few degrees of
cooling on Sunday with slight day to day differences for Sunday
through Tuesday. High temperatures for Sunday through Tuesday will
be a few degrees below average at lower elections to around 4 to
8 degrees below average for the inland valleys. High temperatures
for Sunday through Tuesday will range from the 70s near the coast
to the mid 80s to lower 90s for the Inland Empire with 100 to 104
for the lower deserts.

Nights will be cooler by Sunday and Monday with low pressure on
Sunday and Monday mostly in the 50s for the inland valleys and in
the 60s for coastal areas into the western valleys.

&&

.AVIATION...
090530Z...Mostly VFR conditions expected across the region through
Wednesday with SCT to locally BKN clouds between 10-20 kft. CBs
based at 9 kft MSL leading to some SHRA/TSRA mainly over mountains,
but locally into deserts or inland valleys 19-01Z Wednesday. Tops
will reach 35 kft. Localized gusty winds and heavy rain in and near
any TSRA.

&&

.MARINE...
No hazardous marine conditions are expected through Sunday.
 at 2 AM
&&

.BEACHES...
High tides peaking near 7 feet this evening and again Wednesday
evening could produce minor tidal overflow, coastal flooding, and
localized beach erosion. The surf has diminished and will not
continue to pose a hazard. Check the Coastal Hazard Message for
details.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Extreme Heat Warning until 8 PM PDT this evening for Orange
     County Coastal Areas-Orange County Inland Areas-San
     Bernardino and Riverside County Valleys-The Inland Empire-
     San Diego County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Valleys-
     Santa Ana Mountains and Foothills.

     Coastal Flood Advisory until 11 PM PDT this evening for Orange
     County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Heat Advisory from 8 PM this evening to 8 PM PDT Thursday for
     Orange County Coastal Areas-Orange County Inland Areas-San
     Bernardino and Riverside County Valleys-The Inland Empire-
     San Diego County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Valleys-
     Santa Ana Mountains and Foothills.

     Heat Advisory until 8 PM PDT Thursday for Riverside County
     Mountains-San Diego County Mountains.

     Extreme Heat Warning from 10 AM this morning to 8 PM PDT Friday
     for Coachella Valley-San Diego County Deserts-San Gorgonio
     Pass near Banning.

PZ...None.

&&
