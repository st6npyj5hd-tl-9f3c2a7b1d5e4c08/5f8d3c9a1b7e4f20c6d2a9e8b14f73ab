FXUS66 KSGX 272355
AFDSGX

Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
455 PM PDT Sun Sep 27 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Cloudy and cooler through Monday, along with a slight chance of
light showers for our southeastern regions through early Monday.
The trough of low pressure moves through Southern California on
Monday, bringing back the marine layer and breezy conditions to
the mountains and deserts. Weak high pressure will then bring dry
and warmer weather Tuesday into next weekend, with a shallow
marine layer for coastal low clouds during the nights and mornings.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

...UPDATED AVIATION DISCUSSION...

This afternoon...Satellite imagery shows widespread mid and high
clouds covering the region. These clouds are associated with the
moisture plume from the weakening tropical cyclone Odalys. The
moisture is being drawn into SoCal by the southwest flow aloft.
Low clouds south and east of Orange County can`t be seen in the
satellite imagery but a few locations in the south-coastal areas
of San Diego County are reporting patches of low clouds. Temperatures
are generally lower than at this time yesterday with a few locations
in the inland valleys and mountains as much as 10-15 degrees
cooler.

Circulation around a low pressure system moving in from the
northwest will maintain the southwest flow over SoCal until the
upper low moves east late Monday night. Meanwhile, the mass of
clouds moving in from the southwest will continue to bring slight
chances for light precipitation into Monday. Not much accumulation
is expected and will most likely occur in the mountains of San
Diego County. Even there, rain totals are unlikely to exceed one
tenth of an inch. This low pressure system will also bring cooler
weather with stronger onshore flow. Monday will likely be the
coolest day of the week, with daytime high temperatures near
normal at the coast, 5-7 degrees below normal in the inland
valleys and deserts, and as much as 10-15 degrees below normal in
the mountains. The onshore flow will be strongest on Monday with
westerly winds gusting 30-40 mph in the wind-prone areas of the
mountains and adjacent deserts slopes. Onshore winds will weaken
late Monday.

The upper low will move east on Tuesday and a drying and warming
trend will begin as high pressure builds in from the west.
Northerly flow aloft on the upstream side of the low will combine
with weak offshore pressure gradients to produce a relatively
brief period of offshore flow on Tuesday. The position of this low
pressure system will keep any moisture from Polo well to our south
and east.

The dry and warm weather will continue into next weekend as the
high pressure ridge settles over the Southwest. Daytime high temperatures
will return to above normal by Wednesday but the warmest day will
likely be Saturday. High temperatures on Saturday will likely be
2 to 7 degrees above normal in the mountains and deserts, and as
much as 15 degrees above normal in the inland valleys and coastal
areas. The marine layer will be shallow and any low clouds will
likely be restricted to the coastal areas during the nights and
mornings.

&&

.AVIATION...
280000Z...Coast/Valleys...Ongoing BKN-OVC high clouds AOA 15,000ft
MSL will prevail through about 16z Mon, becoming FEW-SCT thereafter.
Patchy low clouds around 1000ft MSL will be possible along the
coastline, but maintaining no CIGs at any TAF sites. VCSH for most
locations through Monday morning, dry thereafter.

Mountains/Deserts...VFR prevails with BKN-OVC clouds AOA 15,000ft
MSL through Monday morning. Pockets of -SHRA possible between 00-14Z
Monday. Little to no surface impacts expected, but some brief lower
CIGs below 5000ft are possible. Best chance in eastern San Diego
County. High clouds become FEW-SCT after 20z Mon.

&&

.MARINE...
Northwesterly flow gusting to 20-25 knots in the outer waters is
expected Monday afternoon and evening. A Small Craft Advisory has
been issued for this hazard. Additionally, choppy, mixed seas is
expected Monday afternoon/evening. Winds and the choppy seas are set
to diminish Monday night into Tuesday. No hazardous marine
conditions are expected Tuesday onward.

&&

.BEACHES...
Southwesterly swell (3-4 ft at 13 seconds from ~200 degrees) from
Hurricane Odalys persists today into Monday, bringing surf of 3-5
feet with sets up to 6 feet, highest along south and southwest
facing beaches. A swell from Hurricane Polo (~4 ft at 12-13 seconds
from 160-170 degrees) then increases on Monday. Given this
direction, most of San Diego County beaches will be largely
sheltered from this swell, but Orange County beaches (especially in
north OC) should be impacted. Meanwhile, a northwesterly swell (3-4
ft at 7 seconds from ~280 degrees) arrives later today and Monday.
Between these two swells, surf of 3-5 feet with sets to 6 feet looks
probable along south/southwest facing beaches with surf 3-5 feet
along west-facing beaches through Tuesday. With this elevated surf,
there is a high risk for rip currents through Tuesday.

Tides are already observed over 6.8 feet and are expected to peak
around 7.0ft for the late-morning high tides on Monday-Wednesday,
highest on Tuesday. A Beach Hazard Statement remains in effect
through tonight due to the risk of minor tidal overflow and minor
coastal erosion. A Coastal Flood Advisory is in effect from early
Monday morning through Wednesday afternoon to account for the
highest tides combining with the expected elevated surf. Primary
impacts during the morning high tides Monday-Wednesday will be minor
coastal flooding in low lying areas in addition to coastal erosion.

Please note - listed tide predictions have been adjusted to reflect
actual measured tide observations.

&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Beach Hazards Statement through late tonight for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Coastal Flood Advisory from 5 AM Monday to 5 PM PDT Wednesday
     for Orange County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal
     Areas.

PZ...Small Craft Advisory from 11 AM to 11 PM PDT Monday for Waters
     from San Mateo Point to the Mexican Border Extending 10 to
     60 nm out including San Clemente Island.


&&
