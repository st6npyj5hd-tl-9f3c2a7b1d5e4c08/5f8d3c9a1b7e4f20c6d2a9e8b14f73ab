Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
1222 PM PST Sun Nov 22 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Increasing clouds and rain chances tonight into early Monday
morning. Warm and dry conditions expected for Tuesday and Wednesday.
Weak to locally moderate Santa Ana winds are expected for
Thursday and Friday which will continue dry and warm conditions
for the end of the week. Precipitation chances could return early
next week, with low confidence in details. Widespread coastal
flooding is expected this week due to King Tides and increased
surf.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

A weakening trough will move across Southern California tonight into
early Monday. This will bring periods of light rain showers to the
coastal basin with the potential for a dusting of mountain snow
above 6000-6500 ft. Overall shower coverage will be scattered
with rainfall rates mostly 0.10" per hour or less. Rainfall totals
will be 0.20" or less. Highs today are expected to be similar to
highs yesterday.

In the wake of the passing trough, weak transitory upper level
ridging will set up over Southern California, bringing a few degrees
of warming. A shortwave trough will move across California
Wednesday. Forecast continues to trend dry for this shortwave
passage with only a subtle change in the temperature forecast
between Tuesday and Wednesday west of the mountains.

For Thursday, guidance has a ridge over California and the
aforementioned trough to our east, with 60 percent of ensemble
guidance showing the trough more over Texas and weaker with 22
percent showing the low over the Arizona/New Mexico border, and 18
percent showing the low in northern Mexico. All of these
solutions indicate offshore flow, with the solution showing the
low over northern Mexico the most likely solution for stronger
winds with the low more set up for weaker winds in the other
solutions. Guidance from the CW3E West-WRF shows a 60-75% chance
of a weak Santa Ana wind event, with a 25-30% chance of weak to
moderate Santa Ana winds. One thing we can say with confidence is
conditions will be warmer and drier. Highs Thursday and Friday
will be 5 to 15 degrees above average. Friday looks to be the
warmest day with NBM chances of high temperatures of 80 or higher
across the coastal basin and low deserts at 70-90%. Chances of
high temperatures of 90 or more are zero at this time.

By Saturday, it looks likely the upper level ridge will get pushed
east by an incoming upper level trough. 60% of ensemble members
indicate the trough moving into Northern California with the
other 40% showing more zonal flow. The troughing solutions are
the ones most likely to bring us light precipitation in Southern
California. The current forecast follows the NBM with slight
chances of precipitation as early as Sunday with gradual cooling
Saturday into Sunday.

&&

.AVIATION...
221800Z...SCT-BKN clouds AOA 20000 ft MSL the VFR conditions
prevailing through the day today. Low clouds with bases 3500-5000 ft
MSL increase in coverage across the coastal basin after 18z Sunday
as some disturbed weather moves into the region. VCSH across the
nearshore waters and coasts develops as early as 02-03z. Heavier
pockets of more defined -SHRA become favored for the 08-12z period.
VCSH will generally taper off between 14-18z Monday morning.

&&

.MARINE...
No marine hazards are expected through Tuesday.

The next storm system and associated swell is likely to move into
the region Wednesday afternoon into Thursday, which would to
increase winds and seas to hazardous levels. Winds in the outer
waters look to gust 20-25 knots with seas 8-12 feet, locally up to
14 feet in the outer waters. Winds and seas diminish Thursday into
Friday.

&&

.BEACHES...
Morning high tides will be on the increase this week, and has
already been observed at 7.35 feet (MLLW) today at La Jolla. Highest
tides will be Tuesday through Thursday, with the King Tides peaking
on Wednesday. Daily high tides each morning on Tuesday through
Thursday will reach 7.9-8.3 feet. These expected tides will likely
re-break the all time tidal records recently set in late October.
Forecast tides have been adjusted 1.1-1.2 feet above baseline
astronomical predictions based on trends in recent observations.

Background surf on top of the tides will remain around 3-5 feet
through Wednesday, but an incoming swell Wednesday afternoon into
Thursday will bring a sharp increase to surf late Wednesday into
Thursday. Breaking waves of 6-8 feet are expected on Thursday, with
sets of 10+ feet on west-facing beaches. Surf will come down a bit
for Friday, but should still remain elevated to 5-8 feet.

The combination of forecast record high tides and the elevated surf
through Wednesday will likely lead to moderate to locally major
coastal flooding through Tuesday. A Coastal Flood Advisory remains
in effect through early Tuesday morning. With King Tides peaking Tue-
Thu and the notable swell arriving late Wednesday, major coastal
flooding is expected, with locally extreme flooding expected on
Thursday. The Coastal Flood Watch has been upgraded to a Coastal
Flood Warning and has been extended into Friday`s period.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Coastal Flood Advisory until 4 AM PST Tuesday for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Coastal Flood Warning from 4 AM Tuesday to 1 PM PST Friday for
     Orange County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

PZ...None.

&&
