Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
942 PM PDT Mon Aug 17 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
High pressure aloft will slowly build in from the east this week,
bringing gradual warming, a shallower marine layer, and mostly dry
weather. For Thursday, temperatures will generally be about 3 to 7
degrees above normal. Shower and thunderstorm chances return for
the mountains Thursday through Sunday with marginal cooling as the
upper high shifts east and weakens.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

...UPDATED AVIATION DISCUSSION FOR 06Z TAF PACKAGE...

This afternoon...Skies are mostly clear except for a few marine
layer low clouds at the beaches. The marine layer remains about
1400 ft deep with a strong inversion. Temperatures are near to
about 4 degrees warmer than at this time yesterday in the coastal
areas, up to 6 degrees warmer in the inland valleys, 2 to 6
degrees warmer in the mountains and 2 to 4 degrees warmer in the
deserts. High temperatures today will be in the 70s to low 80s
near the coast, mid 80s to low 90s for the western valleys, mid to
upper 90s in the Inland Empire, mid 70s to upper 80s in the mountains,
mid 90s to low 100s in the high desert, and 105-110 in the low
desert.

Numerical guidance is in good agreement through Friday with
respect to the overall pattern, keeping SoCal sandwiched between
high pressure to the east and a trough of low pressure to the
northwest. There will be some minor differences in the forecast
details but the trends should hold. We can expect gradual warming
trend through Thursday when temperatures will be mostly 3-7
degrees above normal. By Wednesday, there is a 90-100% chance of
at least moderate HeatRisk over the inland valleys, lower
elevation mountains and deserts. Chances of major HeatRisk are
around 10-30% over the far inland valleys, around 50% for the
lower elevation parts of the high desert and 55-85% for the low
desert. While high temperatures won`t be significantly above
normal, continued humidity and above normal overnight temperatures
will contribute to the higher HeatRisk.The marine layer will
slowly become shallower, with night and morning low clouds and fog
restricted to the coastal areas for Wed-Fri.

The position of the high will bring more of a southerly flow
aloft and an increase in deep-layer monsoonal moisture for Thu
through the weekend. The added moisture will bring chances for
afternoon/evening showers/tstms, mainly to the mountains but the
potential is there for showers/tstms in the deserts as well.

After Friday, model solutions begin to diverge but overall it
seems more likely than not that the high pressure will weaken a
little as it is displaced farther east and south. The effects will
be marginal at best, with high temperatures remaining near or up
to 6 degrees above normal on Monday.

&&

.AVIATION...
180600Z....Coasts...Patchy, intermittent low clouds along the
immediate coasts of SD county continue to linger this afternoon, but
VFR conditions prevail at all terminals. Low clouds will gradually
increase in coverage through 12z Tuesday but remain quite patchy.
There is low confidence in persistent CIGs at KCRQ and KSNA
overnight, but higher confidence at KSAN. Best window for CIGs
tonight is generally 11-15z. Any clouds that pushed ashore will
clear to the coast by 15-16z Tuesday.

Otherwise...VFR prevails with FEW-SCT high clouds AOA 15000 feet MSL
increasing this evening, lingering into Tuesday.

&&

.MARINE...
No hazardous marine conditions are expected through Friday.

&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...None.
PZ...None.
