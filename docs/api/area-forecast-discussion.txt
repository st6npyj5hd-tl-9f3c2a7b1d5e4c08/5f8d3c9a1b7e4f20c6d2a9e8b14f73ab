Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
226 AM PST Mon Nov 16 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Cool and sometimes wet, breezy weather will continue this
upcoming week. The first weather system will move through Southern
California today and tonight, bringing rain and a chance of
thunderstorms. A more potent system appears to bring greater
rainfall and stronger winds Thursday, moving out early Friday. Dry
and slightly warmer weather is expected late Friday through
Saturday, followed by the next chance of rain Sunday.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

A mix of high, middle and low clouds are gradually filling
Southern California skies this morning. There is a band of light
showers over the coastal waters. This band will get a boost of
energy from an incoming low-pressure trough, which will enhance
the showers and spread them ashore late this morning and inland
this afternoon, before heading out of the region late tonight.
Rainfall will range from about 0.10 inch in northern Orange County
and the northern Inland Empire, up to about 0.70 inch in top
mountain spots. Light measurable snow is expected above 7,500 feet
or so. It appears San Diego County will get a greater share of
rain from this storm. Winds will increase too, mainly over the
coastal waters, mountains, and deserts. Widespread gusts in the
mountains and deserts should exceed 30 mph, with isolated spots
exceeding 40 mph. As the system exits stage right, drier but not
warmer weather will follow Tuesday through Wednesday night. High
temperatures will range from 2-12 degrees below seasonal averages,
with highs only in the 60s in the coastal basin, lower 70s in
lower deserts, 50s in the High Desert, and 40s and 50s (yes, for
highs) in the mountains. The next system arriving early Thursday
is driven by a stronger jet so it will have more energy to make
rain from the moisture and propel stronger winds than today`s
system has. Thursday`s system tracks a little more to the north,
so there is some uncertainty in its strength for SoCal`s latitude,
but we`ll gain greater focus on the intensity and impact in the
next couple days. For now we are fairly certain it will bring
greater rainfall and stronger winds than today`s system.
Preliminary precipitation estimates range from a half inch around
San Diego, 1 inch in Orange County, up to 2.5 inches in the San
Bernardino Mountains, one-third to one-half inch in the High
Desert, and less than one quarter inch in the lower deserts.
Isolated wind gusts are likely to top 60 mph in the favored wind
corridors, 35-55 mph across the rest of the mountains and deserts,
and 25-30 mph for coast and valleys. Snow levels will come down
to around 6,500 feet (Big Bear Lake level), and a few inches could
accumulate above 7,000 feet. Yet another system appears on the
horizon, arriving Sunday and/or Monday. This one looks weaker, so
not as wet or windy as the Thursday system. Its projected
location, timing, and character is all over the place among
ensemble solutions. The most certain thing we can say is that our
weather will remain unsettled and cool.

&&

.AVIATION...
161200Z...Coast/Valleys...VFR conditions prevail this morning with
SCT-BKN clouds based around 4500-6000 ft MSL. ISO-SCT -SHRA over
coastal waters will begin to increase in coverage and push ashore
later this morning, some as early as 14z Mon into Orange County.
However, a bulk of the rain will spread into coastal San Diego and
Orange counties by 21-00z Tue. As the -SHRA moves in, intermittent
MVFR CIGs/VIS may occur across any TAF sites in the coastal basin.
Pockets of +SHRA or even a stray thunderstorm are possible,
primarily between 00-06z Tuesday, of which could briefly bring IFR
conditions (VIS down to near 1SM and CIGs down below 1000 ft MSL).
Precipitation largely moves out of the coastal basin and into the
coastal mountain slopes by 09z Tuesday.


Mountains/Deserts...VFR conditions prevail with FEW-SCT mid-level
clouds. Lower clouds will develop across the coastal slopes after
12z Monday, which will obscure higher terrain. -SHRA will spread
into the coastal slopes by 21 Mon - 00z Tues, eventually tapering
off by 09-12z Tuesday.

&&

.MARINE...
Isolated light showers continue over the coastal waters this
morning. Coverage of these showers will increase through the late
morning and into the afternoon as a front approaches. Pockets of
heavier showers is expected along with a slight chance (10%) of
embedded thunderstorms.

As the cold front pushes into the region, winds and seas ramp back
up this afternoon with 25-30 knot gusts expected in addition to
steep, high seas of 8-11 feet with an average period of 8 seconds.
Wind gusts will be strongest near San Clemente Island, where near-
gale conditions are possible. A Small Craft Advisory remains in
effect from 4 PM this afternoon through 4 AM Wednesday due to the
hazardous conditions expected from the gusty winds and high seas.
Winds and seas will slowly diminish Wednesday morning.

Conditions look to deteriorate once again Thursday as the next storm
system approaches. Steep, high seas of 8-11 ft are expected with
wind gusts peaking near 25-30 knots for Thursday into Friday morning.

&&

.BEACHES...
A series of a mixture of long-period (3-5 feet at 15-17 seconds) and
short period (6-8 feet at 7-8 seconds) swells, both from the west-
northwest (280-290 degrees), will generate periods of elevated and
high surf through much of this week. The next surge of northwesterly
swells arrives this afternoon, keeping surf very elevated up to 6 to
8 feet, with sets to 10 feet along west-facing beaches. This
maintains the threat for high surf and strong rip currents through
mid-week. A High Surf Advisory remains in effect through 4 AM
Wednesday.

This next surge of the northwesterly swell will gradually wane
Wednesday afternoon into Thursday, bringing a brief decrease in wave
heights. However, the next northwesterly swell is set to arrive
Thursday afternoon, likely to bolster the dangerous surf conditions
into the weekend.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.

&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...High Surf Advisory until 4 AM PST Wednesday for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

PZ...Small Craft Advisory from 4 PM this afternoon to 4 AM PST
     Wednesday for Coastal Waters from San Mateo Point to the
     Mexican Border and out to 10 nm-Waters from San Mateo Point
     to the Mexican Border Extending 10 to 60 nm out including
     San Clemente Island.
