AFDSGX

Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
333 AM PST Sun Nov 22 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Dry weather with increasing high cloud cover through this
afternoon. A weak trough moving through will bring isolated to
scattered light showers this evening into Monday morning. Warmer
with weak offshore flow Tuesday with little change for Wednesday.
Stronger offshore flow and high pressure aloft will lead to much
warmer and drier weather Thanksgiving into Saturday, with winds
and temperatures peaking on Friday. Precipitation chances return
next Sunday.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...


...UPDATED AVIATION DISCUSSION...

Morning satellite imagery shows scattered high clouds ahead of
the next low pressure system moving into the region from the west.
Most of the day will be dry with high temperatures around 5 to 10
degrees below normal inland. As the weak upper level trough
passes through this evening into Monday morning, isolated to
scattered light showers will develop for the mountains westward,
with the greatest coverage in the coastal areas and San Diego
County valleys. Showers taper off over the northern areas by
around sunrise Monday, lingering over San Diego County through
mid- morning as flow turns more northwesterly. A few showers may
persist over the coastal waters into the afternoon with dry
conditions over land. Snow levels will be near 5500-6500 ft, but
given the lack of precipitation only expect a light dusting if
anything. Rainfall totals will be around 0.20" or less.

Once this wave passes, weak offshore flow and transitory upper
level ridging follow for Tuesday, bringing warmer and drier
weather. High temperatures will be near normal for inland areas,
and around 3 to 5 degrees above normal near the coast. Models have
come into better agreement with the evolution of the midweek
short wave trough. On Wednesday, there are only subtle differences
in the north/south placement of the wave, which would result in
minor differences in temperatures. The forecast has trended much
drier during this period, and now only a few ensemble members have
any precipitation. Current forecast PoPs are less than 10% on
Wednesday.

By Thanksgiving, ensemble spread grows with regards to the
amplification of this wave over AZ/Mexico. The deterministic GFS
and 25% of the global ensemble members have a more amplified
pattern/deeper low, 20% have a less amplified wave (mostly
consisting of GEFS members), and the remaining 55% show very
little amplification of this wave as it moves downstream. The GFS-
like solutions would offer cooler weather and stronger offshore
winds due to increased upper-level support. By Friday, only 18% of
the members keep the upper low in our vicinity while the rest have
the wave progressing eastward and more dominant upper level
ridging across So Cal for warmer and drier weather. SAN-DAG
pressure gradients are strongest Thursday night into Friday
morning in both the EC and GEFS ensembles, so offshore winds are
expected to peak during this time. The CW3E West-WRF shows a 45%
chance of weak Santa Ana Winds on Friday, and a 25% of a weak to
moderate event. What is certain is that Thanksgiving through
Saturday will be much warmer, hottest on Friday when upper level
ridging and offshore flow is the strongest. For Friday, the NBM
has a 60-80% chance of high temperatures of 80 or higher across
the coastal basin and low deserts, but chances of high
temperatures of 90 or more is zero at this time.

The upper level ridge begins to break down on Saturday and
pressure gradients trend back onshore for some cooling, though
highs will still be 5-10 degrees above normal. Another amplifying
trough enters the picture late Saturday or Sunday, though there is
a lot of spread in the timing of this trough and how much (if any)
precipitation it will produce. For now it is looking like a weak
event with light to moderate amounts (chances of 0.25" or more
around 20-40% for the coast to the mountains). Additional storm
systems could move through during the first week of December, and
the CPC 8-14 day outlook currently has a 33-40% chance of above
normal precipitation for all of California.

&&

.AVIATION...
221130Z...SCT-BKN clouds at or abv 20000 ft MSL through Sunday
morning. Low clouds with bases 4-7 kft increase in coverage across
the coastal basin after 18Z Sunday. ISOLD-SGT -RA/-SHRA develop over
the coastal waters, VCNTY KSAN/KCRQ around 23Z Sunday. -SHRA chances
increase through the evening, with the most likely chance of being
on station at KCRQ and KSAN between 05-09Z, then tapering off
thereafter, mainly after 12Z.

&&

.MARINE...
No marine hazards are expected through Tuesday.

The next system and associated swell is likely to move into the
region Wednesday afternoon into Thursday, which could increase wind
and wave heights to hazardous levels. There remains uncertainty in
the forecast track/strength of this storm, but at this time, winds
may gust up to 25 kts with combined seas of 8-12 feet.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Coastal Flood Advisory until 4 AM PST Tuesday for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Coastal Flood Watch from late Monday night through Thursday
     afternoon for Orange County Coastal Areas-San Diego County
     Coastal Areas.

PZ...None.

&&

$$

PUBLIC...SS
AVIATION/MARINE...Stewey
