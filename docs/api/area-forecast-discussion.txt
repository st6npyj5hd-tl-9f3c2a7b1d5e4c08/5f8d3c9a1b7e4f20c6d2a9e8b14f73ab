Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
356 AM PST Sat Nov 21 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Cooler and dry weather this weekend. A weak weather system will
bring a 25-50% chance of light showers for the mountains westward
Sunday evening into early Monday morning. Cooler and drier
weather on Tuesday before another weather system nears the
region, which may bring a slight chance for light showers on
Wednesday. High pressure will move into the region on Thanksgiving
into the end of the week, bringing drier and warmer weather.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...


...UPDATED AVIATION DISCUSSION...

A few island band showers developed downwind of San Clemente
Island but remained over the coastal waters. These showers are now
shifting south off the coast of Baja as flow turns more northerly.
Pressure gradients are trending offshore, currently around -1 mb
LAX-DAG. Isolated gusts of 25-30 mph are being reported in the
Cajon Pass and northern Santa Ana Mountains. Pressure gradients
and winds will strengthen through the morning with peak gusts
around 30-40 mph in the wind-prone passes and canyons. Otherwise a
sunny but cool day is in store with high temperatures around 5-10
degrees below normal away from the coast, and near normal at the
coast. Onshore flow returns on Sunday as another low pressure
system begins to move in.

Ensembles have trended faster with the arrival of the next short-
wave trough, which is now expected to move in by Sunday evening.
Ensembles have also trended wetter - at least in terms of the
number of members showing precipitation. PoPs have increased to
25-50% for the mountains westward Sunday evening into Sunday
night, with showers tapering off by Monday morning. Overall
rainfall amounts remain light: NBM probabilities of at least 0.10"
are around 25-40%, and probabilities of at least 0.25" are around
10-25%. Snow levels will be near 5500-6000 ft, but given the lack
of liquid equivalent, so totals will generally be less than 1".

Weak transitory ridging builds in behind this wave for the
remainder of the day Monday and Tuesday for dry weather. A touch
of weak offshore flow develops again Tuesday morning to bring some
additional warming west of the mountains and high temperatures
near to a couple degrees below normal across the region.

Wednesday`s weather remains questionable as the evolution of a
short wave moving through the mean flow continues to differ
amongst the models. The GFS and about 50% of the GEFS members
begin to dig this wave much further west and is more amplified for
cooler weather and a better chance of precipitation. However,
this remains the minority solution and only accounts for 18% of
the total ensemble space. The remaining 82% have a lower amplitude
and more inside track, maintaining dry weather with less cooling.
Overall trend has been towards a drier solution as less ensemble
members are showing precipitation now.

As we head into Thanksgiving, upper level high pressure begins to
build in from the west and surface high pressure settles into the
Great Basin, driving another round of offshore flow. Depending on
how the upper wave evolves, we may get some upper level support
for stronger winds, but again this is the outlier GFS-like
solution. The CW3E West-WRF has a 50% chance of weak Santa Ana
winds on Thanksgiving, and a 10% of weak to moderate winds. For
Thanksgiving, high temperatures are forecast to be around 2-5
degrees above normal, increasing to 5-10 degrees above normal on
Friday as the upper ridge strengthens and offshore flow continues.
The ridge weakens and shifts east on Saturday as another waves
moves inland to our north, bringing cooling but otherwise dry
weather. The pattern remains active with potential for more
precipitation the following week.

&&

.AVIATION...
211200Z...VFR conds. FEW-SCT clouds with bases 4-6 kft MSL over the
foothills are will diminish through the morning. Brief N/NW winds
gusting 20-30 kts locally through 15Z on the San Bernardino County
mountain foothills and through the Cajon Pass. Slightly weaker E/NE
winds during this time downwind of the Santa Ana Mountain peaks and
Peninsular Range west facing foothills. Clouds will regather towards
the end of the forecast period, with -SHRA arriving in the vicinity
of KSAN after 15Z Sunday.

&&

.MARINE...
No marine hazards are expected through Tuesday.

The next system and associated swell is forecast to move into the
region for Wednesday into Thursday, which could increase wind and
wave heights back to hazardous levels. There remains uncertainty in
the forecast track/strength of this storm, so stay tuned for details
in the coming days.

&&

.BEACHES...
Morning high tides will be on the increase next week, exceeding 7
feet by Sunday. Highest tides will be Tuesday through Thursday, with
the King Tides peaking on Wednesday. Daily high tides each morning
on Tuesday through Thursday will reach 7.9-8.2 feet. Background surf
on top of the tides will remain around 3-5 feet through Wednesday,
but an incoming swell Wednesday into Thursday could bring notably
higher surf beginning on Thursday. A Coastal Flood Advisory has been
issued for Sunday morning through Tuesday morning for the rising
high tides, with a Coastal Flood Watch in effect for Tuesday morning
through Thursday afternoon.

NOTE: Forecast tides have been adjusted above baseline astronomical
predictions accordingly, based on trends in recent observations.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Coastal Flood Advisory from 4 AM Sunday to 4 AM PST Tuesday for
     Orange County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Coastal Flood Watch from late Monday night through Thursday
     afternoon for Orange County Coastal Areas-San Diego County
     Coastal Areas.

PZ...None.

&&

PUBLIC...SS
AVIATION/MARINE/BEACHES...Stewey
