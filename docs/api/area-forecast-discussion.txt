Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
946 PM PST Tue Nov 24 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Dry conditions expected through Saturday. Today will be a few
degrees warmer than yesterday, with minimal changes in
temperatures for Wednesday. A more noticeable warming is expected
for Thursday and Friday. Weak to locally moderate Santa Ana winds
are expected for Thursday and Friday. Precipitation chances could
return early next week, with low confidence in details.
Widespread coastal flooding is expected this week due to King
Tides and increased surf.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

...UPDATED AVIATION, MARINE, AND BEACHES DISCUSSION...

The transient ridge over the Western United States will get pushed
east this evening into Wednesday morning by an upper level low
pressure system. This will bring increased high cloud coverage into
Wednesday, but conditions will remain dry. Temperatures also are not
expected to change much, staying within a few degrees of today`s
high temperatures. The upper level low is expected to dig south
Thursday, which will allow for ridging to build back over Southern
California and for the pressure gradient to turn offshore.

Guidance continues to show weak to locally moderate Santa Ana winds
on Thursday and Friday. Wind gusts look to peak in the mountains,
foothills, and passes at 35 to 45 mph, with gusts to 45 mph confined
to wind prone locations. The pressure gradient is most offshore on
Thursday, which is when the strongest winds will be. In addition to
winds, temperatures will peak Thursday and Friday. High temperatures
will be 5 to 15 degrees above average, with the warmest conditions
on Friday. West of the mountains, highs will be in the mid to upper
70s to mid-80s.

By Saturday, most guidance has high temperatures cooler than Friday,
but still 5 to 12 degrees above average. More noticeable cooling is
expected early next week as a more persistent troughing pattern sets
up along the Western United States. While our first chance at
precipitation remains Sunday, latest model trends have pushed the
timing back to Sunday night/Monday. ECMWF and GFS ensemble
members continue to show mostly light rain for that time period.
Snow levels look to stay above 8000 ft. A stronger trough that is
expected to dig further south is expected for the middle of next
week. This system has the potential to bring more substantial
precipitation, increased winds, and continued cooler conditions to
Southern California. There remains uncertainty in the details
surrounding timing and precipitation amounts. NBM chances for 1
inch or more of rainfall Tuesday (Dec 1) and Wednesday (Dec 2) are
55 to 65 percent for the coast and valley valleys, 75 to 85
percent for the mountains. The chance for 2 inches or more of
rainfall ranges from around 25 to 35 percent for the coast and
valleys, to 45 to 55 percent for the San Bernardino County
mountains. The chance for 5 inches or more of rainfall is around 5
to 10 percent for the San Bernardino County mountains.

&&

.AVIATION...
250600Z...VFR prevailing. Increasing high clouds at/above 20,000 ft
MSL and unrestricted VIS through Wednesday. Clouds clear Wednesday
afternoon/evening. Northeast winds increase Wednesday afternoon and
evening with gusts 25-35 kts below mountain passes.

&&

.MARINE...
No hazardous marine conditions are expected through early Wednesday
morning.

A large, long period west swell will arrive Wednesday afternoon
and build into Thursday, bringing combined seas of 8-12 feet.
Additionally northwest winds may gust near 20 kts Wednesday
afternoon in the far outer coastal waters. A Small Craft Advisory
remains in effect for the outer waters from late Wednesday morning
through late Thursday morning due to the high seas. Seas begin to
wane Thursday afternoon, falling below 10 feet by Thursday evening.

&&

.BEACHES...
Record-breaking morning high tides this week. Highest tides each day
through Thursday will be between 7.9-8.3 feet, peaking Wednesday.
These forecast tides have been adjusted 1.1-1.2 feet above baseline
astronomical predictions based on trends in recent observations, and
will likely break the all time tidal records just recently set in
late October.

Background surf on top of the tides will remain around 2-5 feet
through Wednesday morning, but an incoming west swell of 7-8 ft at
16-18 seconds arrives late Wednesday into Thursday. This will
generate large breaking waves of 6-8 feet on Thursday, with sets of
10-12 feet on exposed west-facing beaches. Surf lowers to 3-7 feet
on Friday. A High Surf Advisory remains in effect for San Diego
County for 1 PM Wednesday through 4 AM Friday with the swell
focusing the higher surf on the west-facing beaches.

The combination of forecast record King Tides and elevated to high
surf will bring moderate to major coastal flooding, with locally
extreme flooding expected during Thursday morning`s high tide. A
Coastal Flood Warning remains in effect this morning through early
Friday afternoon.

&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Coastal Flood Warning until 1 PM PST Friday for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     High Surf Advisory from 1 PM Wednesday to 4 AM PST Friday for
     San Diego County Coastal Areas.

PZ...Small Craft Advisory from 10 AM Wednesday to 10 AM PST Thursday
     for Waters from San Mateo Point to the Mexican Border
     Extending 10 to 60 nm out including San Clemente Island.


&&
