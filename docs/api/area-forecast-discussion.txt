AFDSGX

Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
458 AM PDT Thu Oct 29 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Gradual cooling will continue through the weekend with Sunday high
temperatures around 10 to 15 degrees below average for inland
areas. Clouds will increase with a slight chance of light showers
from the coast to the coastal slopes of the mountains for tonight
into Friday morning. Light rain could return for Saturday
afternoon into Saturday evening with heavier rainfall possible for
late Saturday night into Sunday morning. Dry and warmer for early
next week with high temperatures warming to around average for
next Tuesday.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

...UPDATED AVIATION, MARINE, AND BEACHES DISCUSSIONS...

.SHORT TERM (Today through Saturday)...
A gradual cooling trend will continue through Sunday with high
temperatures on Sunday 10 to 15 degrees below average for inland
areas. Clouds will increase and there is a 20 to 30 percent chance
for measurable rainfall from the coast to the coastal slopes of the
mountains, mainly for tonight into Friday morning. Any rainfall
should be mostly less than 0.10 inch with only a 10 percent chance
for more than 0.25 inch.

&&

.LONG TERM (Saturday night through Wednesday)...
Chances for precipitation will increase from Saturday afternoon
into Saturday night with the most likely time for any heavier
rainfall for late Saturday night into Sunday morning. The most
likely rainfall through 11 PM Saturday is around 0.10 inch.

For Saturday morning through Sunday night, from the coast to the
mountains, the chance for 0.10 inch or more of rainfall ranges
from 75 percent in eastern Los Angeles County to 85 percent for
southern San Diego County. The chance for 1 inch or more of
rainfall ranges from 35 to 40 percent for eastern Los Angeles
County to 55 to 65 percent for southern San Diego County.

Cluster analysis shows ensemble members of the GFS are decidedly
drier than those of the ECMWF and Canadian models with little
overlap with the ensemble members of those two other ensemble
systems.

A warming trend is expected for Monday through Wednesday of next
week with high temperatures warming to around average on Tuesday.
Monday and Tuesday are expected to be dry. There is around a 10
to 15 percent chance for light precipitation around next
Wednesday from a low pressure system moving into northern and
central California.

&&

.AVIATION...
291130Z...BKN-SCT marine layer clouds based 3000-4000 feet MSL will
eventually scatter out by 17Z. Low clouds based at 3000-3500 feet
MSL will rapidly but randomly redevelop across the coastal basin
after 01Z. Most of the coastal basin will be covered overnight into
Friday. Areas of -SHRA with cigs down to 2500 feet MSL near the
coast after 08Z Friday.

&&

.MARINE...
No hazardous marine conditions are expected through Saturday. As a
storm system moves through the coastal waters this weekend, shifting
winds and seas are likely to increase by Sunday morning, possibly to
hazardous levels. There is a slight chance of thunderstorms Saturday
night and Sunday morning. Stay tuned for updates.

&&

.BEACHES...
High tides during the late mornings will generate significant
coastal flooding through Friday. Tides will be anywhere from 1.4 to
1.6 feet above astronomical predictions, bringing high tides to near
7.7 feet just after 11 AM today and near 7.25 feet around noon
Friday. A Coastal Flood Warning through midday will become a Coastal
Flood Advisory at 1 PM this afternoon until 2 PM Friday.

A storm system should bring increased surf Sunday and Monday.

&&

.CLIMATE...
The water temperature at Scripps Pier on Wednesday was another
record warm temperature for the date at 73.6 F, 10 degrees above
the long-term average for the date. Water temperatures at Scripps
Pier have largely plateaued for the past month, though were a
little cooler for the middle of October, at a time of year when
water temperatures would normally be declining. Hence, the recent
stretch of new daily record warm water temperatures day after day
after day. The ensemble mean of the ECMWF seasonal forecast from
October was for water temperatures along the southern California
coast to average 10 degrees F above normal for December through
February.

The water level at San Diego during the peak of the highest high
tide on Wednesday was the third highest on record for San Diego.
The highest water level for today is expected to be around 0.28
feet lower than on Wednesday, which would place it as the 8th
highest water level recorded at San Diego. This recent 5-day
stretch from Sunday through Thursday of this week will likely
include 5 of the 10 highest water levels ever recorded at
San Diego.

The record high water levels of this week have been the result
of the combination of higher astronomical high tides during
the full moon combined with sea levels that are around 17 inches
higher than a year ago as a result of thermal expansion of
the record warm water from the strengthening El Nino.

Highest water levels at San Diego:

1)  Oct 27, 2026:  2.80 feet MHHW (8.52 feet MLLW)
2)  Oct 26, 2026:  2.72 feet MHHW (8.44 feet MLLW)
3)  Oct 28, 2026:  2.71 feet MHHW (8.43 feet MLLW)
4t) Jul 14, 2026:  2.54 feet MHHW (8.26 feet MLLW)
4t) Nov 25, 2015:  2.54 feet MHHW (8.26 feet MLLW)
6)  Aug 02, 2023:  2.53 feet MHHW (8.25 feet MLLW)
7)  Dec 13, 2012:  2.45 feet MHHW (8.17 feet MLLW)
8)  Oct 25, 2026:  2.40 feet MHHW (8.12 feet MLLW)

Water levels will likely be at least a few inches
higher during king tides the week of Thanksgiving and the
week of Christmas.

&&

SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.

&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Coastal Flood Warning until 1 PM PDT this afternoon for Orange
     County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Coastal Flood Advisory from 1 PM this afternoon to 2 PM PDT
     Friday for Orange County Coastal Areas-San Diego County
     Coastal Areas.

PZ...None.

&&
