AFDSGX

Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
1255 PM PDT Thu Oct 8 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Cooling will spread inland into the weekend with high temperatures
cooling to around average on Saturday. High temperatures for the
coast and valleys will be mostly in the 70s for Sunday through
Tuesday. One low pressure system from the southwest on Sunday and
a second from the north on Monday and Tuesday combined with
tropical moisture will bring showers for late Saturday night
through Sunday night with continuing chances for showers
through Tuesday night. There will also be stronger southwest to
west winds on Sunday.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

Tonight through Saturday...

The ridge is continuing to break down, which is allowing for a
cooling trend beginning today into tomorrow, and then even moreso on
Saturday as we begin to become influenced by an approaching trough
and tropical system moving into the region. Mostly clear skies will
persist through tomorrow, with improving onshore flow and likely
some slightly better coverage developing overnight with the marine
layer, with ceilings generally around 500 to 1000 ft MSL. There will
also be some patchy fog developing within the higher inland valleys
again, although a little further inland as ceilings are expected to
rise a little higher from where they were the previous night. Much
cooler temperatures will be felt on Saturday, along with increasing
low to mid level storm clouds and a slight chance of a few rain
showers developing by later in the evening as a deepening trough
upstream is met with the tropical cyclone (currently a hurricane),
Rachel, continues to approach the region. As a result, highs by
Saturday will be back to being either at, or slightly below, the
seasonal average for this time of year.

Sunday through Thursday...

Depending on the track of Rachel, there could be substantial impacts
to the region, in terms of heavy rainfall and the risk of flash
flooding, along with strong and gusty winds. An overall consensus of
the latest forecast member tracks have the system potentially making
landfall somewhere between Punta San Carlos and Ensenada, in
Northern Baja California, Mexico. However, there are a few of the
members, and even the latest 12Z GFS model run which has this storm
moving further north and up into the SOCAL bight, deepening down to
977 DM with further intensification as it nears the coastal areas of
San Diego and Orange Counties, with a possible landfall occurring
during the early morning of Sunday. The important aspect to note
with Rachel is that it is going to obtain a lot of moisture with it,
as PWATs increase to nearly 600% as it is churning over the Pacific
waters well to our southwest through tomorrow, and these high PWATs
will maintain a nearly 350-400% capacity as this system eventually
propagates over southern California by later this weekend. What this
translates to is the potential for moderate to heavy rainfall rates,
that may occur with some of these heavier showers and/or
thunderstorms embedded within the rain shield associated with Rachel
as it transitions over the area. Because of this, confidence of
rainfall rates exceeding the 0.40 inches per hour for the
coastal/inland areas, and up to 0.60 inches for the
mountains/adjacent deserts, and 0.50 inch per hour rates for the
lower deserts/Coachella Valley, is quite high. Despite this,
confidence of total rainfall rates remains low, and is almost
exclusively depended on the track of Rachel, where she decides to
make landfall, and whether or not she maintains her tropical storm
status all the way to the region. Moreover, there will also be a
secondary wave transitioning over the region on Monday, and keeping
a chance of rain continuing through then, although considerably less
amounts expected. As of now, the highest rainfall rates are expected
along the southern facing slopes of the San Bernardino Mountains,
with amounts generally 1.00-1.25 inches, and locally up to 1.5
inches for some of the highest elevations. Due to this, WPC has
included our CWA in a marginal outlook for excessive rainfall from
the mountains to the deserts, and slight for the deserts. In
addition, strong and gusty winds could also cause for the need of
wind advisories. There will also be increased swell and wave
action, which may further contribute to erosion and possible
coastal flooding for some of the low-lying areas. All of this
will depend on the track of Rachel, so please stay tuned for
updates to this system and changes to our forecast as we continue
to know more about the potential impacts from Rachel to southern
California. Showers will continue into Monday as the secondary low
moves over the region and high pressure slowly builds back in.
Temperatures will begin to rebound a bit, but remain on the cooler
side through next Thursday.

&&

.AVIATION...
081830Z...Coast...SKC and VFR through the afternoon and evening.
Patchy low clouds and fog based around 600-800 feet MSL develop over
coastal areas starting 06-08z Fri. Clouds slowly expand in coverage
overnight reaching 10-15 miles inland. Minor vis reductions (1-5SM)
expected for elevated coastal terrain and western valleys. Clouds
scatter back toward the coastline 15-17z Fri.

Otherwise...mostly clear through Friday morning, except for shallow
cumulus over mountains 19-01Z.

&&

.MARINE...
No hazardous marine conditions are expected through Saturday. The
remnants of Rachel will bring a rapid increase in southerly swell
along with gusty winds and choppy seas Sunday, diminishing somewhat
on Monday.

&&

.BEACHES...
Surf of 3-5 feet with sets to 6 feet is expected on south and
southwest facing beaches through Friday. Elevated surf and strong
rip and longshore currents will create hazardous swimming
conditions. This elevated surf will combine with high tides during
the mornings and evenings to generate local coastal flooding and
beach erosion.

Another south swell arriving Sunday along with wind waves and
continued morning high tides near 7 feet will maintain a threat of
coastal flooding and beach erosion into Tuesday. Surf on Sunday is
currently expected to be 6-9 feet, decreasing to 3-6 feet for Monday
and Tuesday. There is still a lot of uncertainty in exact surf
heights for now. The Beach Hazards Statement and Coastal Flood
Advisory contain more details.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Extreme Heat Warning until 8 PM PDT this evening for Orange
     County Coastal Areas-Orange County Inland Areas-San
     Bernardino and Riverside County Valleys-The Inland Empire-
     San Diego County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Valleys-
     Santa Ana Mountains and Foothills.

     Beach Hazards Statement through Friday evening for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Coastal Flood Advisory until 2 PM PDT Tuesday for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Extreme Heat Warning until 8 PM PDT Friday for Coachella Valley-
     San Diego County Deserts-San Gorgonio Pass near Banning.

PZ...None.

&&
