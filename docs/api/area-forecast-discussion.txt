Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
146 AM PDT Tue Oct 20 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
A low pressure system moving southward off the California coast
will spreading cooling inland through Thursday. It is expected to
bring showers and slight chance of thunderstorms for late
Wednesday through Thursday is it moves inland through California
into Nevada. Then warmer and drier for next weekend.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

.SHORT TERM (Today through Thursday)...
The center of a closed low pressure system will move southward off
the California coast today, then move slowly eastward to near
Point Conception for early Thursday, then northeastward into
Nevada for Thursday evening. Cooling of high temperatures will
continue to spread inland for today and Wednesday. The greatest
cooling is expected on Thursday with the mountains and inland
valleys around 10 degrees cooler than Wednesday.

High temperatures on Thursday will range from around 5 degrees
below average near the coast to 15 to 20 degrees below average for
mountain elevations above 6000 feet. High temperatures on Thursday
will be in the 60s to around 70 for the coast and valleys and in
the 40s and 50s for the mountains.

Satellite imagery and surface reports show coastal low clouds
beginning to spread inland, but with cloud bases around 1000 feet
near the coast as the marine layer deepens. Through sunrise this
morning, the low clouds will increase in coverage for coastal
areas and spread into the far western valleys. The low clouds are
expected to spread into the far inland valleys or lower coastal
slopes of the mountains for late tonight as the marine layer
continues to deepen for today and tonight.

Moisture being drawn northward by the low pressure system off the
California coast could bring some mostly light showers to the
mountains for this afternoon and early evening, mainly for the San
Bernardino Mountains and for the Riverside County mountains.

As that low pressure system off the California coast begins to
move slowly eastward on Wednesday, showers could spread into
coastal areas during the afternoon on Wednesday. Those showers
are expected to spread farther inland for Wednesday night into
Thursday with the greatest chances for thunderstorms for late
Wednesday night into Thursday.

Through Thursday night, rainfall across Orange and southwestern
San Bernardino Counties could range from around 1 inch near the
coast to 1.5 to 2 inches in the mountains. Rainfall for San Diego
County will range from around 1 inch north to 0.50 to 0.75 south.
High desert areas will receive around 0.50 inch of rainfall with
0.25 inch or less for the lower deserts.

NBM probabilities for 0.10 inch or more of rainfall from
Wednesday morning through Thursday night exceed 90 percent for
the coast, valleys, and mountains. Probabilities for 0.50 inch or
more exceed 80 percent from the coast to the mountains as far
south as the northern half of San Diego County. Probabilities for
1 inch or more exceed 50 percent from the coast to the mountains
for Orange and southwestern San Bernardino Counties. Probabilities
for 2 inches or more exceed 10 percent from inland Orange County
to the mountains of southwestern San Bernardino County.

&&

.LONG TERM (Friday through Monday)...
Warming and drying is expected for the weekend into early next
week with some weak offshore winds possible during the weekend.
For Saturday through Monday, high temperatures for coastal areas
will range from 5 to locally 10 degrees above average with inland
areas around 5 degrees below average to locally 10 degrees below
average in the mountains. High temperatures for the coast and
valleys will be mostly in the mid 70s to lower 80s with the lower
deserts in the lower to mid 80s with the mountains in the 50s and
60s.

&&

.AVIATION...
200530Z...Coast...Gradually increasing low clouds based around 1000-
1200 feet MSL overnight. Vis reduced 3-5SM on mesas. Cigs lifting
some before scatter out 16-18Z Tuesday. Low clouds based around 1500-
2000 feet MSL to redevelop after 03Z Tuesday evening.

Otherwise...mostly clear and VFR conditions through Tuesday morning,
until SCT-BKN clouds based around 10 kft develop over inland areas,
especially mountains 19-01Z. Isold SHRA 21-01Z Tuesday.

&&

.MARINE...
No hazardous marine conditions are expected through Wednesday
morning. A storm system moves into the region later Wednesday
through Thursday, bringing gusts at or exceeding 20 knots and
associated choppy seas, along with a 10-20% chance of TSRA.

&&

.BEACHES...
Southerly swells will continue to bring surf of 3 to 6 feet with
local sets to 7 feet, highest at south and southwest-facing beaches
through Tuesday. This will bring hazardous swimming conditions for
most. While surf and rip currents will be elevated, daily high tides
will not be particularly high, and the threat of coastal flooding is
minimal. Details are in the Beach Hazards Statement.

&&

.CLIMATE...
Water levels at the coast are still running around 18 inches
higher than a year ago with water temperatures near the coast
still in the lower to mid 70s. The water temperature at Scripps
Pier on Monday was 74.5 F, a record warmest for the date and
around 8.5 degrees above the long-term average for the date.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.

&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Beach Hazards Statement through this evening for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

PZ...None.

&&

$$

AVIATION/MARINE/BEACHES...MM
