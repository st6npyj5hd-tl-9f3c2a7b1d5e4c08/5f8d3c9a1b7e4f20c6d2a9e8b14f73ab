Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
339 AM PST Fri Nov 13 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Cooler wit periods of precipitation today through late next week.
Light to moderate showers will continue this morning with chances
of isolated thunderstorms over the coastal waters locally into
the beaches. More widespread light to moderate showers tonight
into Saturday. In addition to periods of rain, winds are expected
to increase over the mountains, deserts, and coastal waters.
Sunday will be dry with our next chance of precipitation and
gusty winds Monday through Wednesday.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

...UPDATED AVIATION, MARINE, AND BEACHES DISCUSSIONS...

Isolated moderate showers have developed this morning ahead of the
upper level low. So far the only detected lightning strikes have
been closer to the center of the low, west-southwest of San Diego.
Showers will become more widespread in the early morning as the
upper low moves overhead, along with a 10-15% chance of
thunderstorms over the coastal waters and coastal areas. Rainfall
rates may briefly exceed 0.50"/hr in heavier showers, but this
will be highly localized as storm cores are quite small. Lighter,
more widely scattered showers persist into the afternoon with
residual moisture and weakly cyclonic flow in place. Through this
afternoon, rainfall totals will range from about 0.25-0.50" for
the coasts and valleys with local amounts to 0.75", and a few
hundredths to around 0.30" in the mountains. A few light showers
could spread into the high desert with accumulations generally
0.10" or less. Snow levels will be near 8,000-8,500 ft today with
only trace amounts on the higher peaks.

The next short-wave trough will move through tonight into Saturday
morning, though models have trended a bit further north with the
track of this wave and weaker forcing across Southern California.
Still, there is a well-defined front that is forecast to sweep
through, generating more widespread but lighter showers with peak
rainfall rates closer to 0.25"/hr and only around a 5% chance of
thunderstorms. Showers will end for most areas by late Saturday
morning, but scattered showers could persist into Saturday
afternoon for San Diego County, ending by Saturday evening.
Rainfall totals for this second system range from around
0.10-0.30" for the coast to the mountains with locally higher
amounts on the west facing coastal mountain slopes, less than
0.05" in the high desert, and no accumulations in the low desert.
The snow level remains around 8,500 ft during the bulk of the
precipitation, falling to around 7,000 ft behind the front as
precipitation tapers off. Any accumulation at the resort levels
will be less than an inch.

In addition to the precipitation, gusty southwest to west winds
develop across the coastal waters, mountains, and deserts this
afternoon and continuing into late Saturday morning, weakening
behind the front Saturday afternoon. Wind gusts in the mountains
and deserts will be between 30-40 mph with isolated higher gusts
in the passes. Winds may briefly gust to around 20-30 mph near the
immediate coast in the vicinity of the cold front Saturday
morning.

A brief break in the weather is expected on Sunday under weak
upper level ridging. High temperatures today and Saturday will be
around 5-10 degrees below normal, warming by about 3-6 degrees on
Sunday. Ensembles remain in good agreement with the timing of the
next trough, sweeping through California Monday into Tuesday.
Differences remain in the depth of the trough, resulting in more
spread in precipitation amounts and snow levels. Around 52% of
the members have a less amplified trough (less precipitation and
snow levels closer to 7,000 ft), while the remaining 48% have a
deeper trough (more precipitation and snow levels as low as 5,500
ft). Chances of at least 0.25" with this system range from 40-70%
from the coast to the mountains, around 30% in the high desert,
and 10% in the low desert. Chances of at least 0.50" range from
25-60% for the coast to the mountains, 10-20% in the high desert,
and less than 10% in the low desert. Another break it the weather
is expect on Wednesday, but another system follows quickly for
Thursday into Friday. Ensemble solutions really begin to diverge
on the track of this low - 58% have a more western/wetter track
similar to the deterministic GFS, while while the other 42% have
a drier/inland track similar to the deterministic EC. For now
precipitation chances are between 40-60% on Thursday, lowering to
15-20% by Friday afternoon.

&&

.AVIATION...
131130Z...Coast/Valleys...A band of SHRA/TSRA will move west to east
reducing vis 2-5SM and cigs down to 2500 feet MSL. After 16Z the
band will dissipate and ISO to SCT -SHRA will remain with cloud
bases closer to 3000-4000 feet MSL. After 08Z Saturday, more
widespread low clouds and a band of -RA will move northwest to
southeast through the region Saturday morning.

Mountains/Deserts...-SHRA in mountains with cloud bases occasionally
obscuring higher terrain. Winds increase after 20Z, with W/SW gusts
increasing to 25-35 knots (locally 40-45 kts) over typically windy
ridges, desert foothills, and through passes. Winds will gradually
taper off early Saturday.

&&

.MARINE...
At 2 AM, a band of showers and isolated thunderstorms was tracking
west to east through the coastal waters. Any thunderstorm that forms
will be capable of producing gusty, erratic winds, choppy seas,
lightning, and briefly heavy rainfall with reduced visibility. The
chance of thunderstorms and any associated hazards will diminish
after 8 AM. There is a Marine Weather Statement in effect through 8
AM. Southerly winds will increase today, turning westerly and
strengthening late this afternoon and continuing through Saturday
morning. Swell and wind waves will increase considerably at the same
time. A Small Craft Advisory is in effect through Saturday
afternoon. Winds and seas diminish late Saturday and Sunday, before
increasing again on Monday as the next weather system arrives.

&&

.BEACHES...
A series of swells (14-15 seconds) from the west-northwest (270-280
degrees) will generate elevated surf starting this afternoon, which
will increase to a peak of 5 to 9 feet for west and northwest facing
beaches Saturday and Sunday. A High Surf Advisory is in effect for
these two days with dangerous conditions for both swimming and
surfing. Surf begins to wane Sunday afternoon but will remain
elevated for west-facing beaches into Monday morning. Another
northwesterly swell will arrive late Monday, extending the threat
for high surf and strong rip currents.

Additionally, there is a threat for lightning at the beaches through
8 AM and a Beach Hazards Statement contains the details.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.

&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Beach Hazards Statement until 8 AM PST this morning for Orange
     County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     High Surf Advisory from 4 PM this afternoon to 10 AM PST Sunday
     for Orange County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal
     Areas.

PZ...Small Craft Advisory from 10 AM this morning to 4 PM PST
     Saturday for Coastal Waters from San Mateo Point to the
     Mexican Border and out to 10 nm-Waters from San Mateo Point
     to the Mexican Border Extending 10 to 60 nm out including
     San Clemente Island.
