FXUS66 KSGX 191929
AFDSGX

Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
1129 AM PST Thu Nov 19 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Cool weather will continue for the next week. Rain, isolated
thunderstorms, high elevation mountain snow, and stronger winds
are expected today as a storm system moves through California.
Dry but cool weather will follow Friday through Sunday. The next
system looks weaker and could bring light rain Sunday night and
Monday, potentially followed by another system mid to late next
week.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...


...UPDATED AVIATION, MARINE, AND BEACHES DISCUSSIONS...

Very light precipitation has begun to develop well ahead of the
main precipitation band/Atmospheric River, which is currently
moving through Central CA. The latest (07Z) run of the HRRR seems
to have a decent handle on the timing of the front, bringing
heavier rain to Orange/San Bernardino Counties beginning around
10 AM, then into central Riverside and NW San Diego County by
around 1 PM and continuing to move southeast through the
afternoon. While isolated rainfall rates over 1"/hr have been
observed in Santa Barbara County (similar to HRRR/REFS mean
maxima), rainfall intensity is forecast to weaken by the time it
reaches our forecast area, with max rates near 0.50-0.75"/hr north
of San Diego County, and around 0.30"/hr by the time the band
reaches San Diego County. This is still enough to produce roadway
flooding, especially in poor drainage areas, and rockslides in the
mountains. So far the only lightning has been observed in the Bay
Area, closer to the mid-level cold core. While this much colder
air never moves over So Cal, maintained slight thunderstorm
chances for the coastal waters east into the western valleys as
weak destabilization occurs this afternoon, especially with SSTs
still in the low to mid 70s.

Latest storm total rainfall calls for around 0.40-0.75" for Orange
County and the Inland Empire, locally up to 1" near the LA County
border, 0.25-0.50" for the San Diego County coastal areas/valleys,
0.50-1.50" in the mountains, highest on the south facing slopes,
0.10-0.30" in the high desert, and 0.10" or less in the low
desert. Snow levels will be 8,000 ft or higher during the heaviest
precipitation, falling to around 6,500 ft behind the cold front as
precipitation begins to taper off. While the highest peaks of the
San Gabriel/San Bernardino/San Jacinto Mountains will receive
around 3-5" of snow, mountain communities such as Big Bear will
likely see less than 1".

In addition to the precipitation, gusty southwest to west winds
are expected today for the desert mountain slopes into the
deserts, coastal waters, and briefly for the coastal areas as the
front passes this afternoon. For the mountains and deserts, peak
gusts of 45-55 mph are expected, with gusts closer to 25-35 mph
along the coast. A Wind Advisory remains in effect for the
mountains, high desert, and San Gorgonio Pass through 4 PM this
afternoon.

Cool but drier weather follows for Friday. A second short wave
digging down but passing to the northeast will produce a brief
round of weak offshore flow Saturday morning. Cool weather
persists on Sunday under weak troughing, then a weak short wave
moves through sometime late Sunday into Monday. Ensembles are
trending towards a weaker wave/further north path, and some
several members of the EPS, GEFS, and GEPS are completely dry, and
the AI versions of the EPS and GEFS are even drier. Overall it
looks like if we get any precipitation out of this system, it`ll
be very light. Weak offshore flow and ridging aloft on Tuesday
will produce minor warming and dry weather, though highs will
still be several degrees below normal.

There is the potential for another system towards the middle of
next week (including Thanksgiving), but there is huge model spread
on the evolution of that low. The 00Z GFS develops a deep low
which closes off and digs through NV/AZ, but this is not supported
by any of the ensemble clusters. The ensemble solutions range
anywhere from zonal flow or weak troughing (light to moderate
precipitation depending on if/how far south any short wave
develops) to weak ridging/drier weather. By Thanksgiving, around
half of the ensemble members have a slightly inside
trough/potential for light precipitation while the other half have
weak ridging for warmer and dry weather. Either way the weather
should be fairly well-behaved for the holiday.

&&

.AVIATION...
191915Z...BKN-OVC skies are covering the area as a narrow weak cold
front is moving through the region. RA/+RA from the narrow front is
sweeping through OC and the IE, having just moved south of KSAN and
passing over KONT/KSBD through the next half hour. By 21z, the main
front will likely be passing through northern SD County and the
eastern valleys of the IE (and VCSH through the lower deserts),
clearing through all of SD County by 01z Fri. Brief IFR vis/cigs
(~20-40 mins) during the frontal passage. Otherwise, mostly down to
MVFR cigs for SHRA.

-SHRA/SHRA to continue regionwide after the passage of the front,
tapering off through the evening for northern areas and tapering off
at night through SD County.

Gusty west winds expected at the coast and desert slopes through
tonight, weakening overnight.

&&

.MARINE...
Hazardous boating conditions to continue through Friday afternoon.
Strongest winds and choppy seas are moving through the waters this
afternoon, from northwest to southeast. A Small Craft Advisory is in
effect and contains more details. There is a 10-15 percent chance of
thunderstorms with the front. If any thunderstorms occur, they will
be capable of producing gusty/erratic winds and lightning, with a
slim chance of an isolated waterspout. No additional marine hazards
are expected late Friday through the weekend.

&&

.BEACHES...
A northwesterly swell is building today, elevating surf through
early Friday. Surf of 3 to 6 feet with sets to 7 feet are expected
on west facing beaches. Strong rip currents and minor coastal
flooding during high tides are expected. A Beach Hazards Statement
contains more details.

King Tides are expected to peak next week, but chances of tides
exceeding 7 feet start as early as Saturday Nov 21.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Beach Hazards Statement through Friday morning for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Wind Advisory until 4 PM PST this afternoon for Apple and
     Lucerne Valleys-Riverside County Mountains-San Bernardino
     County Mountains-San Diego County Mountains-San Gorgonio
     Pass near Banning.

PZ...Small Craft Advisory until 10 AM PST Friday for Coastal Waters
     from San Mateo Point to the Mexican Border and out to 10 nm-
     Waters from San Mateo Point to the Mexican Border Extending
     10 to 60 nm out including San Clemente Island.


&&

PUBLIC...SS
AVIATION/MARINE/BEACHES...Westerink
