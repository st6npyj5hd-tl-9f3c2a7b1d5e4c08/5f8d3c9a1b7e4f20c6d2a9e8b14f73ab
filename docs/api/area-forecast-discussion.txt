AFDSGX

Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
451 AM PDT Sun Sep 27 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Cloudy and cooler today along with a slight chance of light
showers for our southeastern regions through early Monday. The
main trough moves through Monday, bringing back the marine layer
and breezy conditions to the mountains and deserts. Weak high
pressure will then bring dry and warmer weather Tuesday into next
weekend.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

...UPDATED AVIATION/MARINE/BEACHES DISCUSSION...

Satellite shows upper level clouds streaming through the region,
sourced from moisture from Hurricane Odalys hundreds of miles to the
south-southwest and carried through the jet stream. The 00z 9/26
sounding shows a shallow moist profile at around 25,000 feet, which
is expected to deepen down to around 10,000 feet by this afternoon.

Today through Monday...Despite the near surface remaining drier,
PWAT`s today will increase to around 1.50" for San Diego County and
deserts east of the mountains, enough for extensive cloud cover
throughout the day but not enough for precipitation without any
background instability or forcing. As a result, precip chances above
10% have been reduced to southeastern portions of San Diego County
and other mountain peaks from this afternoon through early Monday
morning. Elsewhere, virga showers aloft may bring some sprinkles to
briefly reach the ground. Because of the cloud cover region-wide,
high temperatures will be noticeably cooler today relative to
yesterday, around 6-12 degrees cooler. A sharp boundary of drier air
aloft moves through SoCal during the day Monday, out ahead of the
trough axis, which barely dips south enough to bring cooler
temperatures aloft and thus cooler daytime temperatures despite
increasing sunshine. Monday will be the coolest day of the week by
far with highs 75-85 degrees across the coastal basin, 60s for the
mountains and mid-90s in the lower deserts. With the troughs gradual
passage in the afternoon, onshore winds will be breezy east of
mountain passes and along desert slopes (25-35 mph, higher through
Banning Pass) and the marine later will build into the evening, with
low clouds likely Monday night for much of the coastal basin.

Tuesday...With the trough axis moving east, a weak offshore gradient
between the Coastal Basin and the High Desert coupled with modest
northerly flow aloft will bring a decent (60%) chance for weak
northerly offshore flow. This would noticeably drop the relative
humidity Monday night into Tuesday for the deserts, mountains and
Inland Empire (especially near the Cajon Pass). High temperatures
increase noticeably to mid-80s to low-90s across the Coastal Basin
with perhaps a few low 80s at the coast.

Wednesday through the weekend...While offshore winds dissipate
Wednesday, daytime temperatures increase through the end of the week
while relative humidities generally continue to decrease for inland
areas. A ridge will be slowly developing out in the Pacific, moving
toward the southwest US through the end of the week.

By the end of the week, daytime RH`s drop to 15-25% for elevated
interior valleys, the Inland Empire, mountains and deserts (locally
down to 10% in the High Desert) while temperatures reach into the
mid-90s to low-100s in these areas. Even the lowland urban areas
just a few miles inland of the coast will likely (50-80% chance)
reach at least into the upper 80s and perhaps (20-60% chance) above
90F for Thurs/Fri/Sat.

&&

.AVIATION...
271145Z...Coast/Valleys...BKN-OVC high clouds AOA 15,000ft MSL will
continue for much of the TAF period. Very patchy low clouds mainly
offshore should stay over the waters this morning, where cigs are
near 500-800 ft MSL. Low clouds clearing completely by 15Z.
Increasing confidence on very patchy low clouds (if any) forming
tonight, with minimal cigs for coastal TAF sites through Monday
morning.

Mountains/Deserts...VFR prevails with BKN-OVC clouds 10-20 kft
through the period. Pockets of -SHRA possible between 00-14Z Monday,
little to no surface impact, but some brief lower cigs below 5000ft
possible. Best chance in eastern San Diego County.

&&

.MARINE...
There will likely be northwest wind gusts of 20-25 knots in the
outer waters Monday afternoon and evening, accompanied by choppy
mixed seas. Otherwise, no hazardous marine conditions are expected
through Thursday.

&&

.BEACHES...
Southwesterly swell (3-4 ft at 13 seconds from ~200 degrees) from
Hurricane Odalys persists through this weekend, bringing surf of 3-5
feet with sets up to 6 feet, highest along south and southwest
facing beaches. The swell from Hurricane Polo (3 ft at 14 seconds
from ~180 degrees) then increases on Monday. Given this direction,
most of San Diego County beaches will be largely sheltered from this
swell, but Orange County beaches (especially in north OC) should be
impacted. Meanwhile, a northwesterly swell (3-4 ft at 7 seconds from
~280 degrees) arrives later today and Monday. Between these two
swells, surf of 3-5 feet with sets to 7 feet looks probable along
south/southwest facing beaches with surf 3-5 feet along west-facing
beaches through Tuesday. With this elevated surf, there is a high
risk for rip currents through Tuesday.

Tides are already observed over 6.2 feet and are expected to
gradually increase into early next week, peaking around 7.0ft for
the late-morning high tides on Monday-Wednesday, highest on Tuesday.
A Beach Hazard Statement remains in effect through tonight due to
the risk of minor tidal overflow and minor coastal erosion. A
Coastal Flood Advisory is in effect from early Monday morning
through Wednesday afternoon to account for the highest tides
combining with the expected elevated surf. Primary impacts during
the morning high tides Monday-Wednesday will be minor coastal
flooding in low lying areas in addition to coastal erosion.

Please note - listed tide predictions have been adjusted to reflect
actual measured tide observations.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Beach Hazards Statement through late tonight for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Coastal Flood Advisory from 5 AM Monday to 5 PM PDT Wednesday
     for Orange County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal
     Areas.

PZ...None.

&&

$$

PUBLIC...Westerink
AVIATION/MARINE...APR
