Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
407 PM PST Wed Nov 11 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Cooler conditions with increased cloud coverage is expected for
Thursday through early next week. Periods of rain are expected
Friday through early next week. The first round is expected Friday
morning, with chances of isolated thunderstorms over the coastal
waters locally into the beaches. The next chance will be Friday
evening into Saturday, with more widespread light to locally
moderate showers. In addition to periods of rain, winds are
expected to increase over the mountains and deserts. Sunday could
see mostly dry conditions with our next chances of precipitation
and increased winds Monday and Tuesday.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...


...UPDATED AVIATION DISCUSSION...

Another day of high temperatures 10 to 15 degrees above average for
coastal areas and 5 to 7 degrees above average elsewhere. An
incoming upper level low will push the ridge over the Southwest US
further east by Thursday, bringing cooler conditions and increased
onshore flow. Highs on Thursday are expected to be 3 to 5 degrees
cooler than today for areas west of the mountains with more
noticeable cooling for Friday. Below average temperatures are
expected for Saturday through next Wednesday.

The aforementioned upper level low will dig south and move east into
Northern Baja on Friday. This will bring periods of rain to the area
by early Friday morning. The highest chances of rain for this first
period of rain are along the coast. Additionally, there is potential
for isolated thunderstorms to develop over the waters, locally into
some coastal communities. The thunderstorm chances are largely
focused to coastal waters and the coast due to the temperature
difference between sea surface temperatures (running well above
average for this time of year) and the cold temperatures associated
with the passing low pressure system. High resolution guidance is
picking up on an increase in instability with CAPE values increasing
to 500-1000 J/kg early Friday morning. There is some potential for
thunderstorms to locally reach coastal communities but atmospheric
instability drops drastically the further inland you go, which will
limit thunderstorm potential. Where thunderstorms develop, expect
erratic winds and locally heavy rain that may lead to localized
flooding. Rainfall Friday morning is generally expected to be on
the lighter side for inland areas. Isolated rainfall rates of 0.40-
0.60 inches per hour are most likely to develop along the coast
where thunderstorms or heavier rain develops.

Chances of rain decrease during the day Friday, but isolated light
showers at times cannot be ruled out. Rainfall chances increase
again later in the day Friday as another upper level low moves east
through Central California. This will bring another round of rain
Friday evening into Saturday, which is expected to be more
widespread across the coast, valleys, and mountains. Peak rainfall
rates of 0.20-0.30 inches per hour are expected during this time.
Rainfall is expected to taper off from north to south late Saturday
morning through the early afternoon. Dry conditions look likely for
Saturday evening into Sunday.

Another low pressure system is expected to dig south along the West
Coast sometime around Monday and Tuesday. Ensemble guidance is in
good agreement that precipitation will develop but there is low
confidence in exact timing of rainfall and amounts. This system also
looks like it has enough cold air associated with it to drop the
snow levels below 8000 ft, although there are model differences in
just how low the snow level will get.

Winds are expected to increase over the mountains and deserts with
each passing system. Expect a slight increase in winds late
Thursday, with a more noticeable increases Friday evening into
Saturday. Peak gusts Friday evening into Saturday will be mainly 35
to 45 mph, with gusts up to 55 mph through wind prone passes. Winds
are expected to increase again early next week with the next trough
passage, with the track of the low the main determining factor in
expected wind speeds.

Additional precipitation and winds are possible for the latter half
of next week, but there is low confidence in details. Continue to
monitor the forecast for more information.

&&

.AVIATION...
120000Z...Mostly SKC and VFR conditions through tomorrow. Localized
patchy FG near the coastline (15-25% chance) between 10z-16z.
Increasing low/mid-level cloud cover across the coastal basin after
22z Thu.

&&

.MARINE...
Isolated thunderstorms possible over the marine waters early Friday
morning, beginning before sunrise with decreasing chances by Friday
afternoon. Onshore winds increase late Friday and seas build
Saturday, which are likely to generate hazardous boating conditions.
Winds diminish late Saturday and seas diminish late Sunday.

&&

.BEACHES...
A series of swells (14-15 seconds) from the west-northwest (270-280
degrees) will generate elevated surf starting Friday, with higher
surf and sets to 7-8 feet by Saturday. Surf begins to wane on Sunday
but may remain elevated for west-facing beaches into Monday.
Additionally, isolated thunderstorms will be possible at the beaches
early Friday to late Friday morning.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...None.
PZ...None.

&&

AVIATION/MARINE/BEACHES...Westerink
