Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
257 AM PST Tue Nov 17 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Cool and unsettled weather will continue this coming week. A
weather system will move out this morning with showers ending, but
strong winds lingering today. Fair weather Wednesday will be
followed by the next storm system arriving Thursday with rain, a
slight chance of thunderstorms, and strong winds. Friday and
Saturday are expected to be mostly dry, with additional chances of
precipitation on Sunday or Monday. High surf will lead to periods
of minor coastal flooding during high tides.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

Showers will continue to track eastward across mountains, deserts,
and funky little island bands of showers moving through southwest
San Diego County. All showers will end soon and few if any
showers should linger after sunrise. Stronger winds especially in
the coastal waters, mountains, and deserts will increase this
morning and peak around midday before gradually diminishing late
this afternoon and evening. Wind gusts in mountains and deserts
will be 25-40 mph. Top gusts on mountain ridges and through
mountain passes will exceed 40 mph, with very isolated gusts
around 50 mph. It will turn mostly sunny today, but cool and
breezy, with highs in upper 60s at lower elevations, lower 70s in
the low desert, 50s in the High Desert, and upper 30s to lower 50s
in the mountains. These max temps are roughly 2-12 degrees below
average. Wednesday looks mostly sunny and dry and not windy but
only a couple degrees warmer as high pressure does a poor job of
building. The next weather system with rain and wind arrives
Thursday morning in Orange and western San Bern Counties,
gradually moving southeast across the region during the day. The
details are becoming better resolved, so we can say with greater
confidence our most likely scenario. By the time the storm wraps
up and moves east early Friday, rainfall should total a little
over 1 inch in the mountains and areas in the L.A. Basin. Amounts
drop off to the south and east with San Diego and Riverside
Counties receiving less than 0.75 inch. The area around San Diego
may get only 0.25 inch. Snow levels will drop to around 6,500 feet
by the time the precip ends, which should give Big Bear light
snowfall, but a few inches could accumulate above 7,500 feet, such
as on the higher ski slopes. Winds Thursday will be similar to
today`s winds, with top gusts in mountains and deserts around
35-50 mph. Friday and Saturday look fair and dry as the jet stream
pauses the parade of systems crossing the Pacific. By Sunday and
Monday, things get really uncertain. It could continue dry, but
most ensemble solutions bring in a weak system that would bring
light rain either Sunday, Sunday night, or Monday. After that,
there are some solutions building an anomalously strong ridge over
the West that would bring dry and very warm pre- Thanksgiving
weather. Other solutions keep us in this unsettled, cool, and
sometimes wet and windy pattern.

&&

.AVIATION...
181100Z...Coast/Valleys...ISO-SCT -SHRA continues to move out of the
region with MVFR conditions expected only with any passing
-SHRA. Gusty Winds decrease again from north to south through 18z,
but intermittent gusts to 20 knots continue through the afternoon
for San Diego County coastal areas. Clouds will regather later this
evening along the coastal areas of bases around 3000-4000 ft MSL,
with a low (10-20%) chance of going BKN intermittently (mainly from
10-14z Wed) along the coastal areas, and possibly at KSAN.

Mountains/Deserts...VFR conditions will become MVFR intermittently
for the mountains this early morning with -SHRA eventually tapering
off. Clouds scattering later this morning. S/W winds increasing
across the desert slopes after 03Z Tue with gusts 30-45 kts at times
by later this afternoon, then decreasing through the evening.

&&

.MARINE...
Winds and seas with 25-30 knot gusts expected in addition to steep,
high seas of 7-10 feet with a period of 8-15 seconds. Wind gusts
will be strongest near San Clemente Island, where near-gale
conditions are possible. A Small Craft Advisory remains in effect
through 4 AM Wednesday due to the hazardous conditions expected from
the gusty winds and high seas. Winds and seas will slowly diminish
Wednesday morning.

Conditions look to deteriorate once again Thursday as the next storm
system approaches. Steep, high seas of 7-10 ft along with wind gusts
peaking 20-30 knots for Thursday into Friday morning.

&&

.BEACHES...
A series of a mixture of long-period (3-5 feet at 15-17 seconds) and
short period (6-8 feet at 7-8 seconds) swells, both from the west-
northwest (280-290 degrees), will generate periods of elevated and
high surf through much of this week. The next surge of northwesterly
swells arriving now will elevate surf to 6 to 9 feet, with sets to
11 feet along west-facing beaches. This maintains the threat for
high surf and strong rip currents through mid-week. A High Surf
Advisory remains in effect through 4 AM Wednesday.

The shorter period northwest swell will wane Tuesday afternoon,
leaving a 3-5 ft swell with a 13-14 second period for Wednesday.
This will result in a brief decrease in surf, down to 3-5 feet with
sets to 6 feet. However, northwesterly swell builds again Thursday,
elevating surf once again through Friday.

Coastal flooding is expected at times through the week. High surf
will contribute to increased coastal flood chances even though tides
will be under 6.5 ft through Friday. King Tides are expected to peak
next week, but chances of tides exceeding 7 ft start as early as
Saturday Nov 21.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.

&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...High Surf Advisory until 4 AM PST Wednesday for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

PZ...Small Craft Advisory until 4 AM PST Wednesday for Coastal Waters
     from San Mateo Point to the Mexican Border and out to 10 nm-
     Waters from San Mateo Point to the Mexican Border Extending
     10 to 60 nm out including San Clemente Island.

&&

PUBLIC...MM
AVIATION/MARINE/BEACHES...Stewey
