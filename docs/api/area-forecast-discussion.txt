Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
426 PM PST Mon Nov 2 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Quiet weather with slightly below normal temperatures through
Wednesday and a lackluster marine layer. Warmer and drier weather
returns late week, with well-above-normal temperatures and Santa
Ana winds for the weekend.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...


...UPDATED AVIATION DISCUSSION...

The deep trough responsible for our rainy and cold Halloween weekend
has moved into the Great Basin, evident on mid-level water vapor
satellite imagery. On the back side, a thermal ridge axis sets up
over California as temperatures warm today around 5-10 degrees
over Sunday with clear skies. This upper level ridge is somewhat
compressed, situated between the aforementioned trough over the
Great Basin and a deepening low off the coast of Oregon. This
ridge will likely be pushed out before it can be too helpful,
with the Oregon low moving into Northern California by Tuesday
night. Despite the significant warm up today, temperatures will
hold steady with a lackluster marine layer into Wednesday given
the quick changes in the upper level pattern.

Gusty onshore winds accompany the Oregon low on Tuesday as the
gradient tightens over SoCal. Winds will pick up over the coastal
waters as early as late morning on Tuesday, with inland spread
through the afternoon. Gusts will reach up to 25-30 mph over the
waters and as high as 20 mph for coastal and valley areas. Winds
will decrease a bit as the low moves inland, likely calming by
sunrise Wednesday morning. Some models continue to support light
rainfall Wednesday morning for far southern San Diego County as
this trough moves across, but chances only sit around 15-25% at
this point. Still, it wouldn`t be surprising if some light wetting
rain was observed early Wednesday morning, primarily for SD
County south of the 56.

As previously mentioned, temperatures won`t be impacted much by the
low as it swings across, likely out of California by peak heating
Wednesday afternoon. As the low moves off, a positively tilted
ridge will move ashore, bringing subsidence for Thursday/Friday as
areas west of the mountains finally warm back above normal. Late
week into early weekend, another significant pattern change will
affect much of the western United States, with a very large ridge
stretching from northern Canada down into the Desert Southwest. As
high pressure moves over the Four Corners Saturday, offshore
winds will increase for most of California, bringing about well-
above-normal temperatures for areas west of the mountains. These
dry, downsloping winds will push temperatures to the 80s for even
coastal communities, around 10-15 degrees above normal, and minor
to moderate Heatrisk will return for the western portions of the
area. Current forecast indicates that by Saturday, temperatures
near the coast may be even hotter than the Coachella Valley.
Humidity will plummet, potentially as low as 15% for Saturday and
Sunday afternoons. While there is still some disagreement in the
placement of the ridge for the weekend, which will drastically
influence the Santa Ana wind strength, most models remain in favor
of the significant warming and stronger easterly winds this
weekend. Should this pan out, it will be quite the contrast to the
past gloomy Halloween weekend.

&&

.AVIATION...
030000...VFR conditions expected through the period with increasing
clouds AOA 20kft this evening. Weakly offshore winds will very
likely keep any low clouds offshore into Tue AM. High-level clouds
return Tue PM, then eventually low level clouds above 2,000 ft MSL
develop over SD County.

&&

.MARINE...
Weak winds expected tonight through Tuesday morning, followed by
more elevated onshore winds Tuesday afternoon and evening, with
gusts in the outer coastal waters potentially exceeding 20 knots.

&&

.BEACHES...
Tides are expected to increase for the end of the week, reaching 7
ft as early as Thursday in Orange County and Friday in San Diego
County. Tides will peak over the weekend into early next week at 7.5-
7.7 ft. Minor coastal flooding is possible. We`re monitoring the
potential for an incoming west swell of 3 ft from 290 with a 14-16
second period. If this swell materializes, max surf of 6 to 7 feet
are possible, which would increase the flood potential on west
facing beaches.

Reminder, tides mentioned have been corrected to reflect the current
trends in actual tide observations.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Beach Hazards Statement until 6 PM PST this evening for Orange
     County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

PZ...Small Craft Advisory from 1 PM Tuesday to 4 AM PST Wednesday for
     Waters from San Mateo Point to the Mexican Border Extending
     10 to 60 nm out including San Clemente Island.


&&
