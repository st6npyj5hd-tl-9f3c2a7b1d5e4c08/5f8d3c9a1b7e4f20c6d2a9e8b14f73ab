FXUS66 KSGX 151049
AFDSGX

Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
249 AM PST Sun Nov 15 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
A series of low pressure systems will keep temperatures near or
below normal throughout the next 7 days. The first of these will
impact southern California primarily on Monday and Tuesday. It will
bring another round of precipitation and gusty onshore winds,
along with a slight chance of thunderstorms over the coastal
waters and beach hazards. Another system late in the week will
likely bring similar impacts but may have stronger onshore winds.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...


...UPDATED AVIATION DISCUSSION...

This morning...Satellite imagery shows low clouds extending from
the coastal waters to the low deserts. A weak wave undercutting
the dynamic ridge currently over SoCal is likely producing a low-
level circulation which is bringing low clouds from the south into
the Borrego Desert and the Coachella valley. Radar shows a cluster
of weak echoes nearly stationary over the coastal waters. These
are light showers. High-resolution short-range models show these
echoes (light showers) remaining nearly stationary until Monday
when a low pressure system moves in from the northwest.

Ahead of this system a transient (dynamic) ridge will bring fair
weather and slight warming for today, although scattered low
clouds will persist. Daytime highs today will be mostly about 5
degrees higher than yesterday.

The first low pressure system will bring another round of widespread
and organized precipitation. Ahead of the main band of rain and
showers, light precipitation could begin as early as Monday morning.
The main band of precipitation will move through SoCal from northwest
to southeast Monday night/Tuesday Morning, moving off to the east
by late Tuesday afternoon. As the upper level trough axis
approaches, enhanced instability and dynamic forcing could produce
some isolated thunderstorms late Monday/early Tuesday. Snow
levels will likely remain above 7,000 ft through Tue morning
before dropping to around 6,500 ft Tuesday afternoon. By then,
most of the precip will have moved east. This system will not have
access to large amounts of precipitable moisture. As a result,
current rainfall estimates range from 0.10-0.25 inch in the
coastal areas and valleys to between 0.25 and 0.40 inch on the
coastal mtn slopes. Most of the snowfall will remain above 7,000
ft and accumulations are unlikely to exceed about 1 inch. Onshore
flow begins to strengthen on Monday then peaks late Tuesday
morning, with southwest to west winds gusting 35-40 mph in the
mtns and adjacent desert slopes. This will not be a particularly
cold system and daytime high temperatures on Tue will be similar
to what they were yesterday...remaining in the 60s west of the
mtns, the 50s in the high desert and the 70s in the lower desert.
Temperatures in the mtns will range from the 40s in San Diego
County to the 20s in the higher elevations of the San Bernardino
and San Jacinto Mtns.

Another brief period of fair and (slightly) warmer weather on Wed
under a weak transient ridge sandwiched between low pressure
systems. Daytime high temps will likely still be 5-10 degrees
below normal in the inland valleys and as much as 10-15 degrees
below normal in the mtns.

Although the timing and track of the next system are still
somewhat uncertain, it does seem likely that we will get more
unsettled weather for Thursday into Saturday. Colder conditions
with widespread precipitation is likely for late Thursday and
Friday. This system does seem to have tighter pressure gradients
so is likely to produce stronger westerly winds than the
preceding event. Winds will likely peak Thu night/Fri morning.
The system will move east on Sat with precip tapering off Saturday
morning.

&&

.AVIATION...
151200Z...Coast/Valleys...ISO-SCT -SHRA is lingering off the San
Diego County coast this morning and will continue to do so into the
afternoon, though the showers will gradually drift further offshore.
In addition to patches of low clouds based near 1000 ft MSL along
the immediate coasts this morning, FEW-SCT clouds based 3000-5000 ft
MSL will also linger into the afternoon. With this, VFR conditions
are expected to persist through Sunday morning. VCSH (and locally
lower CIGs/VIS) will spread back towards the coast around 10z
Monday, with more widespread -SHRA expected after 12z.

Mountains/Deserts...SCT-BKN cloud bases 3-6 kft MSL in place across
the coastal mountain foothills obscuring higher terrain will
gradually decrease in coverage throughout the morning. Otherwise,
VFR conditions prevail throughout the period.

&&

.MARINE...
Conditions have improved over the waters this morning, and will
remain sub-hazardous until Monday. Scattered light rain showers are
lingering off the coasts, and will continue through much of the day
today.

Winds and seas ramp back up Monday afternoon with 25-30 knot gusts
expected in addition to steep, high seas of 8-11 feet with an
average period of 8 seconds. Wind gusts will be strongest near San
Clemente Island, where near-gale conditions are possible. With this,
a Small Craft Advisory has been issued for both the near-shore and
outer waters zones due to the hazardous conditions expected from the
gusty winds and high seas. This Small Craft Advisory in effect from
4 PM Monday until 4 AM Wednesday. These conditions will persist into
early Wednesday morning with improving conditions by Wednesday
afternoon.

Conditions look to deteriorate once again come Thursday afternoon as
the next storm system approaches. Steep, high seas of 7-9 ft are
expected with wind gusts near 25 kts for Thursday into Friday.

&&

.BEACHES...
A series of a mixture of long-period (3-5 feet at 15-17 seconds) and
short period (6-8 feet at 7-8 seconds) swells, both from the west-
northwest (280-290 degrees), will generate periods of elevated and
high surf through much of this week. The current swell that produced
waves up to 10-12 feet yesterday will gradually weaken today and
into early Monday, but surf will remain elevated at 3-6 feet, with
sets to 7-8 feet for west-facing beaches.

The next surge of northwesterly swells arrives Monday afternoon,
ramping surf back up to 6 to 8 feet, with sets to 10 feet along west-
facing beaches. This will extend the threat for high surf and strong
rip currents into mid-week. As a result, the High Surf Advisory
currently in effect has been extended through 4 AM Wednesday.

&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...High Surf Advisory until 4 AM PST Wednesday for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

PZ...Small Craft Advisory from 4 PM Monday to 4 AM PST Wednesday for
     Coastal Waters from San Mateo Point to the Mexican Border
     and out to 10 nm-Waters from San Mateo Point to the Mexican
     Border Extending 10 to 60 nm out including San Clemente
     Island.


&&
