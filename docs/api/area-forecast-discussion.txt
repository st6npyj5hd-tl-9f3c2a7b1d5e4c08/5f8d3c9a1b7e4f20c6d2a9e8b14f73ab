Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
241 AM PDT Sat Oct 31 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Isolated light showers will continue for the coastal basin
this morning. More widespread moderate showers develop late this
evening into Sunday morning, becoming more scattered during the
day Sunday before tapering off Sunday afternoon and evening. Dry
and warmer for the remainder of the week with highs near to a few
degrees below normal each day.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...


...UPDATED AVIATION DISCUSSION...

Morning water vapor imagery shows an upper level low near the Pacific
NW coast with a trough extending south well off the coast of
central Baja. The base of this trough is tapping some tropical
moisture with PW in excess of 2" currently observed on satellite.
The bulk of this moisture is forecast to remain to our south, but
a weak and brief Atmospheric River is poised to move into far
Southern CA this evening into Sunday morning. Ensemble mean IVT
from the GEFS and EPS are on the order of 300-400 kg/m/s across
San Diego County, and closer to 250 kg/m/s further north. A few
light showers are possible this morning ahead of the trough, but
the bulk of the precipitation from this system is expected to
occur late this evening into Sunday morning. The majority of the
high-res guidance is holding off on the heavier/more widespread
precipitation until after 8 PM, so most will likely luck out on
dry candy collecting conditions this evening. For the most part
rainfall rates will be around a third-inch per hour or less, with
isolated rates of half-inch per hour or more mainly in San Diego
County. Showers will become more scattered and lighter by mid-
morning Sunday, then tapering off from north to south Sunday
afternoon, ending by Sunday evening.

Current rainfall forecast is for around 0.35-0.70" for the coastal
areas and valleys, 0.50-1" in the mountains, and around 0.10-0.30"
in the deserts, with the highest accumulations in San Diego
County. Snow levels will be over 10,000 ft during the heaviest
precipitation, falling to around 9,000 ft as precipitation tapers
off this evening, leaving only trace amounts on the highest peaks
of the San Bernardino and San Jacinto Mountains.

Transitory ridging and weak offshore flow builds in for Monday
and Tuesday for relatively warmer weather, though highs will be a
few degrees below normal inland - generally low to mid 70s for the
coasts and valleys, low 50s to low 60s in the mountains, upper
60s in the high desert, and mid 70s to low 80s in the low desert.
Another trough is forecast to move inland across the West Coast
towards the middle of the week, though there is a lot of spread in
the ensembles on the timing of this trough. For now forecast
remains dry with this system with just some minor cooling. Weak
troughing remains in place through Friday with little change in
high temperatures each day.

&&

.AVIATION...
310940Z...Multiple cloud layers across the coastal basin between
3000-6000 ft MSL with widespread BKN high-lvl clouds AOA 10,000 ft
MSL. ISO -SHRA possible through this morning mostly along the coast,
bringing intermittent and brief MVFR cigs 2500-3500 ft MSL as they
pass. Heavier/more widespread precip moves into the coastal basin
after 03Z Sun, bringing vis reductions as well as cig reductions
under heavier rain, especially in SD County where rain is likely to
be heavier overall. Low to moderate confidence on timing of heavier
rain occurrence in TAFs.

&&

.MARINE...
Westerly winds increase late tonight into Sunday afternoon, but
strongest wind gusts will likely stay below 21 knots. There is a 5%
chance of isolated thunderstorms Sunday morning. Weaker winds
expected Monday, followed by more elevated onshore winds Tuesday
afternoon and evening, with gusts in the outer coastal waters
potentially exceeding 20 knots.

&&

.BEACHES...
An increasing southwesterly swell (3 ft at 15 seconds from 200
degrees) will bring elevated surf this weekend into Monday, with
breaking wave heights 3 to 6 feet Sunday/Monday. While high tides
will continue to wane through the weekend, tides will remain just
above 6 feet which could still lead to local minor tidal overflow
during high tides where breaking waves approach 5-6 feet, mostly on
southwest facing beaches Sun/Mon.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...None.
PZ...None.

&&
