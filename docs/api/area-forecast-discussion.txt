Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
425 PM PDT Tue Jul 28 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Near to slightly above average temperatures will only last through
Wednesday before another heatwave begins. High pressure to the
east is expected to strengthen and move westward late this week,
bringing a return of moderate to major HeatRisk for Thursday
onward into early next week. Temperatures peak Saturday and Sunday
with some record highs expected in the mountains and deserts along
with locally extreme HeatRisk. The marine layer and associated
morning clouds will become more shallow and confined to coastal
areas by late this week.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...


...UPDATED AVIATION DISCUSSION...

The main story this week is the incoming heatwave beginning on
Thursday. An upper ridge currently located to the east will
strengthen and move westward through this weekend. There is strong
agreement among models that 500 mb heights will peak somewhere
around 597 to 600 dm on Saturday, setting the stage for the
hottest temperatures of the year so far for inland areas. High
temperatures ranging from a few to 8 degrees above normal on
Thursday will combine with slightly above normal lows to create
moderate to locally major HeatRisk for inland areas. By the
weekend, both high and low temperatures increase to 5 to 12
degrees above normal. Peak temperatures this weekend will range
from the 80s in coastal areas to 115 to 120 in the low deserts.
90s expected in the western valleys and mountains below 7000 feet,
low 100s in eastern San Diego Valleys, and 100s to near 110 in
the Inland Empire and High Desert. This will create moderate
HeatRisk for coastal areas, moderate to major HeatRisk for inland
valleys and mountains, and major to extreme HeatRisk in the
deserts. Some daily record highs are expected to fall in the
mountains and deserts this weekend.

As a consequence of the building upper ridge, the marine layer is
expected to become more shallow and confined to coastal areas by
late week. Minor visibility reductions will become possible for
coastal areas each morning this weekend. Additionally, increasing
inversion strength may allow for marine layer clouds to persist
into the afternoon along the immediate coastline. This adds
uncertainty to current high temperature forecasts. Current highs
are expected to range 5 to 10 degrees above normal (generally low
80s) along the coastline this weekend. If clouds are present in
the afternoons, highs will be closer to seasonal averages.

The ridge is expected to weaken early next week, bringing a
slight cooling trend. However, high temperatures are generally
expected to remain above average. Thus, moderate heatrisk will
likely continue for inland valleys/mountains with major HeatRisk
in the deserts. Model guidance diverges more significantly from
mid-next week onward, though there is some indication that a more
favorable monsoon pattern will re-emerge.

&&

.AVIATION...
290000Z....Coast/Valleys...Low clouds with bases 900-1400 ft MSL
redeveloping along the coast 02-04Z, spreading 10-15 mi inland by
10Z. There is a slight (20%) chance of CIGS at KONT 12-16Z. Vis 3-
5SM where stratus nears higher coastal terrain and in the western
valleys. Low clouds clearing to the coast 15-18Z, redeveloping after
30/00Z with similar bases.

Mountains/Deserts...Mostly clear skies with unrestricted vis through
Wednesday evening.

&&

.MARINE...
No hazardous marine weather is expected through Sunday.

&&

.BEACHES...
A south swell from 200 degrees of 3-4 ft feet at 17 seconds will
produce surf of 3-6 feet with sets to 7 feet into Wednesday. Strong
rip currents are expected along with minor tidal overflow during the
evening high tide.

Wednesday, a 4-5 ft swell from 160-180 degrees with a period of 13-
15 seconds associated with Hurricane Genevieve will arrive. Surf of
5-9 ft is expected on south facing beaches in Orange County with
surf around 3-6 ft elsewhere. Surf will peak on Thursday and Friday,
when the swell direction shifts to 180-190 degrees. Strong rip and
longshore currents will create dangerous swimming conditions at most
area beaches. In addition to the high surf, evening high tides will
be above 6 ft this week. The combination of high tides and surf
increases the risk of coastal flooding. The swell, period, and tides
are expected to decrease over the weekend.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Beach Hazards Statement through Wednesday afternoon for Orange
     County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Coastal Flood Advisory from 5 PM Wednesday to 3 AM PDT Saturday
     for Orange County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal
     Areas.

     High Surf Advisory from 5 PM Wednesday to 5 PM PDT Saturday for
     Orange County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Heat Advisory from 10 AM Friday to 8 PM PDT Sunday for Orange
     County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Extreme Heat Warning from 10 AM Thursday to 8 PM PDT Sunday for
     Apple and Lucerne Valleys-Coachella Valley-Riverside County
     Mountains-San Bernardino and Riverside County Valleys-The
     Inland Empire-San Diego County Deserts-San Diego County
     Mountains-San Gorgonio Pass near Banning.

     Extreme Heat Warning from 10 AM Friday to 8 PM PDT Sunday for
     Orange County Inland Areas-San Diego County Valleys-Santa
     Ana Mountains and Foothills.

     Heat Advisory from 10 AM Thursday to 8 PM PDT Sunday for San
     Bernardino County Mountains.

PZ...None.
