AFDSGX

Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
1023 PM PST Fri Nov 27 2026

.KEY MESSAGES...

- Dry weather with a cooling trend will occur through the weekend.

- A strengthening low pressure storm system accompanied by a weak
  to moderate strength atmospheric river will bring widespread
  heavier rain, high elevation snow, and windy conditions next
  Tuesday into Wednesday.

- Chances for light precipitation and cooler weather continue into
  later next week as another weather system nears the region.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

...UPDATED AVIATION DISCUSSION...

A ridge of high pressure remains stretched over our region southward
across the E Pac. This will give one last very warm day west of
the mountains today. High clouds will move in more on Saturday as
the ridge breaks down some and a low pressure system skirts by to
the north. This will allow for cooling, mainly across the western
valleys with highs lowering into the 70s to near 80 degrees.
Cooling spreads further inland toward the mountains by Sunday as
zonal flow takes over the region, with highs staying in the 50s
and 60s across the mountains. This will be accompanied by greater
onshore flow and elevated breezes across the mountains and deserts.

As the low pressure system to the north moves ashore, increased
moisture with the associated trough will begin to bring a greater
chance for light showers to form over Southern California by late
Sunday night into Monday morning. Model data is in consensus that
most of this precipitation will be light for areas from the coast to
the coastal slopes (most areas receiving near one quarter of an inch
of rain or less). Temperatures will continue to cool within 5
degrees of normal as cloudier skies and light showers move in.

As this occurs, a low pressure system off the coast will greatly
deepen as it moves in from the north and west. The system will draw
in large amounts of moisture from the southwest and west,
developing into an atmospheric river. Model consistency with this
particular storm system is quite impressive this far out, in terms
of timing and becoming more consistent with precipitation totals.
As we look at timing, heavier showers will begin to move into the
area sometime late Monday night or Tuesday morning. Models have
shown subtle increases in IVT values from past runs with
increasing confidence in seeing a weak to locally moderate
atmospheric river event. The system is expected to move through
the area, mainly from northwest to southeast on Tuesday, ending
sometime Wednesday morning or afternoon. From run to run, model
ensembles continue to hone in on heavier precipitation to move in
over the region. This, along with increased instability and warmer
ocean temperatures, will contribute to a greater chance for
thunderstorms to occur as well. If these events do unfold, there
will be an increased risk of flash flooding impacts. Since we are
still this far out in time from the event, changes in the track
and strength of the system are still possible, which may have
large effects on precipitation totals and overall impacts. The
enhanced upper level jet associated with the system will also
bring increased winds across the area, but confidence in exact
speeds remains low, though the most impactful winds look to be
near the coastal areas.

In terms of how much rain and mountain snow we will see is still
uncertain, though confidence is increasing in bouts of heavy
rainfall coming to fruition. GEFS members are drier than EPS
members, but still wet overall, showing a general 1"+ to locally
near 3" totals for areas west of the mountains, highest across the
LA basin. The deserts look to get in on the rainfall too, but
models show a bit more of a spread in these areas, as we usually
see in these events. Right now, the lower deserts have a good
chance to see near one half inch in total, though some areas may
see over one inch. Higher totals look likely across areas of the
high desert as well. As is per usual with these types of systems,
snow levels will start on the higher side, then fall as the system
passes through. This is still quite preliminary, but snow levels
will start somewhere over 8,000 feet Tuesday morning, lowering in
the vicinity over 6,000 feet, which will give the chance for snow
showers across the mountain villages of the San Bernardino
Mountains. Again, expect these rainy, windy and snowy details to
change in the coming days. All those across our forecast area
should use this time to continue to monitor the weather forecast
and prepare for flooding and wind impacts.

As the storm system moves out of the area on Wednesday, a weak ridge
may form in its place, as tropical moisture to the south lingers.
This should bring a brief reprieve from the wet weather later on
Wednesday into early Thursday of next week. Model ensembles continue
to point to a troughing pattern maintaining itself across California
as we slide into the latter part of next week. Long-range models
show the potential for a weak area of low pressure to near the
region by this time, which may bring the chance for more cooler
and wetter weather.

&&

.AVIATION...
280600Z...Increasing SCT-BKN high-level clouds overnight will likely
prevent any widespread low-level cloud cover for coastal areas.
However patchy, intermittently impactful CIGs with bases 700-1200 ft
MSL are possible tonight (25% chance vcnty KSAN 06-12Z, 15% chance
vcnty KSNA 03-14Z). Otherwise, VFR conditions continue through
Saturday evening.

&&

.MARINE...
No hazardous marine conditions expected through Monday. Winds become
southwesterly early Tuesday morning, increasing through the day.
While there is some uncertainty on exact wind speeds, widespread
gusts 30 to 35 knots are likely Tuesday morning through Wednesday
morning, along with steep and choppy seas. There is a 20-30% chance
for storm-force wind gusts Tuesday morning and afternoon. There is
also a slight chance of thunderstorms on Tuesday.

&&

.BEACHES...
High tides will continue to decrease through the weekend, with high
tide around 7 ft MLLW Saturday, falling below 7 ft on Sunday.
Forecast tides have been adjusted 1.01 to 1.07 feet above baseline
astronomical predictions based on trends in recent observations.

A long period west swell of 3 ft at 14-15 seconds continues to
generate breaking waves of 3-5 feet, with local sets up to 6 feet on
exposed west-facing beaches this afternoon. A reinforcing 4 ft swell
at 16-17 seconds will move into coastal waters late this evening and
overnight. This will increase breaking waves to 4-7 ft with sets up
to 8 feet on exposed west-facing beaches and near piers for
Saturday, highest in the morning and early afternoon.

The combination tides around 7 feet and elevated surf will result in
minor to locally moderate coastal flooding on Saturday morning. A
Beach Hazards Statement remains in effect through Saturday
afternoon.

&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Beach Hazards Statement through Saturday afternoon for Orange
     County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

PZ...None.

&&

$$

PUBLIC...SS
AVIATION/MARINE/BEACHES...Zuber
