Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
441 AM PDT Thu Oct 8 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Cooling will spread inland into the weekend with high temperatures
cooling to around average on Saturday. High temperatures for the
coast and valleys will be mostly in the 70s for Sunday through
Tuesday. One low pressure system from the southwest on Sunday and
a second from the north on Monday and Tuesday combined with
tropical moisture will bring showers for late Saturday night
through Sunday night with continuing chances for showers
through Tuesday night. There will also be stronger southwest to
west winds on Sunday.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

...UPDATED AVIATION DISCUSSION...

.SHORT TERM (Today through Saturday)...
High pressure aloft will weaken into the weekend. Onshore will
slowly strengthen and a shallow marine layer will slowly deepen
into the weekend. Cooling of high temperatures will continue to
spread inland. High temperatures for the deserts into the
mountains will be a few degrees warmer today. Cooling will
continue to spread inland today with high temperatures a few
degrees cooler for coastal areas to around 5 degrees cooler for
the western valleys in San Diego County. High temperatures on
Friday will cool a few degrees for the deserts and around 5
degrees elsewhere. On Saturday, high temperatures will cool a few
degrees near the coast to 8 to 12 degrees for the mountains,
deserts, and inland valleys.

High temperatures for today will be around 10 to 15 degrees above
average, ranging from the 80s near the coast to the upper 90s to
103 for the Inland Empire with 106 to 110 for the lower deserts.
HeatRisk for the coast and valleys will be Moderate to locally
Major and for the lower deserts Moderate to Major. On Saturday,
high temperatures will cool to around average with high
temperatures ranging from the 70s near the coast to the lower
to mid 80s for the valleys with the lower deserts in the mid 90s.

A shallow marine layer will slowly deepen with patchy low clouds
and locally dense fog developing across the coastal waters and
spreading into adjacent coastal areas early this morning.
Coverage of night and morning coastal low clouds and inland
spread will increase for tonight and again Friday night.

&&

.LONG TERM (Saturday night through Wednesday)...
A closed low pressure system moving southward off the California
coast will interact with a weakening tropical cyclone Rachel to
spread moisture and precipitation into northern Baja and portions
of southern California for Saturday night into Sunday.

NBM probabilities for 0.10 inch or more of rainfall for Saturday
night through Sunday night range from 65 to 75 percent near the
coast to 80 to 90 percent in the mountains with 50 to 70 percent
for the deserts. NBM probabilities for 0.50 inch or more of
rainfall for Saturday night through Sunday night range from 40 to
50 percent near the coast to 65 to 75 percent in the mountains
with 30 to 40 percent for the deserts. NBM probabilities for 1
inch or more of rainfall for Saturday night through Sunday night
range from 20 to 30 percent near the coast to 45 to 55 percent in
the mountains with 15 to 25 percent for the deserts. NBM
probabilities for 2 inches or more of rainfall for Saturday night
through Sunday night are 15 to 25 percent for the mountains.

A second low pressure system moving southward along the California
coast could bring additional showers for Monday and Tuesday. For
Monday through Tuesday night, NBM probabilities for another
0.10 inch or more of rainfall range from 45 to 55 percent near
the coast to 55 to 65 percent for the mountains with 15 to 35
percent for the deserts. For Monday through Tuesday night, NBM
probabilities for another 0.50 inch or more of rainfall range
from 20 to 30 percent near the coast to 35 to 45 percent for the
mountains with less than 15 percent for the deserts. For Monday
through Tuesday night, NBM probabilities for another 1 inch or
more of rainfall range from around 10 percent near the coast to
15 to 25 percent for the mountains.

NHC has Rachel falling below tropical storm strength before
landfall in northern Baja late Saturday night on a track that
would take it into northwest Arizona late Sunday evening. Slight
deviations from the NHC forecast are unlikely to cause any
significant changes to the impacts to southwestern California for
Saturday night through Sunday night.

Winds across the coastal waters are expected to shift to the west
by Sunday afternoon with gusts to 20 to 30 mph. The tide gauge in
the San Diego Bay shows water levels around 18 inches higher than
those of a year ago, that higher current sea level the result of
the thermal expansion of warmer water from a marine heat wave and
the strengthening El Nino and the associated most recent Kelvin
wave moving northward along the California coast. Those higher
water levels combined with south to southwest swell and resulting
surf from Rachel and surge from the winds across the coastal
waters will increase the potential for coastal flooding,
especially on Sunday.

Drying and warming is expected for next Wednesday with northwest
flow aloft and weakening onshore lower-level flow.

&&

.AVIATION...
081130Z...Coast: Areas of low clouds and fog with bases around
400 feet MSL and vis 0-6SM will continue through 15-16Z. Low
clouds and fog based around 600-800 feet MSL with vis as low as
2SM to redevelop after 07Z tonight and expand in coverage into
Friday.

Otherwise...mostly clear today and tonight, except for shallow
cumulus over mountains 19-01Z.

&&

.MARINE...
No hazardous marine conditions are expected through Saturday. The
remnants of Rachel will bring a rapid increase in southerly swell
along with gusty winds and choppy seas Sunday, diminishing somewhat
on Monday.

&&

.BEACHES...
Surf of 3-5 feet with sets to 6 feet is expected on south and
southwest facing beaches through Friday. Elevated surf and strong
rip and longshore currents will create hazardous swimming
conditions. This elevated surf will combine with high tides during
the mornings and evenings to generate local coastal flooding and
beach erosion. Another south swell Sunday and continued morning high
tides near 7 feet will maintain a threat of coastal flooding and
beach erosion into Tuesday. The Beach Hazards Statement and Coastal
Flood Advisory contain the details.
