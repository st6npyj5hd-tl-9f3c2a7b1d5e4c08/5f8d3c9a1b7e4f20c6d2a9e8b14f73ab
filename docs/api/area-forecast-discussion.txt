Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
423 PM PST Tue Nov 17 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Below average temperatures this week with rain, isolated
thunderstorms, mountain snow, and wind late Wednesday night into
Thursday. A few scattered showers may linger into Friday but much
of Friday and Saturday are expected to be dry. Sunday night into
Monday continue to see slight chances of precipitation.
Additionally, unsettled weather will lead to periods of dangerous
boating conditions, high surf, and periods of coastal flooding.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...


...UPDATED AVIATION DISCUSSION...

Mostly dry conditions can be expected today, with areas of clouds
banked up against area mountains. A swath of fairly widespread rain
moved through Southern California yesterday evening and overnight,
bringing wetting rain to much of the area west of the mountains. A
rainfall summary has been headlined on our weather.gov/sandiego
website for more information on which areas saw the highest
rainfall. Dry conditions will continue for much of Wednesday, with
temperatures near average along the coast and below average further
inland. Near to below average temperatures will continue into early
next week.

Tonight an upper level closed low will begin to dig south along the
US West Coast, before moving inland near central California
Thursday. The core of the trough is expected to stay well north of
us, but the trough axis is expected to dig far enough south to
bring periods of moderate to heavy rain, increased winds, and
mountain snow to portions of Southern California. A cold front is
expected to move from northwest to southeast across the area
starting Thursday morning. The front looks most robust as it
enters Orange and San Bernardino Counties and looks to weaken as
it moves south towards Riverside and San Diego Counties late
Thursday morning or early afternoon. With the frontal passage,
expected heavy rain and gusty winds. This front will move fairly
quickly which allow for areas of urban/street flooding or ponding
of water on roads. Peak rainfall rates with the frontal passage
are expected to be 0.50-0.65 inches per hour north of San Diego
County and in area mountains and 0.25- 0.50 inches per hour in San
Diego County with the higher rates more likely in North San Diego County.
Additionally, winds gusting 30-35 mph are possible for the coast
and valleys as the front passes. Be sure to secure outdoor
property that could be easily blown away and remain aware of
falling tree limbs and debris. There is a chance that
thunderstorms may develop within the front at times.

Once the front passes, scattered light to locally moderate showers
remain possible through Thursday for areas in and west of the
mountains. Overall, rainfall totals of 1-2 inches are possible in
the San Bernardino mountains, 0.50-1 inch in Orange County and
portions of the Inland Empire, 0.25-0.50 inch in San Diego County,
0.50-1.50 inches in the San Diego and Riverside mountains, 0.10-0.30
inches in the High Desert, and 0.10 or less in the lower deserts. On
Friday, a shortwave moves across the northern portion of our
forecast area which has the potential to spark additional rounds of
light precipitation although global models have yet to agree on the
timing. The ECMWF has the shortwave passing earlier in the day
Friday, with the GFS showing a later passage of the shortwave.

Due to the core of the low staying well to our north, snow levels
are expected to mainly remain above 7000 ft during the most
widespread precipitation. As precipitation tapers off Thursday
afternoon/evening, snow levels are expected to drop to 6000-6500 ft.
Overall, snowfall totals of 3-5 inches are possible above 7500 ft
with totals of 1-2 inches below 7500 ft.

In addition to gusty winds with the frontal passage for the coast
and valleys, winds will increase over the mountains and deserts late
Wednesday/early Thursday. The winds look like they will be strongest
in the San Bernardino mountains and High Desert. Peak gusts of 50-60
mph are possible over the mountain ridges onto the deserts slopes,
with gusts of 45-55 mph into the High Desert. In the Riverside and
San Diego County mountains and adjacent deserts, peak gusts of 40-55
mph can be expected, strongest on mountain ridges, mountain
slopes, and through passes. Winds will taper off into Thursday
evening.

Dry conditions are expected on Saturday, with potential for another
round of unsettled weather sometime Sunday or Monday. There is still
uncertainty surrounding the details of this system, mainly in
regards to timing and how far south the low will dig. Global
ensembles are indicating at least light precipitation west of the
mountains but more details will be available in the coming days.

&&

.AVIATION...
180030Z...Coast/Valleys...SCT clouds will regather later this
evening along the coastal areas with bases around 3000-4000 ft MSL,
with low confidence in becoming BKN (20-30%) intermittently (mainly
from 10-15z Wed) along the coastal areas. Otherwise, SCT-BKN high
clouds will build into the region Wednesday morning.

Mountains/Deserts...VFR conditions will prevail with clouds 4000-
6000 ft along coastal slopes scattering early this evening. Westerly
winds with gusts 25-35 kts along desert slopes and through passes
will diminish in the next few hours. Otherwise, SCT-BKN high clouds
will build into the region Wednesday morning.

&&

.MARINE...
Elevated winds with 20-25 knot gusts expected through early
Wednesday in addition to choppy seas of 4-7 feet with period of 7-8
seconds. The exception will be south of San Clemente Island where
steep. high seas of 7-10 feet will continue through Wednesday
morning. A Small Craft Advisory remains in effect through 4 AM
Wednesday. Winds and seas will continue to diminish, thereafter.

Conditions look to deteriorate once again Thursday as the next storm
system approaches. Steep, high seas of 7-10 ft along with wind gusts
peaking 20-30 knots for Thursday into Friday morning. Wind direction
initially south-southwest on Thursday will turn westerly by late
afternoon.

&&

.BEACHES...
A series of a mixture of long-period (3-5 feet at 14-16 seconds) and
short period (6-8 feet at 7-8 seconds) swells, both from the west-
northwest (280-290 degrees), will continue to generate periods of
elevated to high surf through much of this week. A High Surf
Advisory remains in effect through 4 AM Wednesday.

The shorter period northwest swell will wane through this evening,
leaving a 3-5 ft swell with a 13-14 second period for Wednesday.
This will result in a brief decrease in surf, down to 3-5 feet.
However, northwesterly swell builds again Thursday, elevating surf
once again late Thursday into early Friday.

Coastal flooding is expected at times through the week. High surf
will contribute to increased coastal flood chances even though tides
will be under 6.5 ft through Friday. King Tides are expected to peak
next week, but chances of tides exceeding 7 ft start as early as
Saturday Nov 21.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...High Surf Advisory until 4 AM PST Wednesday for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Wind Advisory from 11 PM Wednesday to 4 PM PST Thursday for
     Apple and Lucerne Valleys-San Bernardino County Mountains.

PZ...Small Craft Advisory until 4 AM PST Wednesday for Coastal Waters
     from San Mateo Point to the Mexican Border and out to 10 nm-
     Waters from San Mateo Point to the Mexican Border Extending
     10 to 60 nm out including San Clemente Island.


&&
