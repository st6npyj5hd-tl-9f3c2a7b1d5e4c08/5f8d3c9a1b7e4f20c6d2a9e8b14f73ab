Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
936 PM PDT Fri Oct 9 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Tropical Storm Rachel will approach the region Saturday evening into
Sunday. Cooler weather will continue to spread inland this weekend
into early next week as a stronger trough moves in from the north.
These systems will bring showers and locally heavy rainfall, gusty
winds, and coastal impacts to the region. Quieter and warmer weather
will begin to move into the region for the latter half of next week.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

...UPDATED AVIATION DISCUSSION...

Mostly clear skies were seen over SoCal with cumulus clouds across
the mountain areas. A trough is beginning to gain strength over the
Pacific NW as Tropical Storm Rachel churns 800 miles to the SW of
San Diego. The deepening troughing pattern over the West Coast will
bring in more cooler air to the region by Saturday with a few
degrees cooler than today near the coast, closer to 10 degrees
cooler for all other areas. Widespread 70s and 80s will be seen for
areas below 6,000 feet with 90s in the lower deserts. The marine
layer will also build, bringing low clouds and less fog to coastal
areas.

Tropical Storm Rachel is expected to make landfall somewhere near
the US/Mexico Border late Saturday night. While there remains some
uncertainty in the track, tropical storm level impacts have the
potential to impact San Diego County. A Tropical Storm Watch is in
effect for all of San Diego County, including coastal flooding,
wind, and periods of heavy rain Saturday night into Sunday. There is
expected to be a gradient in rainfall amounts with the higher
amounts on the southeastern side of the storm. There still remains
some uncertainty in the track, but a continued northern shift would
increase potential for heavy rain across the county with a southern
shift making it more localized. Areas of flash and urban flooding
are possible, especially where the maximum rainfall rates occur some
mountain areas 0.50-1.00 inch. The San Diego River at Fashion Valley
is currently forecast to peak at 7.4 feet (which is just below
monitor stage) Sunday afternoon.

Increased winds are expected with the onset and passage of Tropical
Storm Rachel. Sustained winds of 39+ mph are expected on the
southeast side of the storm. If the track continues to move further
north, that would put more of San Diego county in a position to see
winds of that magnitude. Based on the current track, more southern
parts of the county are most likely to see winds of this magnitude.
Regardless, strong sustained winds and wind gusts can be expected on
Sunday. Winds may be enhanced by local terrain with gusts 40 to 55
mph on the desert slopes of the mountains and into the deserts.
Local gusts up to 60 mph possible on mountain ridges.

Temperatures will continue to tumble on Sunday, dropping another 10
degrees from those of Saturday. Models continue to differ on the
exact path of where the trough to the north goes by early next week.
As of now, the coolest day of the week will be Monday with highs
struggling to reach 70 west of the mountains with 50s and 60s for
places below 6,000 feet. Colder temperatures will also be expected
for the Big Bear region with highs in the 40s and lows in the 20s
and 30s. Depending on the exact track of the low, chances for
showers continue into Monday, possibly Tuesday. Models are in fair
agreement on trough beginning to lift out of the area by Wednesday
as high pressure off the coast gains strength. This will lead to a
warmer and drier weather trend for the latter half of next week.

&&

.AVIATION...
100000Z...Coast...Patchy low clouds along the immediate San Diego
County coast will remain patchy and intermittent overnight. Highest
chances for low cloud cigs are for southwest SD Co including vcnty
KSAN. Bases are expected to be 1000-1500 ft MSL. Low clouds likely
clear between 13-15Z Saturday. Low clouds are unlikely to make an
appearance Saturday evening as they will be impeded from forming by
thick high level cloud cover.

Otherwise...Mostly clear with unrestricted vis through this evening.
BKN high clouds AOA 20k ft moving into the region tonight into
Saturday.

&&

.MARINE...
No hazardous marine conditions are expected through Saturday. Gusty
northerly winds develop early Sunday morning, turning westerly late
morning into the afternoon. Winds 20 to 30 knots expected throughout
the coastal waters Sunday. Large fresh swell from the northwest will
combine with south swell associated with Tropical Storm Rachel and
wind waves to create combined seas 6 to 10 feet Sunday. Choppy,
steep, mixed seas and gusty winds will produce conditions hazardous
to small craft on Sunday. Winds and seas decrease for Monday, though
gusts will locally approach 20 knots Monday afternoon.

&&

.BEACHES...
Surf of 3-5 feet with local sets to 6 feet will continue on south
and southwest facing beaches through Saturday. Elevated surf and
strong rip and longshore currents will create hazardous swimming
conditions. This elevated surf will combine with high tides near 7
feet during the mornings and evenings to produce significant coastal
flooding and beach erosion.

Fresh south swell associated with Tropical Storm Rachel will arrive
late Saturday into Sunday. This will combine with a larger northwest
swell and strong winds to produce elevated to high surf Sunday for
all area beaches, peaking at 5 to 8 feet with sets to 9 feet. This
will pose a hazard to swimmers and lead to very strong rip currents.
In addition, morning high tides will continue to be 7 to locally 7.5
feet, continuing the threat for significant coastal flooding through
Sunday afternoon. A Coastal Flood Warning is in effect for these
hazards through Sunday at 3 PM. Take the necessary actions to
protect flood-prone property.

Both winds and swells subside for Monday-Tuesday, and surf will
decrease to 3 to 6 feet, though strong rip currents will continue.
Morning high tides will continue to be near 7 feet, meaning that
minor coastal flooding will continue to be an issue through this
period. The Coastal Flood Warning will be replaced with a Coastal
Flood Advisory at 3 PM Sunday and continue through 2 PM Tuesday.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is requested for early Sunday morning through
Sunday night. Weather spotters are encouraged to report
significant weather conditions.

&&

     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Tropical Storm Watch for San Diego County Coastal Areas-San
     Diego County Deserts-San Diego County Mountains-San Diego
     County Valleys.

     Flood Watch from Saturday evening through Sunday evening for
     Apple and Lucerne Valleys-Coachella Valley-Riverside County
     Mountains-San Bernardino County Mountains-San Diego County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Deserts-San Diego County
     Mountains-San Diego County Valleys-San Gorgonio Pass near
     Banning.

     Beach Hazards Statement from Sunday morning through Sunday
     evening for Orange County Coastal Areas-San Diego County
     Coastal Areas.

     Coastal Flood Advisory from 3 PM Sunday to 2 PM PDT Tuesday for
     Orange County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

PZ...Tropical Storm Watch for Coastal Waters from San Mateo Point to
