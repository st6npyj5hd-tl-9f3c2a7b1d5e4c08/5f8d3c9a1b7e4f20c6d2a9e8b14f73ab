Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
202 AM PDT Thu Jul 30 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
High pressure will strengthen and move westward across the area
through Saturday with high temperatures warming to 8 to 12 degrees
above average for inland areas for the weekend with major to
extreme HeatRisk for the deserts by Sunday. High temperatures will
then cool around 5 degrees for inland areas through Wednesday with
little change for low temperatures. Higher humidity will continue
for coastal areas with increasing humidity for inland areas next
week. Night and morning low clouds will continue for coastal
areas.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

.SHORT TERM (Today through Saturday)...
High pressure centered over New Mexico will move westward and
strengthen through Saturday and be centered in the vicinity of Las
Vegas on Saturday. High temperatures will warm a few degrees
each day for today through Saturday, warming around 5 to locally
10 degrees for inland areas from Wednesday through Saturday.

High temperatures on Saturday will range from around 5 degrees
above average near the coast to 8 to 12 degrees above average for
inland areas. High temperatures on Saturday will range from around
80 near the coast to the upper 90s to 108 for the Inland Empire
with 114 to 118 for the lower deserts. For Saturday, there will be
major to locally extreme HeatRisk for the deserts, San Diego
County mountains, moderate to major HeatRisk for the Inland
Empire, and moderate or greater HeatRisk for most areas except at
the beaches and for mountain elevations above 7500 feet.

The marine layer is around 1500 to 2000 feet deep. Coastal low
clouds will spread into the western valleys early this morning.
The marine mayer will slowly decrease in depth into the weekend
with night and morning low clouds spreading inland mainly into
just coastal areas and not into portions of the valleys.

&&

.LONG TERM (Sunday through Wednesday)...
High pressure will slowly weaken into the middle of next week. The
axis of the high pressure will move northward just enough for the
mid level flow across the area to become more easterly by the
middle of next week. High temperatures for inland areas will
decrease around 5 degrees from Saturday through Wednesday while
surface dewpoints for inland areas will slowly increase with
humidity slowing increasing.

Warm sea surface temperatures in the mid 70s, around 5 degrees
above average, will maintain higher humidity for coastal areas
into the western valleys. Low temperatures will change little,
ranging from a few degrees above average for the coast into the
western valleys with the mountains and deserts 5 to 10 degrees
above average.

High temperatures for next Wednesday will range from around 80
near the coast to the mid 90s to 105 for the Inland Empire with
110 to 114 for the lower deserts. Low temperatures for the coast
and valleys will be mostly in the mid 60s to lower 70s, in the
70s for the Apple and Lucerne Valleys, and in the mid 80s to lower
90s for the lower deserts.

HeatRisk will slowly decrease with HeatRisk for Wednesday of next
week moderate to major for the deserts, and mostly moderate for
the inland valleys, higher coastal terrain, and mountain areas
below 7000 feet.

&&

.AVIATION...
300600Z....Coast/Valleys...Low clouds based 1000-1400ft MSL have
already pushed inland around 10-15 miles and will filter in up to 20
miles by 12-14z. Intermittent IFR CIGs possible at coastal TAF
sites, best chances between 10-14z. 70% chance for at least a brief
CIG at KONT and a 20% chance for SBD, although both sites should at
least see some reduced VIS due to HZ. Coastal terrain and western
valleys, VIS down to 3-6SM possible. Clouds scatter out of the
Inland Empire around 15-16z, clearing to the coast by 17-18z.
Stratus may linger at some beaches in San Diego County through the
afternoon. Low clouds based around 800-1100ft MSL develop and move
inland again after 01z Fri.

Mountains/Deserts...Mostly SKC with unrestricted VIS through
Thursday evening.

&&

.MARINE...
No hazardous marine weather is expected through Monday.

&&

.BEACHES...
A 4-5 ft swell from 160-180 degrees with a period of 13-15 seconds
associated with Hurricane Genevieve is currently moving into the
coastal waters. Surf of 5-9 ft is expected on south facing beaches
in Orange County with surf around 3-6 ft elsewhere. Surf will peak
on Thursday and Friday, when the swell direction shifts to 180-190
degrees. Strong rip and longshore currents will create dangerous
swimming conditions at most area beaches. In addition to the high
surf, evening high tides will be above 6 ft this week. The
combination of high tides and surf increases the risk of coastal
flooding. The swell, period, and tides are expected to decrease over
the weekend. Further information on all these hazards is found in
the Coastal Hazard Message.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.

&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Coastal Flood Advisory until 3 AM PDT Saturday for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     High Surf Advisory until 5 PM PDT Saturday for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Heat Advisory from 10 AM Friday to 8 PM PDT Sunday for Orange
     County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Extreme Heat Warning from 10 AM Thursday to 8 PM PDT Sunday for
     Apple and Lucerne Valleys-Coachella Valley-Riverside County
     Mountains-San Bernardino and Riverside County Valleys-The
     Inland Empire-San Diego County Deserts-San Diego County
     Mountains-San Gorgonio Pass near Banning.

     Extreme Heat Warning from 10 AM Friday to 8 PM PDT Sunday for
     Orange County Inland Areas-San Diego County Valleys-Santa
     Ana Mountains and Foothills.

     Heat Advisory from 10 AM Thursday to 8 PM PDT Sunday for San
     Bernardino County Mountains.

PZ...None.

&&

$$

AVIATION/MARINE/BEACHES...Zuber
