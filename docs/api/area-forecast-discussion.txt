Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
1028 PM PDT Wed Aug 12 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
The flow aloft will become gradually southwesterly with drying of
monsoonal moisture the next few days. There remains a slight
chance of showers and thunderstorms for the mountains and deserts
from late this morning through this afternoon. In addition to
drying aloft, there will also be gradual drying of lower level
moisture through early next week. High temperatures will cool
through Thursday, then slowly warm into the middle of next week.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

...UPDATED AVIATION DISCUSSION...

This afternoon through Thursday...

The latest high res models are alluding to more of a drying trend vs
previous model runs, and given the mid-level flow becoming more
southwesterly, there will likely only be some isolated thunderstorms
developing later this afternoon along the mountain/desert interface,
and then these will move off towards the northeast and skirt across
the deserts by later in the afternoon/evening. With much more
troughing upstream influencing the region, temperatures will begin a
notable cooling trend from this afternoon into tomorrow. MUCAPE
through a consensus of guidance appears to be roughly around 1000
J/kg over the San Diego mountains by later this afternoon, but the
majority of the instability (most unstable CAPE) lies just to the
east of our CWA. Given the parameters, and flow in the mid level, it
is going to be hard for storms to get going. Though, through
orographic lifting, along with some shear, there could be a few
storms, with the most likely location being over the San Bernardino
mountains on the eastern slopes, and over the San Jacinto area.
There may also be a storm popping off over southern San Diego County
in the Jacumba area. Otherwise, a transition towards cooler and
drier conditions are expected going into tomorrow. Winds will also
increase more during the day, with gusts approaching 60 mph within
Gorgonio Pass and also gusts of greater than 50 mph across the high
deserts. The marine layer should fill in better for tonight in
comparison to recent nights as onshore becomes more prevalent, and
this will carry into tomorrow morning with increasing ceilings,
extending further east into the inland areas. As the trough
continues to deepen and propagate closer to the region, it will also
allow for a "baroclinic leaf" to develop and provide and extensive
mid to high level cloud deck tomorrow, which will also hinder
daytime heating, with values becoming around 5 to 10 degrees below
the seasonal average for this time of year. The drier air aloft will
cut off chances of convection for tomorrow.

Friday through the middle of next week...

Persistent onshore flow will prevail into the early part of the
upcoming weekend and then the ridge to the east will begin to expand
back towards the west with increasing heights, and thus a gradual
warmup back to around seasonal temperatures for this time of year,
and even a little above average temperatures will return going into
the early half of next week as heights creep back above 595 dm. Due
to this, there may need to be heat products issued for the deserts
again by Monday, as temperatures look to soar north of 110F for
much of the lower deserts and within the Coachella Valley. As this
occurs, there will be some recycled monsoonal moisture that may
make its way back into southern California, and may give our
mountains and deserts a slight chance of afternoon thunderstorms
towards the middle of next week, otherwise remaining dry elsewhere
through then.

&&

.AVIATION...
130515Z....Coast/Valleys...Low clouds with bases 2000-2500 ft MSL
and tops to 3000 ft MSL developing over the San Diego County valleys
and coastal Orange County will spread into the inland valleys by 10-
12Z. Local vis 3-5 SM where clouds near terrain in the valleys
overnight. Clouds clearing inland 15-17Z and closer to the coast 17-
19Z. There is a 60-80% chance of CIGS persisting at the beaches and
up to around 10 mi inland in San Diego County through the afternoon.
Low clouds will begin to push further inland beginning around
14/00Z, filling in over the coastal basin again Thu night. Otherwise
SCT-BKN clouds at/above 20,000 ft MSL through Thu evening.

Mountains/Deserts...SCT-BKN clouds at/above 20,000 ft MSL and
unrestricted vis through Thursday evening.

&&

.MARINE...
No hazardous marine conditions are expected through Monday.

&&

.BEACHES...
High tides of 7 feet occurred earlier this evening. High tides will
be lower on Thursday, minimizing the threat of any coastal flooding.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Coastal Flood Advisory until 11 PM PDT this evening for Orange
     County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

PZ...None.

&&

$$

PUBLIC...Stewey
