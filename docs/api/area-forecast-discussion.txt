Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
209 AM PDT Fri Sep 25 2026

.SYNOPSIS...

Warmer weather moves into the region through Saturday with low
clouds and patchy fog closer to the coast each morning. Tropical
mid and high level moisture will bring more clouds and cooler
temperature, along with a slight chance of showers for much of
our area by Sunday and Monday. Weak high pressure moves into the
area by next Tuesday, bringing warming and drying.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

Low clouds are still struggling to form early this morning over
the coastal waters. The marine layer will remain thin over the
next couple of days, bringing low clouds and patchy fog to areas
within around 10 miles of the coast each late night and morning.
One of the reasons for this will be high pressure aloft over
Mexico beginning to expand further into our region during this
time, bringing slightly warmer weather. With highs in the 90s for
the lower deserts yesterday, there is a very high chance (85+ %)
to see highs eclipse 100 degrees today and Saturday. Valleys west
of the mountains will see the largest warm up of around 5 degrees
with highs in the 90s from Escondido to Temecula to Ontario.

Model trends beginning to come together on a deep trough dropping
in from the north by Sunday and becoming a closed low by Monday.
As it does so, Hurricane Polo moves north on Sunday and
northeastward on Monday, where it is expected to make landfall
somewhere in southern Baja California later on Monday. The
troughing pattern will act as a mechanism to help pull in some of
this tropical moisture, mainly in the form of clouds and humidity.

The key will be where exactly our low pressure system sets up.
Models continue to push the system slightly further to the south,
which would not entrain as much moisture. Two things look more
certain however: increased humidity (though high temperatures
will not be as hot as today and Saturday), and increased swell and
coastal flooding/erosion. Warmer nights can be expected near the
coast with some places staying above 70 degrees. Monday will be
the coolest day overall with highs in the 70s and 80s from the
coastal to the foothills as well as the high desert. NBM gives
Palm Springs only a 50-50 chance to reach over 90 degrees on
Monday as well.

Though moisture increases are seen with PWAT values exceeding one
inch, the low`s track will be important on determining any
accumulating rainfall for area. Moisture in the form of high
clouds may spread over SoCal during this time, but lack of forcing
mechanisms may only provide us cloudier, humid days. These clouds
may also inhibit max temperatures as well.

The area of low pressure should begin to move out of the region by
Tuesday. Depending on its location, offshore flow may accompany
this exit. The strength is still uncertain as well as how hot we
become by Tuesday and Wednesday. This is seen in quite the large
spread in temperature guidance. Take the Miramar Base in San
Diego: near a 15 degree spread in high temperatures from
25th-75th percentiles with highs in the upper 70s to lower 90s.
With these factors, confidence is high that there will be less
humidity and warmer temperatures, just not sure how warm. Weak
high pressure looks to stick around into at least the middle of
next week, keeping temperatures slightly above normal west of the
mountains and closer to normal for the mountains and deserts.

&&

.AVIATION...
250530Z...Coast/Valleys...Patchy and intermittent low clouds
based at 800-1200 feet MSL will eventually congeal overnight with
widespread uniform coverage. Local vis on mesas or inland reduced
3-5SM. Scatter out by 16-18Z Friday. Patchy low clouds to
redevelop after 03Z Friday eve, based at around 600-1000 feet MSL.

Mountains...Mostly clear skies through Friday morning. Cumulus and
local CBs to redevelop and lead to isolated SHRA/TSRA 19-01Z Friday.

Deserts...Mostly clear skies into Friday, with increasing clouds
above 10 kft after 19Z Friday.

&&

.MARINE...
No hazardous marine conditions are expected through Tuesday.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.

&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Beach Hazards Statement from 5 AM PDT early this morning through
     Sunday afternoon for Orange County Coastal Areas-San Diego
     County Coastal Areas.

PZ...None.

&&
