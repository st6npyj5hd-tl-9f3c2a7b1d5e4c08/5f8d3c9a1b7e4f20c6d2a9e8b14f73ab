Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
323 PM PST Mon Nov 16 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Below average temperatures with periods of rain, isolated
thunderstorms, mountain snow, and wind this week. The first round of
unsettled weather is today into Tuesday morning. Dry conditions
expected for much of the day Tuesday into Wednesday, with additional
rain, snow, and wind late Wednesday night into Thursday. Friday and
Saturday are expected to be mostly dry, with additional chances of
precipitation on Sunday or Monday. Additionally, unsettled weather
will lead to periods of dangerous boating conditions, high surf, and
periods of coastal flooding.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...


...UPDATED AVIATION DISCUSSION...

Periods of showers lingering offshore will continue to slowly move
inland through the early afternoon, with increased coverage of
scattered showers later this afternoon into tonight. Light to
moderate rain is expected with rainfall rates peaking at 0.30-0.40
inches per hour. Overall, rainfall totals will be 0.05-0.20 inches
for Orange County and the Inland Empire; 0.10-0.50 inches for San
Diego coast and valleys; 0.50-1 inch for the mountains; less than
0.20 inch in the High Desert; and little to no rain is expected in
the lower deserts. Additionally, there is a chance of thunderstorms
(10%) over the coastal waters and locally along the coast this
afternoon through 10 PM. Where thunderstorms develop, expected
erratic, gusty winds and localized flooding. Showers will taper off
from northwest to southeast Tuesday morning. Snow levels are
expected to stay above 8000 ft during much of the precipitation
leading to 2-4 inches of snow above 8000 ft. Snow levels will drop
to 5500-6000 ft as precipitation tapers off, with only a dusting
possible below 8000 ft. Winds will increase over the mountains and
into the deserts this evening into Tuesday morning. Peak gusts of 40
to 50 mph expected.

Mostly dry conditions expected Tuesday and Wednesday, with below
average temperatures. High temperatures will range from 2-12 degrees
below seasonal averages. Wednesday will be a few degrees warmer than
Tuesday, but the "warming trend" will be brief with Thursday
expected to see widespread cooler conditions. For Thursday through
Saturday highs will be 7-12 degrees below average for inland
locations.

In addition to cooler conditions on Thursday, another round of
precipitation and gusty winds can be expected. The system arriving
late Wednesday night/early Thursday will be driven by a stronger jet
and will have enough IVT associated with it to be considered a weak
Atmospheric River. While there still remains some uncertainty in the
system`s track, we are expecting more rain, stronger winds, and snow
at lower elevations compared to tonight/Tuesday`s system. NBM
chances of exceeding 1 inch of rain from early Thursday to early
Friday are 70-90 percent for areas north of San Diego County
(highest chances in the San Bernardino mountains), 20-50 percent for
San Diego County, and less than 15 percent for the deserts. The
influence from the jet stream will help increase instability,
allowing for chances of isolated thunderstorms across the coastal
basin Thursday afternoon. This is expected to be a colder system
with snow levels expected to start out around 7500 ft Wednesday
night and fall to 6500 ft by Thursday evening. Some accumulating
snow looks possible around 6500 ft, with the majority of the
accumulations above 7000 ft. The last hazard to mention for the
Wednesday night into Thursday time frame is the increase in westerly
winds over the mountains and deserts. First look at the wind
forecast indicates peak gusts 40 to 55 mph, locally up to 60 mph
through wind prone passes and canyons. It looks like precipitation
and gusty winds will taper off Thursday into Friday morning with
dry conditions expected Friday afternoon through Saturday.

Another system appears on the horizon, arriving Sunday and/or
Monday. This one looks weaker, so not as wet or windy as the
Thursday system. Its projected location, timing, and character is
all over the place among ensemble solutions. The most certain thing
we can say is that our weather will remain unsettled and cool.

&&

.AVIATION...
172330Z...Coast/Valleys...ISO-SCT -SHRA will continue through this
afternoon and evening with MVFR conditions expected with any passing
-SHRA. Greatest coverage of -SHRA in San Diego County. A few
embedded +SHRA will introduce a low chance of brief IFR conditions,
particularly at coastal TAF sites. Slight chance (10%) of a TSRA
near the coast. Precipitation largely moves out of the coastal basin
and into the mountains by 09z Tuesday with low to mid level clouds
clearing through Tuesday morning. Westerly winds increasing along
the coast after 04z with wind gusts 20-25 kts at times. Winds
decrease again from north to south 12-18z, but intermittent gusts to
20 knots continue through the afternoon for San Diego County coastal
areas.

Mountains/Deserts...VFR conditions will become MVFR intermittently
for the mountains this afternoon and evening as SCT -SHRA moves
through the region. Higher terrain occasionally shrouded in FG.
-SHRA eventually tapers off by 09-11z Tuesday. Clouds scattering
Tuesday morning. S/W winds increasing across the desert slopes after
03Z Tue with gusts 30-45 kts at times.

&&

.MARINE...
Pockets of heavier showers expected along with a slight chance (10%)
of embedded thunderstorms through 10 PM this evening.

Winds and seas will ramp back up through this afternoon and evening
with 25-30 knot gusts expected in addition to steep, high seas of 7-
10 feet with a period of 8-15 seconds. Wind gusts will be strongest
near San Clemente Island, where near-gale conditions are possible. A
Small Craft Advisory remains in effect through 4 AM Wednesday due to
the hazardous conditions expected from the gusty winds and high
seas. Winds and seas will slowly diminish Wednesday morning.

Conditions look to deteriorate once again Thursday as the next storm
system approaches. Steep, high seas of 7-10 ft along with wind gusts
peaking 20-30 knots for Thursday into Friday morning.

&&

.BEACHES...
A series of a mixture of long-period (3-5 feet at 15-17 seconds) and
short period (6-8 feet at 7-8 seconds) swells, both from the west-
northwest (280-290 degrees), will generate periods of elevated and
high surf through much of this week. The next surge of northwesterly
swells arriving now will elevate surf to 6 to 9 feet, with sets to
11 feet along west-facing beaches. This maintains the threat for
high surf and strong rip currents through mid-week. A High Surf
Advisory remains in effect through 4 AM Wednesday.

The shorter period northwest swell will wane Tuesday afternoon,
leaving a 3-5 ft swell with a 13-14 second period for Wednesday.
This will result in a brief decrease in surf, down to 3-5 feet with
sets to 6 feet. However, northwesterly swell builds again Thursday,
elevating surf once again through Friday.

Coastal flooding is expected at times through the week. High surf
will contribute to increased coastal flood chances even though tides
will be under 6.5 ft through Friday. King Tides are expected to peak
next week, but chances of tides exceeding 7 ft start as early as
Saturday Nov 21.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...High Surf Advisory until 4 AM PST Wednesday for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

PZ...Small Craft Advisory until 4 AM PST Wednesday for Coastal Waters
     from San Mateo Point to the Mexican Border and out to 10 nm-
     Waters from San Mateo Point to the Mexican Border Extending
     10 to 60 nm out including San Clemente Island.
