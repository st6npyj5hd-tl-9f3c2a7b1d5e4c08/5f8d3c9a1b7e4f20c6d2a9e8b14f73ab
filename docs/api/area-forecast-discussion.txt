Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
455 PM PDT Mon Jul 27 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
A slight cooling trend begins today and continues through
Wednesday, bringing temperatures down to around 5 degrees above
average by midweek. Daytime temperatures rise again for Thursday
through next weekend and we could see the highest temperatures of
this summer so far by Saturday. Some monsoonal moisture aloft
later in the week into the upcoming weekend may also provide a
slight chance of afternoon showers and thunderstorms for our
mountains and deserts.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...


...UPDATED AVIATION DISCUSSION...

This afternoon...Temperatures range from a few degrees higher to a
few degrees lower than at his time yesterday. There was a weak
coastal eddy last night and low clouds from a shallow marine layer
are persisting at the beaches of San Diego County. High resolution
models indicate that a weak coastal eddy will likely return
tonight.

A shallow marine layer will persist through the forecast period
with night and morning low clouds mostly restricted to the coastal
areas and western portions of the San Diego County valleys.

The large-scale pattern will remain quite stable, with high
pressure centered over the southern Rockies/Desert Southwest and
low pressure centered over the Gulf of Alaska. Through Wednesday,
the high pressure over SoCal will weaken slightly in response to a
vigorous shortwave trough moving inland to our north. The
resulting height falls and weak cyclonic flow aloft will allow the
marine layer to deepen a little and the low clouds to spread a
little farther inland during the nights and mornings. Temperatures
will be generally lower across the region but will be more
noticeable nearer to the coast. By Wednesday, temperatures will
range from a few degrees below normal in some locations west of
the mtns to around 5 degrees above normal in the low deserts.

For Thursday through next weekend...The high pressure center
will shift to the southwest and build over the Desert Southwest.
This will bring significantly warmer conditions to SoCal, we are
likely to see the highest temperatures so far this summer. Models
indicate that Saturday will likely be the hottest day, with
daytime temperatures ranging from 5-10 degrees above normal in the
coastal areas to about 15 degrees above normal in parts of the
inland valleys and deserts. Temperatures will range from the high
70s to the mid 80s near the coast, the low 90s to low triple
digits in inland OC and SD County, low triple digits to about 110
in the IE, 80s to low 90 in the mtns, up to 113 in the high
deserts and up to around 120 in the low deserts. The building high
pressure will also make the marine layer shallower and restrict
the low clouds to the immediate coast during the nights and
mornings. The heat will likely abate slightly for next Monday as
the high pressure weakens. Confidence in the coming high
temperatures is relatively high due to the stable pattern and good
agreement among model solutions.

&&

.AVIATION...
280000Z....Coast/Valleys...Low clouds with bases 900-1300 ft MSL
currently along the San Diego County coast will spread into KCRQ and
KSAN through 03Z, then pushing 10-15 mi inland and spreading north
into Orange County by 12Z. Local vis 4-6 sm where stratus nears
terrain in the valleys. Clouds clearing to the coast 15-18Z,
remaining BKN-OVC at some of the beaches into the afternoon.

Mountains/Deserts...A few Cu with bases near 15,000 ft MSL
dissipating by sunset, then clear with unrestricted vis through
Tuesday afternoon.

&&

.MARINE...
No hazardous marine weather is expected through Saturday.

&&

.BEACHES...
A south swell of 3-4 ft feet at 17 seconds will produce surf of 3-5
feet with sets to 6 feet into Wednesday. A Beach Hazard Statement
remains in effect through 5 PM Wednesday for the elevated surf,
high rip current risk, and minor tidal overflow.

Wednesday afternoon, a south swell associated with Hurricane
Genevieve will arrive. Some uncertainty exists in the height of the
swell , but current forecast shows a swell (170-180 degrees) of 5 ft
with a period of 14-15 seconds late Wednesday into Friday, becoming
more SSW (190-200 degrees) on Saturday with the period lowering to
around 12 seconds. This may produce surf of 5-8 ft on the south
facing beaches north of Oceanside, surf around 3-5 ft elsewhere, and
strong rip and longshore currents at all beaches. A High Surf
Advisory remains in effect from 5 PM Wednesday through 5 PM
Saturday. In addition to the high surf, evening high tides will be
above 6 ft this week. A Coastal Flood Advisory remains in effect
from 5 PM Wednesday through 3 AM Saturday for the risk of coastal
flooding due to the high tides combined with high surf.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Beach Hazards Statement through Wednesday afternoon for Orange
     County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Heat Advisory until 8 PM PDT this evening for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-Orange County Inland Areas-Riverside County
     Mountains-San Bernardino County Mountains-San Bernardino
     and Riverside County Valleys-The Inland Empire-San Diego
     County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Mountains-San Diego
     County Valleys-Santa Ana Mountains and Foothills.

     Coastal Flood Advisory from 5 PM Wednesday to 3 AM PDT Saturday
     for Orange County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal
     Areas.

     High Surf Advisory from 5 PM Wednesday to 5 PM PDT Saturday for
     Orange County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Extreme Heat Warning until 8 PM PDT this evening for Apple and
     Lucerne Valleys-Coachella Valley-San Diego County Deserts-
     San Gorgonio Pass near Banning.

PZ...None.
