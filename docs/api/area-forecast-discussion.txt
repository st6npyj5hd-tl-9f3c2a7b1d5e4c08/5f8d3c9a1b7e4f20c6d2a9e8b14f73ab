Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
943 AM PST Fri Nov 27 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Coastal flooding is expected to continue today due to King
Tides and elevated to high surf. Warming will continue today
with high temperatures mostly in the 80s for the valleys and
inland coastal areas. A cooling trend will begin on Saturday
and continue into the middle of next week. There is a chance
of showers for late Sunday night and Monday. A strong Pacific
storm for Tuesday into Wednesday will bring heavy
precipitation, strong winds, and a chance of thunderstorms.
Precipitation will decrease for Wednesday night and Thursday.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

...UPDATED AVIATION, BEACHES, AND MARINE DISCUSSION...

.SHORT TERM (Today through Sunday)...
High pressure aloft will continue the warming trend today with high
temperatures 12 to 18 degrees above average from the coast to the
coastal slopes of the mountains. High temperatures near the coast
will be around 80 with high temperatures for the valleys and inland
coastal areas in the 80s. High temperatures in the lower deserts
will be in the 80s and for the mountains in the lower 60s to lower 80s.

High pressure aloft will weaken during the weekend and onshore
lower-level flow will return and strengthen. High temperatures will
cool during the weekend, but remain above average on Sunday. On Sunday,
high temperatures for the coast and valleys will be mostly in the 70s,
for the lower deserts around 80, and for the mountains in the lower 50s
to lower 70s.

High clouds for late Friday night into Saturday morning will decrease
for Saturday afternoon into Sunday.

&&

.LONG TERM (Sunday night through Thursday)...
Clouds will increase for Sunday night and Monday with a chance of showers
for late Sunday night and Monday. Through Monday afternoon, the chance
for 0.25 inch or more of rainfall remains below 25 percent.

Chances for rain will increase for Monday night as a strong Pacific storm
moves into southern California on Tuesday. It could bring heavy
precipitation, strong winds, high surf, and a chance of thunderstorms.

Through Wednesday night, the chance of 1 inch or more of rainfall
exceeds 90 percent from the coast to the mountains. For Orange and
southwestern San Bernardino Counties, the chance of 3 inches or more
of rainfall ranges from 35 to 45 percent near the coast to 60 to 70
percent in the mountains. For San Diego County, the chance for 3 inches
or more of rainfall ranges from 40 to 50 percent over far northern
portions of the county to 25 to 35 percent for far southern portions
of the county. Through Tuesday, snow levels will mostly remain above
8000 feet. Winds could strengthen to gale force over the coastal
waters on Tuesday.

Current model timing would bring the most significant impacts into
southwestern California around Tuesday morning. Precipitation
will continue through Tuesday night into Wednesday, then decrease
for Wednesday night and Thursday as the low pressure system moves
eastward and farther inland.

&&

.AVIATION...
271700Z...VFR prevailing with SKC-SCT250 through this evening.
Increasing amounts of high-level clouds this evening will likely
prevent any widespread low-level cloud cover for coastal regions,
but patchy, intermittently impactful cigs are possible tonight (40%
chance vcnty KSAN 06-12Z, 25% vcnty chance KSNA 03-14Z).

&&

.MARINE...
No hazardous marine conditions expected through Monday. Winds become
southwesterly early Tuesday morning, increasing through the day.
While there is some uncertainty when it comes to exact wind speeds,
widespread gusts 30 to 35 knots are likely Tuesday morning through
Wednesday morning, along with steep, choppy, high seas. There is a
40-50% chance for storm-force wind gusts Tuesday morning and
afternoon.

&&

.BEACHES...
High tide peaks this morning at 10 AM around 7.57-7.7 ft MLLW. High
tides will continue to decrease through the weekend, with high tide
around 7 ft MLLW Saturday, falling below 7 ft on Sunday. Forecast
tides have been adjusted 1.01 to 1.07 feet above baseline
astronomical predictions based on trends in recent observations.

A long period west swell of 3-4 ft at 14-15 seconds continues to
generate breaking waves of 3-5 feet, with local sets up to 6 feet on
exposed west-facing beaches today. The period increases to 16-17
seconds Saturday, increasing breaking waves to 4-7 ft with sets up
to 8 feet on exposed west-facing beaches and near piers for
Saturday, highest in the morning and early afternoon.

The combination tides above 7 feet and elevated surf will result in
moderate coastal flooding during the this morning high tide, with
minor to locally moderate coastal flooding on Saturday morning. A
Coastal Flood Warning remains in effect through early this
afternoon, with a Beach Hazards Statement in effect early this
afternoon into Saturday afternoon.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Coastal Flood Warning until 1 PM PST this afternoon for Orange
     County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Beach Hazards Statement from 1 PM PST this afternoon through
     Saturday afternoon for Orange County Coastal Areas-San
     Diego County Coastal Areas.

PZ...None.

&&

$$

PUBLIC...17
AVIATION/MARINE/BEACHES...CSP
