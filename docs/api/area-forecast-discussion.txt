AFDSGX

Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
1153 AM PST Sun Nov 15 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Below-normal temperatures prevailing this week with multiple
rounds of precipitation. Gusty winds, marine and beaches hazards,
and rain/snow showers with an isolated thunderstorm threat for
Monday into early Tuesday morning. A stronger system
Thursday/Friday could bring all of the above as well as heavier
rainfall and a localized flooding threat.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

Visible satellite shows clear skies for inland locations with the
exception of some lingering high cu over the desert mountain slopes.
Radar maintains light echos over the coastal waters, likely piddly
showers stuck along a stalled boundary over the abnormally warm sea
surface temperatures. These showers may hang just offshore through
Monday morning before being pushed eastward as a elongated upper
level trough approaches tomorrow, bringing spotty showers to much of
the region during the day on Monday. Low and mid-level moisture will
be a bit lackluster with this trough, but decent energy and the
ongoing instability associated with the warmer sea surface
temperatures could allow for isolated thunderstorms to develop on
the back end of an associated front as it pushes across Monday
evening. While the threat will be highest over the coastal waters,
guidance continues to spill higher CAPE values into the valleys and
even parts of the Inland Empire, where a few thunderstorms may hold
together into early Tuesday morning. The HRRR emphasizes Orange
County for thunderstorms on Tuesday, while the RRFS prioritizes San
Diego County. It`s worth mentioning that the HRRR has been resolving
this system better the past two days.

As the trough moves over CA on Tuesday, snow levels are expected to
fall from around 8500ft Monday afternoon to around 6000-6500ft.
While model guidance has trended slightly warmer with this upcoming
system, a light rain/snow mix may be observed down to 6000ft Tuesday
morning as the last of the precipitation comes to an end. With the
warmer trend, snow accumulation totals have decreased as well, and
only a dusting is expected for the highest terrain. Either way,
cooler surface temperatures and gusty winds will accompany this
trough, with highs falling to the 60s for most locations west of the
mountains, 30s to 40 for the mountains, 50s for the High Desert, and
60s to 70s for the low deserts. Winds will gust to around 25-30 mph
in the deserts, 35-40 in the mountains, and up to 45-50 mph in wind-
prone mountain passes. Strong winds over the water will create
hazardous marine conditions on Tuesday as well, discussed below in
the marine section.

Drier air pushes in behind the aforementioned trough as it moves
into the Great Basin on Wednesday, and zonal flow aloft sets up over
California ahead of the next system. Temperatures won`t rebound much
if at all on Wednesday, staying 5-15 degrees below normal. The next
system, a deepening low, approaches the Oregon coastline on
Thursday. Unfortunately, much of the consensus between the global
models yesterday has fallen off, with less confidence today on how
far north the system will stay as it moves through. The trends the
past 24 hours have pushed for a warmer and wetter system, which
could potentially bring very heavy rainfall for our region,
especially the mountains. This would also mean that there is a lower
chance for snow accumulations in the higher terrain, with snow
levels on Thursday/Friday now closer to 5500-6000ft instead of the
4500-5000ft forecasted previously. While we keep a close eye on this
next larger system, confidence is slowly increasing in at least some
decent rainfall, along with an isolated to scattered thunderstorm
threat. Similar to the first system, expect winds to gust as the
low comes across and the gradient tightens, with the strongest
winds currently expected Thursday night into early Friday morning.

&&

.AVIATION...
151800Z...Coast/Valleys...VFR conditions through this evening with
FEW-SCT low clouds based 3-6 kft. SCT-BKN high clouds move into the
region late this evening followed by an increase in low to mid level
clouds based 3-7 kft for the coastal basin overnight. ISO-SCT -SHRA
over coastal waters may begin to push ashore as early as 14z Mon.
-SHRA chances increase for San Diego/Orange Counties through the
late morning into the early afternoon. Best chances for -SHRA will
be in San Diego County. Intermittent MVFR cigs/vis may occur with
any passing -SHRA for coastal TAF sites.

Mountains/Deserts...VFR conditions through early Monday morning
with SCT-BKN high clouds moving into the region late this evening.
Mid-level clouds begin to obscure higher terrain once again
starting 12-18z Monday ahead of the next low-pressure system.

&&

.MARINE...
Isolated light showers will continue over coastal waters through
early Monday. Shower activity increases again on Monday ahead of a
low pressure system with a slight chance (10%) of a thunderstorm or
two Monday afternoon and evening.

Winds and seas ramp back up Monday afternoon with 25-30 knot gusts
expected in addition to steep, high seas of 8-11 feet with an
average period of 8 seconds. Wind gusts will be strongest near San
Clemente Island, where near-gale conditions are possible. With this,
a Small Craft Advisory has been issued for both the near-shore and
outer waters zones due to the hazardous conditions expected from the
gusty winds and high seas. This Small Craft Advisory in effect from
4 PM Monday until 4 AM Wednesday. Winds and seas will slowly
diminish Wednesday morning.

Conditions look to deteriorate once again Thursday as the next storm
system approaches. Steep, high seas of 7-9 ft are expected with wind
gusts peaking near 25-30 knots for Thursday into Friday morning.

&&

.BEACHES...
A series of a mixture of long-period (3-5 feet at 15-17 seconds) and
short period (6-8 feet at 7-8 seconds) swells, both from the west-
northwest (280-290 degrees), will generate periods of elevated and
high surf through much of this week. The current swell that produced
waves up to 10-12 feet yesterday will gradually weaken today and
into early Monday, but surf will remain elevated at 3-6 feet, with
sets to 7-8 feet for west-facing beaches.

The next surge of northwesterly swells arrives Monday afternoon,
ramping surf back up to 6 to 8 feet, with sets to 10 feet along west-
facing beaches. This will extend the threat for high surf and strong
rip currents into mid-week. A High Surf Advisory remains in effect
through 4 AM Wednesday.

&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...High Surf Advisory until 4 AM PST Wednesday for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

PZ...Small Craft Advisory from 4 PM Monday to 4 AM PST Wednesday for
     Coastal Waters from San Mateo Point to the Mexican Border
     and out to 10 nm-Waters from San Mateo Point to the Mexican
     Border Extending 10 to 60 nm out including San Clemente
     Island.
