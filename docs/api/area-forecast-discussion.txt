Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
1004 PM PST Wed Nov 25 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Dry conditions expected through Saturday, with weak to locally
moderate Santa Ana winds Thursday and Friday. Thursday and Friday
will be the warmest days of the week. There will be a cooling
trend Saturday into next week. Widespread coastal flooding is
expected this week due to King Tides and increased surf. Wetter,
windier, and cooler conditions expected next week. Light
precipitation is possible as early as Monday, with higher chances
of widespread and potentially heavier precipitation for Tuesday
and Wednesday.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

...UPDATED AVIATION, MARINE, AND BEACHES DISCUSSION...

High clouds are expected to remain widespread across much of the
area this afternoon. Cloud coverage expected to migrate east,
leaving clear skies from west to east in their wake. The pressure
gradient is 5.6 mb offshore from San Diego to Tonopah, NV and is
expected to trend further offshore into Thursday, before gradually
trending back onshore through the weekend. This will bring dry
conditions and periods of weak to locally moderate Santa Ana winds
Thursday and Friday. Wind gusts look to peak in the mountains,
foothills, and passes at 35 to 45 mph, with gusts to 45 mph
confined to wind prone locations. In addition to winds,
temperatures will peak Thursday and Friday. High temperatures will
be 5 to 15 degrees above average, with the warmest conditions on
Friday. West of the mountains, highs will be in the mid to upper
70s to mid-80s.

By Saturday, most guidance has high temperatures cooler than Friday,
but still 5 to 12 degrees above average. More noticeable cooling is
expected early next week as a more persistent troughing pattern sets
up along the Western United States. Guidance has trended later with
our first precipitation chances. It now looks like precipitation is
possible as early as Monday. ECMWF and GFS ensemble members continue
to show mostly light rain for that time period. Snow levels look to
stay above 8000 ft.

A stronger trough that has the potential to dig south into Central
or Southern California is expected Tuesday through potentially
Thursday next week. This system has the potential to bring more
substantial precipitation, increased winds, and continued cooler
conditions to Southern California. This system looks like it could
produce gusty winds from the coast to the deserts. NBM potential
for winds to exceed 35 mph is 50 to 65 percent along the coast and
30 to 50 percent for the valleys. Mountains and deserts have the
potential to see wind gusts exceeding 50 mph. This looks like a
cold system, so snow levels have the potential to drop below 7000
ft. There remains uncertainty in the details surrounding timing
and precipitation amounts. NBM chances for 1 inch or more of
rainfall Tuesday (Dec 1) through Thursday (Dec 3) are 75 to 85
percent for Orange County, the western Inland Empire, northern San
Diego County and the San Bernardino mountains. Elsewhere in and
west of the mountains chances are 60 to 75 percent. The chance for
2 inches or more of rainfall ranges from around 25 to 35 percent
for the coast and valleys, to 60 to 75 percent for the San
Bernardino County mountains. The chance for 3 inches or more of
rainfall is around 25 to 35 percent for the San Bernardino County
mountains.

&&

.AVIATION...
260600Z...VFR prevailing. SCT high clouds AOA 20kft MSL clear out in
the next few hours with generally SKC through Thursday afternoon.
Northeast winds with gusts 25-35 kts along coastal slopes and
through passes, decreasing around 20z. Another band of SCT-BKN high
clouds AOA 20kft moves in Thursday night/early Friday morning

&&

.MARINE...
A large, long period west swell brought combined seas of 8 to 11
feet for outer coastal waters south of San Clemente Island through
Thursday morning. Additionally, northwest winds may gust near 20 kts
in the far outer coastal waters. A Small Craft Advisory remains in
effect for the outer waters through 10 AM Thursday mainly due to the
high seas. Seas lower Thursday afternoon, falling below 8 feet by
Thursday evening.

&&

.BEACHES...
The La Jolla tide gauge recorded a high tide of 8.11 ft MLLW this
morning, preliminarily breaking the record set on October 27th. King
Tides continue through Thursday, reaching about 8 ft Thursday
morning. Forecast tides have been adjusted 1.1-1.2 feet above
baseline astronomical predictions based on trends in recent
observations.

A long period west swell of 7-9 ft at 16-18 seconds will generate
large breaking waves of 6-8 feet Thursday, with sets of 10-12 feet
on exposed west-facing beaches. Surf lowers to 3-7 feet on Friday. A
High Surf Advisory is in effect for San Diego County through 4 AM
Friday with the swell focusing the higher surf on the west-facing
beaches.

The combination of forecast record King Tides and elevated to high
surf will bring moderate to major coastal flooding, with locally
extreme flooding expected during Thursday morning`s high tide. A
Coastal Flood Warning remains in effect through early Friday
afternoon.

&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Coastal Flood Warning until 1 PM PST Friday for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     High Surf Advisory until 4 AM PST Friday for San Diego County
     Coastal Areas.

PZ...Small Craft Advisory until 10 AM PST Thursday for Waters from
     San Mateo Point to the Mexican Border Extending 10 to 60 nm
     out including San Clemente Island.
