Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
536 PM PDT Mon Sep 28 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
The thick band of mid and high level moisture and clouds has
moved east of the area this afternoon. High temperatures will be a
few degrees lower today, followed by a warming trend into early next
week, with widespread moderate or greater HeatRisk in the coastal
and valley areas for Thursday through the weekend. Those areas
could see near record warm overnight temperatures Friday through
Sunday.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...


...UPDATED AVIATION DISCUSSION...

This afternoon...the thick band of mid and high clouds that
brought us rain and showers overnight has moved east and south as
the low pressure system to our northwest moves to the southeast.
This low pressure system has brought us cooler conditions and
stronger onshore flow. Temperatures are mostly lower than at this
time yesterday, especially in the mountains and deserts where
temperatures are anywhere from 3-12 degrees lower. High
temperatures will be a few degrees lower today, followed by
warming on Tuesday and greater inland warming on Wednesday. High
temperatures on Wednesday will range from around 80 near the coast
to the upper 80s to mid 90s for the Inland Empire with the lower
deserts in the upper 90s. The onshore flow is currently producing
westerly winds gusting 25-30 mph in the mountain passes, canyons
and on adjacent desert slopes. The gusty westerly winds will
weaken this evening. Low clouds persist over and west of the
mountains. Significant moisture remains through a depth of the
atmosphere and the possibility of showers over the mtns can`t be
eliminated, but conditions are stable enough to make that outcome
unlikely.

By Tuesday morning, the low pressure system will move far enough
to the southeast so that the upper low is over the lower Colorado
River valley with northerly winds aloft on the west side of the
upper low. A warming trend begins on Tuesday with locally gusty north
to northeast winds at the surface.

Thursday into early next week... High pressure aloft will
continue the warming trend into the weekend with only slight
cooling on Sunday. Widespread moderate or greater HeatRisk is
expected for the coast and valleys for Thursday through the
weekend, with widespread major HeatRisk for inland coastal areas
and the western San Diego County valleys.

High temperatures for next weekend will range from a few degrees
above average for the lower deserts to as much as 12 to 18 degrees
above average for the coast and valleys in San Diego County. High
temperatures for next weekend will range from the 80s near the
coast to the mid 90s to around 100 for the inland valleys with the
lower deserts around 100.

For the weekend, overnight low temperatures for the Orange County
coastal plain, and for the coast and western valleys in San Diego
County will be mostly in the lower to mid 70s. For Friday through
Sunday, there could be several records tied or broken for record
warm low temperatures, mostly for coastal and valley locations.
The existing records for some of those locations was from 1997.

&&

.AVIATION...
290000Z...SCT cumulus based at around 10 kft over mountains and
deserts. Slight (20%) chance ISO -SHRA for desert slopes / deserts
through 06z.

For the coastal basin...Patchy SCT-BKN low clouds based at 1200-1600
feet MSL to develop after 01Z, passing over SD County coastal areas
intermittently, diminishing in coverage later in the evening and
becoming even more patchy and intermittent after 07z throughout the
coast. Full scatter out of low clouds 15-17z, then VFR prevails
across the region.

&&

.MARINE...
Northwest winds gusting to 20-25 knots in the outer waters this
evening, generating choppy seas. The Small Craft Advisory contains
more details. No hazardous marine conditions are expected Tuesday
through Friday.

&&

.BEACHES...
Southwest swell from former Hurricane Odalys has combined with south-
southeast swell from Hurricane Polo. Elevated surf with sets to 6
feet has been observed today in northern Orange County. There is a
high risk for strong rip and longshore currents through Tuesday. In
addition to elevated surf, high tides will peak at 6.8-7.1 feet
during the late mornings today through Wednesday, highest on
Tuesday. High tides and elevated surf will combine to generate minor
coastal flooding in low lying areas and coastal erosion. A Coastal
Flood Advisory is in effect through Wednesday afternoon.

Please note - mentioned tide predictions in the Advisory have been
adjusted to reflect actual measured tide observations.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Coastal Flood Advisory until 5 PM PDT Wednesday for Orange
     County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

PZ...Small Craft Advisory until 11 PM PDT this evening for Waters
     from San Mateo Point to the Mexican Border Extending 10 to
     60 nm out including San Clemente Island.


&&
