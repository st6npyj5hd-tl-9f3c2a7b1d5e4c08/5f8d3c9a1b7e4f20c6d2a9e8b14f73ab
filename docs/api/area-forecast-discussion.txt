Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
120 PM PDT Wed Oct 7 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
The long duration heatwave is past its halfway point, but extreme
heat will persist through tomorrow for the coasts and valleys,
and through Friday for the lower deserts. Cooling temperatures are
expected for the weekend onward. A low pressure system will
combine with tropical moisture, bringing showers Sunday into
Tuesday, with the greatest chances of rain on Sunday into Monday.


&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...


.SHORT TERM (Today through Saturday)...

The long-duration heatwave will continue for the coast and
valleys through Thursday, and through Friday for the lower
deserts. Inland areas will warm a few degrees today, while the
coast into the western San Diego County valleys cools a few
degrees. There will be High to locally Extreme HeatRisk for the
beaches and far inland valleys today, dropping to Moderate to
locally Major HeatRisk tomorrow. There will be areas of Major to
locally Extreme HeatRisk for the lower deserts today through
Friday. High temperatures will range from the upper 70s to mid 80s
near the coast to the 90s for the Inland Empire with 102 to 106
for the lower deserts for the duration of the heat. By Saturday,
cooling will be very noticeable and only minor to locally moderate
HeatRisk is expected.

Low clouds and locally dense fog over the coastal waters this
evening will spread inland along the coast for late tonight into
Thursday morning. Followed by a little farther inland spread of
the low clouds into coastal areas for late Thursday night into
Friday morning as the marine layer slowly deepens.

&&

.LONG TERM (Sunday through Wednesday)...

KEY POINTS

1. Coastal flooding and beach erosion from high tides 6.5 to 7 ft
and elevated surf at times is expected through next week.

2. Rain chances increase for Sunday through Tuesday across the
area. Highest chances of rain are Sunday into Monday. We`re
monitoring the potential for flash and urban flooding.

3. There is a potential for increased winds from the coastal
waters to the deserts Sunday into Monday

DISCUSSION

The cooling trend will continue into early next week, with high
temperatures falling to as much as 10 to 15 degrees below average
for the mountains, deserts, and inland valleys. High temperatures
Monday through Wednesday will be mostly in the upper 60s to low
70s for the coast and valleys, with the lower deserts in the
lower to mid 80s. This will make for temperatures 20-30 degrees
cooler than observed just a few days earlier during the heatwave.

A low pressure system will absorb the moisture from what is left
of former Hurricane Rachel, then move northeastward into extreme
southern California and northern Baja. This will bring showers
chances for Sunday through Tuesday with the greatest chances of
rain Sunday into Monday. Rain totals for the whole event may as
high as around 0.50 to 2.00 inches (highest in the mountains) for
portions of the forecast area. There could be a few inches of
snowfall on the very highest mountain peaks above 9000 feet for
Monday night and Tuesday as snow levels may briefly fall as low as
8000 feet.

Onshore winds will strengthen for Sunday and Monday with southwest
to west winds for Sunday afternoon through Monday morning gusting
to 20 to 30 mph for the coastal waters to the valleys and to 35 to
45 mph for the mountains and deserts with gusts to 55 to 65 mph
for the northern Coachella Valley below the San Gorgonio Pass.
There is the potential for strong wind from the coastal waters to
the deserts Sunday into Monday.

Please continue to monitor the forecast for the dynamic impacts
due to remnants of Rachel.

&&

.AVIATION...
071130Z...Coast: Isolated and intermittent patches of low clouds and
fog may occur from 03Z-10Z Thu. Any cigs would be around 200-600
feet MSL with vis possibly falling as low as 1SM. Greater coverage
of low clouds and fog more likely after 10Z Thursday. Bases would
be 200-600 feet MSL and vis likely 3-6SM.

Otherwise...clear skies except for cumulus based around 10 kft over
mountains 19-01Z.


&&

.MARINE...
No hazardous marine conditions are expected through Saturday.
Gusty winds and choppy seas are expected Sunday into early next
week. Please stay tuned for more details in the coming days.


&&

.BEACHES...
Increasing south swells due to current Hurricane Rachel will
create surf of 3-5 feet with sets to 6 feet on south and southwest
facing beaches through at least Friday. Additionally, elevated
surf is expected to return for beaches from Sunday into early next
week. Swimming conditions will be hazardous especially for
inexperienced swimmers due to both the surf and rip current risk.

High tides and associated minor coastal flooding and beach erosion
will continue into Tuesday, with Sunday being the main day to
watch with the greatest impacts. Tides will be high during both
the mornings and evenings through Friday, then Saturday onward the
morning tides will be the highest. Please see the Beach Hazards
Statement and Coastal Flood Advisory contain the details. Stay
tuned for more details in the coming days.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Beach Hazards Statement through Friday evening for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Coastal Flood Advisory until 2 PM PDT Tuesday for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Extreme Heat Warning until 8 PM PDT Thursday for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-Orange County Inland Areas-San Bernardino and
     Riverside County Valleys-The Inland Empire-San Diego County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Valleys-Santa Ana Mountains
     and Foothills.

     Extreme Heat Warning until 8 PM PDT Friday for Coachella Valley-
     San Diego County Deserts-San Gorgonio Pass near Banning.


PZ...None.

&&
