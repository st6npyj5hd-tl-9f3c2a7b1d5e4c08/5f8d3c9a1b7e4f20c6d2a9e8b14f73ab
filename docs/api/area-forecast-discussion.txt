Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
516 PM PDT Thu Sep 3 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
It will remain cooler and drier for inland areas through Friday, with
patchy night and morning coastal low clouds spreading into portions
of the western valleys at times. The center of Hurricane Marie
will remain well to the south of the area. But a large increase in
surf is expected for for Saturday into Sunday, then slowly
subsiding for Labor Day into early next week. Mid and high level
moisture from Marie will bring more clouds with increasing chances
for showers for Saturday night into Sunday along with a slight
chance of thunderstorms for the mountains, deserts, and inland
valleys for Sunday afternoon.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

...UPDATED AVIATION DISCUSSION...

Today through Saturday...

Seasonably cool weather continues across the region with the upper
level ridge displaced to the southeast US by a large trough across
the West Coast. This large-scale trough features a strong upper low
currently off the WA/OR coast. Consistent model guidance progs this
feature to maintain intensity, and slowly meander into the Pacific
Northwest into Saturday. Consequently, the cool mornings and below
normal high temperatures will persist into Saturday with a fairly
stagnant synoptic pattern overhead. Slightly elevated flow under
this pattern will keep breezy west-southwesterly winds in place for
the mountains and into the adjacent deserts through Saturday as
well. Peak gusts generally 25-35 mph, locally up to 45 mph within
mountain passes.

Of most interest continues to be the influence the aforementioned
upper level pattern has for Hurricane Marie and the degree of its
influence to southern California. With the coalescence of model
guidance featuring the strong upper low maintaining latitude near
the Pac NW and not pushing further south as guidance earlier in the
week suggested was possible, the upper ridge across the south-
central US is able to extend a ridge axis from the Desert Southwest
into northern Mexico, helping steer Marie to the west and well out
to sea. The only direct impacts from Hurricane Marie continues to be
the elevated swell/surf arriving this weekend...see the Beaches
section below for more details.

Late Saturday into early next week...

While the track for Hurricane Marie takes the storm out to sea and
no direct impacts are anticipated, the larger-scale wave of mid
level tropical moisture is set to move into the region during the
day on Saturday. Combining this with the influence of the
southwesterly flow aloft from the mid-latitude synoptic setup,
widespread chances of rainfall this weekend remains the highlight of
the forecast. There remains a great deal of spread in model guidance
in how much, if any, rain falls, but confidence continues to slowly
increase that at least most of the area will see some accumulations.
Sunday looks to feature the greatest chances for rainfall. Within
this uncertainty, even the higher-end of ensembles don`t suggest a
total wash-out, with the 90th percentile favoring 0.4-0.5" of total
rainfall for the valleys and up to 0.5-1.0" for the coastal
slopes/mountains. Ensemble means, however, are closer to 0.2-0.3".
Until subtle differences in the placement of the large upper low in
the PacNW and associated dynamical forcing are resolved and we
stretch into the high resolution guidance window, a fair amount
of uncertainty in the precipitation forecast will linger.

Despite the rainfall uncertainty, what is more certain is that the
influx of tropical moisture and its influence on the temperatures.
While the addition of moisture and clouds will help keep high
temperatures near to below average into early next week, the cool
mornings will trend warmer, with low temperatures returning back
above normal come Sunday morning.

.AVIATION...
031800Z...Coast/Valleys...Patchy, intermittent low clouds based 1500-
2000 feet MSL will will develop tonight after 02z into Friday.
Clouds may briefly move into coastal San Diego Co 03-05z before
scattering again (40% chance), with the greatest chances for impacts
11-15Z. Any clouds will clear by 16Z Fri.

Mountains/Deserts...Mostly clear skies today and tomorrow. Breezy
westerly winds mainly through mountain passes and along desert
slopes 20-07Z. Gusts 25-35 kts with pockets of moderate
up/downdrafts over and in lee of ridges.

&&

.AVIATION...
040000Z...Coast/Valleys...Intermittent low clouds based 1500-2000ft
MSL will will develop tonight after 03z, spreading up to 10 miles
inland. Clouds will be extremely patchy. Clouds may briefly move
into coastal San Diego Co 03-05z before scattering again (40%
chance), with the greatest chances for 1 hour + impacts 11-15Z. Any
clouds will clear by 16z Fri, with SKC thereafter. Another round of
patchy clouds with bases generally around 2000ft MSL will develop
again Friday evening, after 03z Sat.

Mountains/Deserts...Mostly SKC and VFR today and tomorrow. Breezy
westerly winds mainly through mountain passes and along desert
slopes through 07z. Gusts 25-35 kts with pockets of moderate
up/downdrafts over and in lee of ridges.

&&

.MARINE...
No hazardous marine conditions are expected through Monday. However,
wind gusts around 20 knots are expected this afternoon and evening
around San Clemente Island.

&&

.BEACHES...
A large south swell from Hurricane Marie will move into the region
Friday night and Saturday, peaking Sunday into Monday, before
gradually diminishing Tuesday. This will result in elevated to high
surf and strong rip and longshore currents at majority of beaches.
Additionally, waves will occasionally run much farther up the beach
than expected, especially during increasing and evening high tides.

For south and southwest-facing beaches, expecting high surf 6-10
feet. This includes Orange County and north San Diego County
beaches. For west-facing beaches, expecting elevated surf 4-7 feet.
This includes most of southern San Diego County beaches.

High surf combined with high astronomical tides 6 to about 6.5 feet
will lead to tidal overflow and coastal flooding Saturday through
Tuesday during evening high tides, especially for those south and
southwest-facing beaches as well as low-lying, typically impacted
areas.

&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Coastal Flood Advisory from 6 AM Saturday to 11 PM PDT Tuesday
     for Orange County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal
     Areas.

     High Surf Advisory from 6 AM Saturday to 11 PM PDT Tuesday for
     Orange County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

PZ...Coastal Flood Advisory from 6 AM Saturday to 11 PM PDT Tuesday
     for Coastal Waters from San Mateo Point to the Mexican
     Border and out to 10 nm.

     High Surf Advisory from 6 AM Saturday to 11 PM PDT Tuesday for
     Coastal Waters from San Mateo Point to the Mexican Border
     and out to 10 nm.
