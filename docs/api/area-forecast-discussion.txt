Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
233 AM PDT Sun Sep 6 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Patchy marine layer low clouds and fog can be expected this
morning as mid to high level clouds from Tropical Cyclone Marie
move over the area this weekend. Periods of light to locally
moderate precipitation are possible for today. There is a slight
(5-10%) chance of thunderstorms, mainly over the San Bernardino
Mountains and High Desert on Sunday afternoon. Warming high and
low temperatures through the middle of next week. Slight chance of
mountain showers and thunderstorms return for mid-week.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

Today through tomorrow night...

Increasing mid to high level clouds with some embedded high`lofted
showers are beginning to develop over the region, which is funneling
up from the transverse banding associated with Hurricane Marie,
which is now located roughly just over 700 miles to the southwest of
San Diego. This will continue to track towards the northwest slowly
and continue to usher in more mid level moisture ahead of a longwave
trough upstream and with some forcing, which will result in better
development of bands of light to moderate rain. The best convergence
aloft will be located over the San Bernardino Mountains, with the
highest probability of measurable rain, and possibly an embedded
thunderstorm or two. Most of the lighter showers that are currently
over San Diego County this morning will transition further north by
later in the day. Given the timing and position of where these bands
of light to moderate rain establish themselves in a southwest to
northeast orientation. Total rainfall amounts may reach possibly
over an inch for the the San Bernardino Mountains, and then tapering
to lesser amounts from around a half an inch for the higher terrain
of the mountains in Riverside/northern San Diego counties.
Rainfall amounts will generally be 0.20 to 0.30 inches within the
IE, with slightly lesser amounts to the coastal areas of Orange
County, and drastically tapering off to lesser amounts over San
Diego County (with possibly some areas over southern San Diego
County receiving little to no precip). These bands of rain will
eventually dissipate by later in the evening. Temperatures will
remain on the cooler side today, around and slightly below the
seasonal average for this time of year, with extensive cloud cover
over the region. For tomorrow, temperatures will rebound back to
around average, with drier conditions and only a very slight
chance of an afternoon shower or thunderstorm over the mountains
and adjacent deserts.

Tuesday through Friday...

Drier conditions will return by Tuesday, with most of the moisture
associated with Hurricane Marie exiting the region, and only some
lingering moisture being advected up some the south which may allow
for a slight chance of a afternoon shower or storm to develop over
the mountains. As the ridge continues to build into the middle of
the week, with will warm temperatures back above the seasonal
average, with the warmest day peaking on Wednesday, which will find
high temperatures returning back into the low to mid 90s for the
inland areas. Wednesday will also bring a better bout of mid level
moisture being advected in from the east, with a better chance of
afternoon showers and thunderstorms returning for the mountains
and adjacent deserts, along with a gradual cooldown throughout the
rest of the work week. The marine layer will likely also continue
to be displaced and patchy throughout this next week.

&&

.AVIATION...
060600Z...Coast/Valleys/Mountains...Mid/high clouds around 10,000-
20,000ft MSL currently over San Diego and eastern Riverside counties
will continue to move across and spread north to cover the rest of
the area by 10Z. Light showers will be possible at times, mainly in
the mountains, through 12Z. Rain chances increase after 12Z over
much of the area with the highest chances over Orange County, the
Inland Empire, the high deserts and the San Bernardino and Riverside
County mountains 16-23Z. Isolated TSRA over mountain areas,
especially in the San Bernardino mountains, 18Z Sunday-01Z Monday.
Vis restrictions of 4-6SM and BKN-OVC CIGs at 5,000ft MSL or lower
in areas of heavier rain. Chances for rain decrease after 01Z Monday.

Deserts...Mid/high level clouds AOA 10,000-20,000ft over the south
deserts are making their way into the high deserts, and will persist
through the TAF period. VCSH will be possible in the deserts after
12z, with instances of -SHRA in the High Desert. Otherwise, VFR with
no significant impacts expected.

&&

.MARINE...
No hazardous marine conditions are expected through Thursday.

&&

.BEACHES...
A large south swell from Hurricane Marie is set to peak today into
Monday before gradually diminishing Tuesday. This will result in
elevated to high surf and strong rip and longshore currents at the
majority of beaches. Additionally, waves will occasionally run much
farther up the beach than expected, especially during increasing and
high tides.

For south and southwest-facing beaches, expecting high surf 6-10
feet. This includes Orange County and north San Diego County
beaches. For west-facing beaches, expecting elevated surf 4-7 feet.
This includes most of southern San Diego County beaches.

High surf combined with high astronomical tides 6.2 to about 6.8
feet will lead to tidal overflow and coastal flooding through
Tuesday during evening high tides, especially for south and
southwest-facing beaches as well as low-lying areas.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Coastal Flood Advisory until 11 PM PDT Tuesday for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     High Surf Advisory until 11 PM PDT Tuesday for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

PZ...None.

&&

$$
