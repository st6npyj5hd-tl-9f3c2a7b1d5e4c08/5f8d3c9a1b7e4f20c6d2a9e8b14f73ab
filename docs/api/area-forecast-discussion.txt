Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
119 PM PDT Wed Sep 9 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Another very hot and humid day is on tap for Southern California
today. Major to Extreme HeatRisk expected in the coastal basin and
low deserts with moderate HeatRisk elsewhere. Isolated to scattered
showers and thunderstorms expected over the mountains again today
and a slight chance of showers on Thursday. A slow cooling trend
will begin Thursday, though hot and humid conditions will be slow to
subside. Moderate to Major HeatRisk will continue through Thursday
for the coastal basin and through Friday in the low deserts. The
cooling trend will continue through the weekend with temperatures
dropping below normal from Sunday into early next week. Accompanying
this cooldown will be marine layer clouds for the coastal basin and
afternoon breezes for the mountains and deserts.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

The stage has been set for another dangerously hot and humid
afternoon and evening in Southern California. Several locations
in the coastal basin astonishingly did not drop below 80 degrees
this morning. Strong upper ridging is peaking today and tonight
with 500 mb heights around 595-596 mb. Also, surface analysis
reveals a thermal trough sitting near or just offshore as of this
morning, which will hinder the attemps of the seabreeze to cool
the coastal basin. Temperatures this afternoon are currently
ranging from 100-110 for inland areas and generally in the 90s
near the coast. The combination of heat and humidity have pushed
heat indices above 100 even near the coastline this afternoon,
producing dangerous conditions for those without air-conditioning.
In fact, heat indices are locally exceeding 115 in the western
valleys of San Diego and Orange Counties. Overall, major to
extreme HeatRisk is occurring or imminent in the coastal basin
today and will last well into the evening hours. Major HeatRisk is
also expected in the Coachella Valley this afternoon where
temperatures will peak near 105-110. Moderate HeatRisk elsewhere,
except for mountain locations above 6000 ft, where it should be a
rather pleasant next few days.

Speaking of the mountains, isolated to scattered thunderstorms are
expected again this afternoon. Storm activity exceeded expectations
yesterday, so we are hedging on the side of more activity again
today. There is less instability and greater capping today as
indicated by the 12z KNKX sounding, but PWATs remain very high near
1.8 inches. Isolated rainfall rates near 0.5 in/hr will likely occur
today along with potential for gusty winds with any storm that
develops. Southeasterly flow will permit a slight chance (10-20%)
for a storm or two to drift into the eastern valleys. For Thursday,
instability will decrease yet again. Therefore, expecting only
isolated showers over ridgetops at this time.

A slow cooling trend begins on Thursday as the upper ridge begins to
weaken. However, this humid, tropical airmass will remain in place
through Friday. Therefore, areas of major HeatRisk will continue
through Thursday for the coastal basin and through Friday for the
low deserts. By Saturday, an upper trough to the northwest will
begin to push the weakening ridge out of the region bringing more
relief from the heat and humidity. High temperatures will fall 5 to
10 degrees below normal across the region from Sunday into early
next week.

Morning marine layer clouds and fog will likely begin to reform
over coastal waters as moderate onshore flow returns from Friday
onward. There is a chance for fog Saturday morning for coastal
areas and western valleys. Thereafter, the marine layer should
deepen further into early next week with low clouds filling most
of the coastal basin each morning. Additionally, breezy conditions
are expected for outer coastal waters, mountains, and deserts
over the weekend. By late next week model solutions diverge, but
the mean of the guidance tilts toward high pressure and monsoonal
moisture building back into the region from the east.


&&

.AVIATION... 091800Z...Mostly VFR conditions expected across the
region through Thursday morning with SCT-BKN clouds between 10-20
kft. CBs based at 9 kft MSL with tops reaching to 35 kft will lead
to some SHRA/TSRA mainly over mountains, but locally into deserts or
inland valleys 19-01Z. Localized gusty winds and heavy rain in and
near any TSRA.

&&

.MARINE...
No hazardous marine conditions are expected through Sunday.

&&

.BEACHES...
High tides peaking near 7 feet this evening could produce tidal
overflow, coastal flooding, and localized beach erosion. Check the
Coastal Hazard Message for details.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Coastal Flood Advisory until 11 PM PDT this evening for Orange
     County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

     Extreme Heat Warning until 8 PM PDT this evening for Orange
     County Coastal Areas-Orange County Inland Areas-San
     Bernardino and Riverside County Valleys-The Inland Empire-
     San Diego County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Valleys-
     Santa Ana Mountains and Foothills.

     Heat Advisory from 8 PM this evening to 8 PM PDT Thursday for
     Orange County Coastal Areas-Orange County Inland Areas-San
     Bernardino and Riverside County Valleys-The Inland Empire-
     San Diego County Coastal Areas-San Diego County Valleys-
     Santa Ana Mountains and Foothills.

     Heat Advisory until 8 PM PDT Thursday for Riverside County
     Mountains-San Diego County Mountains.

     Extreme Heat Warning until 8 PM PDT Friday for Coachella Valley-
     San Diego County Deserts-San Gorgonio Pass near Banning.

PZ...None.

&&
