Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
946 PM PST Thu Nov 19 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Intermittent showers and locally heavy rain will push through
southern areas through early evening with mostly dry conditions by
Friday. Cooler weather remains across the region through early next
week. A weak weather system will bring a slight chance of light
showers closer to the coast by Sunday night into Monday morning. Dry
and cooler weather prevails for the early part of the week with
another system moving closer to the area by the middle of next week
as we head toward the Thanksgiving holiday.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

...UPDATED AVIATION DISCUSSION FOR 06Z TAF ISSUANCE...

A cold frontal band will continue to push through the county warning
area this afternoon, dissipating by early evening. Areas of heavy
rain associated with this system have have been observed over the LA
basin. Hourly rain rates have generally been between 0.40-0.60
inches per hour with rain totals nearing one inch in some spots over
the coastal slopes and near the LA County line. The cold front will
continue to weaken, pushing south and east through the afternoon,
pushing through the San Diego metro around 2-5 PM.  Hourly rain
rates will lower near one half inch across this area at times. Snow
levels around 7,000 feet early this afternoon will continue to drop
near 6,000 feet by the late afternoon hours. As the weather system
moves out of the area, light snow may fall in places like Big Bear
Lake near one half inch or so in total. Isolated light showers may
linger into parts of the San Diego metro on Friday as the system
moves out with clearing skies.

Cooler weather will stick around Friday into the weekend as another
low pressure system moves closer to the Pacific Northwest. Friday
will be mostly sunny but highs will be 5 to almost 15 degrees below
normal with temperatures only topping out in the 30s and 40s across
the mountains, only in the 50s in the high desert. As the low moves
closer to the Pacific Northwest, a weak shortwave moves into
Northern California. This will bring cloudier conditions to our area
with continued cooler weather. A portion of this shortwave may drift
far enough south and even become a closed low over the desert
southwest. This will bring the chance for ever so light
showers/drizzle closer to the coast by Sunday night into Monday
morning. If anything falls, it will be light in nature.

Models are in fair agreement about another large area of low
pressure moving over the NE Pac by early next week. There still
remains a lot of uncertainty on where this system goes and what parts
of it make it far enough to impact Southern California. Some
possible scenarios as we head toward the Thanksgiving holiday
include: the system staying to the north as high pressure to the
south expands over our region. This would bring dry and warmer
weather. The GFS handles this a bit differently, where the system
splits in two, and a potential trough moves closer to our region
which would bring potentially wetter weather by around next
Wednesday. We are still close to a week away form this, so please
keep updated with the forecast.

&&

.AVIATION...
200545Z...Currently, a band of light to moderate showers is moving
across southeastern San Diego County. MVFR and intermittent IFR
cigs/vis will continue in this area for a few more hours. BKN-OVC
low clouds with bases 3-5 kft west of the mtns will scatter out from
west to east through this evening as this precipitation band exits
the area to the east. Mostly SCT clouds with unrestricted VIS after
12Z Sat.

ISO-SCT -SHRA/SHRA will taper off this evening for northern areas
and Friday morning in SD County. SCT low to mid level clouds will
linger through Friday morning (mostly in SD County). Mostly SKC
expected regionwide by Friday afternoon.

Gusty west winds on the desert slopes of the mountains will weaken
overnight. Moderate up/downdrafts over and east of mtns (including
Inland Empire) through 12Z.

&&

.MARINE...
Hazardous boating conditions will continue through Friday morning.
West winds gusting to 25 knots with steep, choppy seas 5 to 8 feet
this evening will slowly come down Friday morning. A Small Craft
Advisory is in effect and contains more details. No additional
marine hazards are expected through Tuesday.

&&

.BEACHES...
A northwest swell will continue building, elevating surf through
early Friday. Surf of 3 to 6 feet with sets to 7 feet are expected
on west facing beaches. Strong rip currents and minor coastal
flooding during high tides are expected. A Beach Hazards Statement
contains more details.

King Tides are expected to peak next week, but chances of tides
exceeding 7 feet start as early as Saturday Nov 21. Morning high
tides may even exceed 8 feet next Tuesday through Thursday bringing
widespread moderate impacts.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Beach Hazards Statement until 9 AM PST Friday for Orange County
     Coastal Areas-San Diego County Coastal Areas.

PZ...Small Craft Advisory until 10 AM PST Friday for Coastal Waters
     from San Mateo Point to the Mexican Border and out to 10 nm-
     Waters from San Mateo Point to the Mexican Border Extending
     10 to 60 nm out including San Clemente Island.


&&
