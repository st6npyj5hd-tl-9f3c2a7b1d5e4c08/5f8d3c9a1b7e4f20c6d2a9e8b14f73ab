AFDSGX

Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
917 PM PST Wed Nov 18 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Cool weather will continue for the next week. Rain, isolated
thunderstorms, high mountain snow, and stronger winds are
expected Thursday as a storm system moves through California. Dry
but cool weather will follow Friday through Sunday. The next
system looks weaker and could bring light rain Sunday night and
Monday, potentially followed by another one mid to late next week.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...

...UPDATED AVIATION/BEACHES/MARINE DISCUSSION...

.SHORT TERM...Wed Nov 18 through Thu Nov 19...Broad cyclonic flow
is currently present over the region, leading to weak onshore
winds and cool temperatures this afternoon. Skinny cumulus clouds
around 5000 feet this afternoon are indicative of a little bit of
moisture in the mid-levels; meanwhile, upper level clouds above
20k feet are starting to stream into the region ahead of a robust
cold front that will move through the region Thursday.

A mid-level low pressure center currently offshore of northern
California/Oregon will move slightly southwest to cover the CA/OR
border, sending a quick-moving, well-defined cold front through
southwest California with the southeast edge of the low. As this
approaches, winds will increase over the mountain ridges late
tonight into early Thursday morning. These winds will become more
widespread across the mountains into parts of the desert late
Thursday morning into the early afternoon with the main frontal
band, with peak gusts 45 to 60 mph over the mountain ridges into
the deserts foothills as well as through the mountain passes.
Winds will also become breezy for the valleys and coast as the
front passes, with peak gusts 15 to 30 mph.

As for rain, the vast majority of rain associated with this front
will fall Thursday morning and afternoon. While light, pre-
frontal rain is possible for northern Orange County/IE as early as
about 7 AM, the main impactful band looks to hit the far northern
reach of our region (northern OC and IE) around 10 AM, then
far northwest San Diego County around 1 PM. This will bring
widespread moderate to heavy rain as it passes through. Warm ocean
water will add to the slight instability associated with the
system, bringing a convective element with embedded thunderstorms
to the rain. For this reason, rainfall rates of 0.5 to 0.65" an
hour are possible, especially for the northwest part of the
region, which could produce urban flooding, small stream rises,
and small rockfalls to the mountain regions. The front will lose
energy as it moves inland and gets farther from the ocean, so
rates are expected to diminish as it moves in southern OC/IE and
into San Diego Co.

Rainfall will taper off quite quickly behind the main band, which
will move out of OC and the IE around 3-4 PM and out of San Diego
County by about 6 PM. Another wave or so of off-and-on rain will
follow behind, but rain will be much lighter with very little
chance for any thunderstorms. Snow levels start fairly high with
the main precipitation at above 7500 feet, though heavier
precipitation could locally drive this down to about 7000 feet.
Snow levels drop behind the front to about 7000 feet late Thursday
afternoon, then to about 6000 feet late Thursday night. The
majority of snow is expected above the communities/main roads on
the ridges, though a few inches at the resort level and on the
high mountain passes is expected. Rain will likely transition to
snow later Thursday in Big Bear, but with wet, heavy snow we`re
not expecting a lot in the way of accumulation, around 0.1" to
about an inch.

&&

.LONG TERM...Fri Nov 20 to Wed Nov 25...A few showers will linger
in southwest San Diego County through Friday morning, before the
weather dries and skies clear. Another shortwave trough rotates
through the region on the east side of the larger trough Friday,
but this will mostly just keep us cooler than average. This is
followed by a brief period of ridging Saturday, though this will
be weak and transient enough that temperatures will only bump up a
few degrees for Saturday afternoon.

Another weak shortwave follows late Sunday into Monday. This has a
moderate chance (~40% chance) of bringing some more precipitation
to the region, though this would be much, much lighter. A handful
of global ensemble members are still showing mostly dry weather,
so confidence in the forecast for next week is moderate. Another
brief ridge is likely late Monday into Tuesday, before another low
pressure system looms towards late Tuesday-Wednesday. This latter
system has trended slightly wetter, with more ensemble members
showing precipitation than previous runs, but there are large
differences in strength, timing, and depth of troughing that will
make huge differences in the temperature, rain, etc. forecast for
next week.

&&

.AVIATION...
190515Z...Coast/Valleys...Multiple FEW-SCT cloud layers with bases
3000-5000 ft and AOA 20000 ft MSL. BKN mid-level clouds (3000-5000
ft MSL) expected to increase into early Thursday AM. A band of rain
will move through the region from north to south starting at KSNA
around 17-20Z down to KSAN closer to 21-23Z. When the main rain band
is passing +RA and IVR cigs/vis expected, especially for locations
north of San Diego County, otherwise periods of -RA and MVFR
cigs/vis can be expected. Rain ending north to south Thursday
afternoon/evening with slowly clearing cigs. South winds gusting 20-
25 kt expected with the passage of the rain band, weakening and
becoming westerly after the band passes.

Mountains/Deserts...High level clouds SCT-BKN AOA 10000 feet MSL
will continue into Thursday. A band of rain will move through the
region from north to south starting around 16-18Z in northern
mountains, closer to 19-22Z in San Diego county. When the main rain
band is passing +RA and IVR cigs/vis expected, especially for
locations north of San Diego County, otherwise periods of -RA and
MVFR cigs/vis can be expected. Rain ending north to south Thursday
afternoon/evening with slowly clearing cigs. Southwest winds will
increase after 10Z with peak gusts 40-55 kt on the mountain slopes,
with gusts 30-40 kt in the High Desert. Winds will become westerly
after 00Z Friday and weaken after 12Z Friday.

&&

.MARINE...
A Small Craft Advisory has been issued for 12z Thursday through 18z
Friday. Steep seas of 5 to 8 ft, along with wind gusts peaking 20-30
knots, are expected for Thursday into Friday morning. Wind direction
initially south-southwest on Thursday will turn westerly by late
afternoon and continue through early morning on Friday. Conditions
will begin to improve by later in the day on Friday with much calmer
seas expected by the weekend.

&&

.BEACHES...
Northwesterly swell builds Thursday, elevating surf once again
through early Friday. Surf of 4 to 6 ft with sets to 8 ft are
expected on west facing beaches. Strong rip currents and minor
coastal flooding during high tide are expected.

King Tides are expected to peak next week, but chances of tides
exceeding 7 ft start as early as Saturday Nov 21.

&&

.SKYWARN...
Skywarn activation is not requested. However weather spotters are
encouraged to report significant weather conditions.


&&

.SGX WATCHES/WARNINGS/ADVISORIES...
CA...Wind Advisory until 4 PM PST Thursday for Apple and Lucerne
     Valleys-Riverside County Mountains-San Bernardino County
     Mountains-San Diego County Mountains-San Gorgonio Pass near
     Banning.

PZ...Small Craft Advisory from 4 AM Thursday to 10 AM PST Friday for
     Coastal Waters from San Mateo Point to the Mexican Border
     and out to 10 nm-Waters from San Mateo Point to the Mexican
     Border Extending 10 to 60 nm out including San Clemente
     Island.


&&

PUBLIC...CSP
AVIATION/MARINE/BEACHES...CO
