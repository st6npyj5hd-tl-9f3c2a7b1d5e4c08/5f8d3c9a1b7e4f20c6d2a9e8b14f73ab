Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service San Diego CA
441 AM PDT Sat Oct 10 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
Tropical Storm Rachel will approach the region Saturday evening into
Sunday. Cooler weather will continue to spread inland this weekend
into early next week as a stronger trough moves in from the north.
These systems will bring showers and locally heavy rainfall, gusty
winds, and coastal impacts to the region. Quieter and warmer weather
will begin to move into the region for the latter half of next week.

&&

.DISCUSSION...FOR EXTREME SOUTHWESTERN CALIFORNIA INCLUDING ORANGE...
SAN DIEGO...WESTERN RIVERSIDE AND SOUTHWESTERN SAN BERNARDINO
COUNTIES...


...UPDATED AVIATION DISCUSSION FOR 12Z TAF ISSUANCE...

Thin mid/upper level clouds from TS Rachel are passing overhead
which will yield to bright overcast skies today, with a slight (10-
15%) chance for isolated thunderstorms Saturday afternoon and
evening east of the mountain peaks into the deserts, ahead of the
more widespread unsettled weather. High temperatures in the valleys
and high desert will only reach into the 80s while temperatures at
the coast remain in the upper 70s. In the low deserts, temperatures
will likely only reach the mid-90s. The weather theme over the next
few days will be of cloudy skies, rain and wind at times from TS
Rachel and an upper-level trough from the north. Let`s take a look
on how it will affect our weather...

Rainfall...As mentioned above, there is a slight chance for
thunderstorms over the desert slopes and deserts later today ahead
of TS Rachel. With high resolution models now in play, confidence on
timing has increased quite a bit, while confidence on amounts
remains low. The three rainfall scenarios that seem to be common
across models are highlighted below this section. From a timing
standpoint, generally models agree on bringing the main swath of
rainfall from the TS around 10 PM - midnight this evening into San
Diego County, expanding quickly northward through the rest of the
region overnight (1-4 AM). The entire region should expect periods
of rain through the morning, scattering to showers near the coast
in the late morning (8-11 AM). In the afternoon, Rachel`s surface
low will likely be far north or east of the region; shower
activity will then become isolated through most of the region,
with steady rainfall continuing in the foothills and coastal
facing mountain slopes through Sunday evening. Peak rain rates in
the mountains are broadly expected to range between 0.5-1"/hr.
Here are the three rainfall scenarios for Sunday, broken down by
forecasted track:

Scenario #1 (most likely ~ 60% chance): The system tracks around 50-
100 miles south of the US border. We see periods of moderate and
locally heavy rainfall across the region during the day Sunday,
yielding to increasingly scattered showers in the lowlands during
the afternoon. Totals in this scenario range from 0.75-1.5" in San
Diego County west of the mountains (around 2" in the foothills).
North of SD County, there will be a rainfall gradient from west to
east, ranging from ~0.3-0.5" in northwestern OC to ~0.75-2" in the
IE. The deserts and desert slopes will be quite a wild-card
depending on the flow but generally 0.5-1.5". For the mountains,
around 2-3" with consistent rain expected all day, interspersed with
locally higher rain rates. Areas of urban flooding and flash
flooding in the mountains could occur in this scenario.

Scenario #2 (more rain ~ 20% chance): In this case, we see very high
PWAT`s over 2" with the storm core making a close approach to San
Diego, which would give higher rain totals and potential for more
impacts. This would entail 1.25-2.5" through the coastal basin (more
east, less west), 3-4" in the mountains, 1-3" along desert slopes
and 1-2" over the deserts. Urban flooding impacts could be more
numerous in this instance, especially in the lower deserts. Flash
flooding concerns would be particularly elevated for the desert
slopes.

Scenario #3 (less rain ~ 20% chance): This is the case where the low
level circulation makes landfall well south of the US/Mexico
border and tracks northeastward. Rainfall amounts would be much
lower: around 0.25-1" across inland areas and desert with amounts
less than 0.25 for much of Orange County and northwestern SD
County. Amounts in the foothills and mountains would be limited to
0.75-2" and impacts would be much more isolated.

During the day Monday, the trough axis moves through, bringing a
much more traditional round of light rainfall of around 0.05-0.25"
through the coastal basin, with higher amounts in the mountains
(light snow above ~8,000 feet) and rain shadowing the deserts.
Shower activity looks to persist until Tuesday morning before
clearing out Tuesday afternoon.

Wind...As of 08z today, the ECMWF/EPS and GFS/GEFS are narrowing in
on a landfall of TS Rachel right around Ensenada, Mexico while the
Google FNV3 and other AI models show the storm making landfall much
farther south. There are also three scenarios for wind on Sunday:

Scenario #1 (most likely ~ 50% chance): The system reaches land
around 20-100 miles south of the US border, around Ensenada, Mexico.
The low pressure would be close enough to induce gusty winds near
the circulation, especially on the east side of the circulation.
In this case, peak gusts move through the region in the early
hours of Sunday around 25-45 mph through the coastal basin and
50-70 mph near the mountain ridges. Strongest winds would begin
easterly on the SD mountain slopes very early on Sunday, then turn
westerly by sunrise, affecting the ridges and desert slopes.
These gusty winds would continue throughout the afternoon,
weakening by the evening.

Scenario #2 (30% chance): The system makes landfall more than ~100
miles south of the US border. Winds would be much less significant
(gusts < 30 mph) for most of the coastal basin. Winds would remain
westerly everywhere and only pick up by Sunday afternoon with still
very strong (50-70 mph) gusts in the mountains and desert slopes.

Scenario #3 (20% chance): Some ensemble members of the GEFS and EPS
bring the tracks approach very close to the US/Mexico border, which
would bring very strong winds to San Diego County, including urban
areas, with gusts on the order of 40-60 mph (locally higher). The
wind forecast for OC and the IE wouldn`t change too much - still
ranging around 30-45 mph.

Temperatures...Sunday and Monday will be dominated by cloudy skies,
rain and an approaching trough from the north. As a result,
temperatures will once again cool dramatically, down 10-15 degrees
regionwide (widespread low 70s through the coastal basin, 50s to low
60s in the mountains). Monday, the upper trough axis moves through
with 500-mb heights around 565 decameters, bringing high
temperatures down to the mid-40s in the mountains and into the 60s
across the rest of the region (except low deserts - low/mid 70s). As
the trough moves east and as clouds clear out, Tuesday`s
temperatures rebound to around Sundays temperatures. The warming
trend continues through the end of the week, bringing seasonable
highs back by the end of the week (perhaps a return of widespread
80s/90s next weekend, including the coast). Note that low
temperatures are expected to drop into the 20s for high elevation
valleys early this week due to the cold air aloft. Elsewhere, lows
will fall to the low-50s inland and low 60s at the coast with
coolest morning temps Tue/Wed as clouds clear out.

&&

.AVIATION...
101200Z...Coast...Patchy low clouds along the immediate San Diego
County coast will remain patchy and intermittent through this
morning. Highest chances for low cloud cigs are for southwest SD Co
VCNTY KSAN. Bases will be 1000-1500 ft MSL. Low clouds likely to
clear between 13-15Z. Low clouds with CIGs below 3000 ft MSL are
unlikely to return in the next 24-36 hours.

Otherwise...BKN clouds AOA 20k ft with unrestricted VIS through
tonight.

&&

.MARINE...
No hazardous marine conditions are expected today. Gusty northerly
winds develop early Sunday morning, turning westerly late morning
into the afternoon. Winds 20 to 30 knots expected throughout the
coastal waters Sunday. Large fresh swell from the northwest will
combine with south swell associated with Tropical Storm Rachel and
wind waves to create combined seas 6 to 10 feet Sunday. Choppy,
steep, mixed seas and gusty winds will produce conditions hazardous
to small craft on Sunday. Winds and seas subside for Monday, though
gusts will locally approach 20 knots Monday afternoon.

.BEACHES...
Surf of 3-5 feet with local sets to 6 feet will continue on south
and southwest facing beaches through today. Elevated surf and strong
rip and longshore currents will create hazardous swimming
conditions. This elevated surf will combine with high tides near 7
feet during the mornings and evenings to produce significant coastal
flooding and beach erosion.

Fresh south swell associated with Tropical Storm Rachel will arrive
late today into Sunday. This will combine with a larger northwest
swell and strong winds to produce elevated to high surf Sunday for
all area beaches, peaking at 5 to 8 feet with sets to 9 feet. This
will pose a hazard to swimmers and lead to very strong rip currents.
In addition, morning high tides will continue to be 7 to locally 7.5
feet, continuing the threat for significant coastal flooding through
Sunday afternoon. A Coastal Flood Warning is in effect for these
hazards through Sunday at 3 PM. Take the necessary actions to
protect flood-prone property.

Both winds and swells subside for Monday-Tuesday, and surf will
decrease to 3 to 6 feet, though strong rip currents will continue.
Morning high tides will continue to be near 7 feet, meaning that
minor coastal flooding will continue to be an issue through this
period. The Coastal Flood Warning will be replaced with a Coastal
Flood Advisory at 3 PM Sunday and continue through 2 PM Tuesday.
